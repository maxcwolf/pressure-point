Accidents on the floor

No restroom means pee and poop on the ground. Before any of it is built, here are three poops and three puddles of pee, each drawn in the game’s soft clay-toy style and photographed in the real game, so you can pick one of each. The poops are A, B and C; the puddles are D, E and F. The recommendation is C and D, and their pictures wear an amber frame.

How the pictures were made: a new sandbox game in the browser at 1440×900 at noon, with three rooms floored in oak, checkerboard and bamboo off the corridor, each model laid on all four floors and guests standing beside three of them. Each option is shot as near as the game lets you zoom, at the default zoom every game starts at, and zoomed right out, both as it is and grown half as big again. Crops are at the game’s own pixel size; click any picture to see it whole. Nothing in the simulation has changed yet: the models are on the branch, in the model lab at /lab.html?model=scenery:accidents (pick a look from its settings), and the game pictures were taken by laying them straight into the scene.

All six, and what they must not be mistaken for

Pee is lemon yellow, well clear of kombucha’s amber, and lies as one round pool with a smaller one run into it and a few splashes, where kombucha is three round pools of the same size. Poop stands up off the floor in brown, where litter is three bright wrappers and soot a flat black smudge. A poop is 33 cm tall, about up to a guest’s knee, and a puddle 60 cm across, as big as a puddle of kombucha. At the default zoom a poop is about 14 pixels across and a puddle 25; zoomed right out, 7 and 13.

Soft-serve swirl

Poop

The cartoon one: three shiny chocolate tiers, each a little off the one under it so it twists, and a curl on top.

On the floor
The outline alone says what it is, from every side and on every floor, even zoomed right out. Brown on oak is the weakest of the four floors, and still reads by its shape and shine.
Funny, not gross

Cost

What could go wrong
Nothing much. It lies still, so a fresh one and one left for an hour look the same.

Poop

On the floor

The nearest of the three to the real thing, so the least funny and the most likely to put someone off.

C

Swirl with flies

Recommended

Poop

The swirl from A, with two cartoon flies looping round it, wings up in a V, bobbing as they go, one each way round.

On the floor
Everything A has, and the only accident that moves: two dark specks circling catch the eye at the default zoom before the poop does, which is what a mess you should send someone to needs.
Funny, not gross
The flies say it smells without showing anything more. Big round cartoon flies, not real ones.
Cost
Four more small parts, two materials, no shadows; a few sums a frame to fly them, only while there is a poop, which is rarely.
A good use of the flies
They could arrive only once a poop has been left a while, say half a minute, so you can tell a fresh one from one nobody is seeing to. Your call; it changes nothing in the picture.
What could go wrong
Zoomed right out the flies are a pixel or two; the swirl still shows, more so grown.

Recommendation

C, the swirl with flies, and D, the glossy puddle

The swirl’s outline says what it is on every floor and at every zoom, and the flies make it the funniest of the three and the one that catches the eye, without anything gross. The glossy puddle is the clearest of the three puddles: lemon yellow well away from kombucha’s amber, a splash shape unlike kombucha’s pools, and a shine that says wet. E’s sparkles say the floor has just been cleaned, and F’s rim pulls it toward amber.

Whichever you pick, grow it when zoomed out, as speech bubbles and the censor mosaic do: drawn at its own size close up and at the default zoom, and up to half as big again as the camera pulls right back, so a mess in a big center still shows. The grown pictures above show the most it grows.

One rule to settle while you pick

Does a long wait for the restroom count as having none?

An accident happens when a guest’s bladder is full and there is no restroom they can use: none built, none finished, every one flooded, or none they can reach. Today toilets have no line: a bursting guest goes when a toilet is free and loses patience faster until then. So the question is what happens when there are restrooms, but too few toilets for the crowd.

  1. Only when there is no restroom to use at all. A center with too few toilets never has an accident; its guests just get cross, as now.
  2. Recommended: also when every toilet is taken and they have been bursting a long while, about 40 seconds at full, against 3½ seconds for a visit. Too few toilets for the crowd then shows on the floor, warned first by the bursting bubble, and fixed by building another restroom. A center with enough toilets never sees it.

What I will build once you pick

  • The guest leaves the poop or puddle where they stand, and is embarrassed: a big hit to their mood, and often they go home.
  • Guests near it think less of the place, as with litter but more so.
  • It stays until a Caretaker cleans it, through the same job list as litter, ahead of litter and behind flood water. A puddle slows walkers, as kombucha does.
  • Warned first: the bursting bubble, then the first time, an advisor line and an announcement. The guest’s and the room’s inspector say why, and the fix is a restroom and a Caretaker.
  • None before the tutorial has asked for a restroom, and a sandbox switch to turn them off.
