The Research Drip

Four ways to keep new things arriving through a center's middle and after its last star, the way Two Point's Research room does. Pictures first: each option shows a real panel today and the same panel with the option drawn in, then a strip of a center's first 24 months. One is recommended.

The gap

Today everything new arrives in three lumps, at the stars, plus the karma shop. Equipment upgrades are bought from the inspector with nothing to research, nothing new turns up between stars, and the room count stops at eighteen. After the third star a center sits on its money with Nothing left to prove. For now. and the Foundation's three gifts as the only thing to spend it on. The bot's three-star Orchard Estate below was at $339,836 on day 900.

The strips use The Exchange with stars at months 4, 8 and 13, a good player's pace on today's code (Max's own games reached its first two at months 3.9 and 6.4; the good bot's median is about 8, 12 and 20). It has massage and acupuncture only, so its third star opens no technique at all. Foundation gifts land where the bot makes them, months 17 and 22. Already designed elsewhere: Grand Occasions moves the third star's techniques and items to the second star and gives the third star a finale. All four options here sit beside it.

A

Option A · recommended

What's Going Around

Every six to eight weeks an ailment goes around town, announced a week ahead with a fanfare, and it opens what treats it, even ahead of its star.

What the player sees

  • Seven days out: the dialog, a PA line and the objectives card.
  • For 30 days about a quarter of new guests have that ailment, all wearing its prop, so one line visibly grows.
  • They pay 25% more while it lasts.

What the player chooses

Chase it or let it pass. Chasing costs a training course, a practitioner off the floor while they learn, maybe a second table or a room, and a crowded line that squeezes the others. Letting it pass costs nothing: Reception tells those guests to come back later.

What arrives, across two years

About ten moments where today has three. Before a star, a wave pulls one of the next star's techniques forward. After the last star, ailments from the other centers visit, and once a year a discipline the center does not have arrives with its room: sound healing at The Exchange.

One new sim rule

For 30 days one named ailment makes up a quarter of arrivals, and whatever treats it is open.

PA: “Cold Feet is going around. Moxibustion is open; train an Acupuncturist now.”

First slice

One ailment going around at The Exchange (Cold Feet, opening Moxibustion), with the warning, the dialog, the card and the PA line, and a bot rerun.

First slice: 5–7 daysWhole: 12–15 days

What could go wrong

  • Chasing could always be right, the "accept every emergency" problem again. The cost has to be real: the bot should sometimes do better letting one pass.
  • A new discipline after the last star touches discipline switches, rooms marked for one center and the guide. That is the big part of the whole.
  • The Foundation stays the only karma. A wave pays only for guests you actually heal, never for ignoring it.

Fits with

The horoscope (Under the Stars) can name next month's ailment; the Rag can headline it; ZenMart Express copies the busiest discipline, which is often the one you just chased. A Grand Occasion is a wave at full scale, so waves pause around one. The folder parked "Wellness Fads" for a later doc; this is that doc's shape, with the town choosing the fad. The Trend Lab wild card could later let the player start one.

B

Option B

The Research Room

Two Point's shape: from the first star, a practitioner at a Research Desk turns days and money into the next thing you choose.

What the player sees and chooses

A Research Room with one desk opens at the first star. The player picks the study: one of the next star's techniques, the next upgrade level for one kind of equipment, or, after the last star, a refinement of a technique. One study at a time per desk; a second desk doubles the pace.

What it costs

$4,000 to $9,000 a study before the last star, $15,000 to $40,000 a refinement after, and a practitioner of that discipline off the floor while they sit at the desk. At The Exchange the refinements come to about $225,000.

One new sim rule

A study moves on one day for each day a practitioner of its discipline works the desk; finished, it opens its thing.

PA: “The Research Desk is empty. Seat a practitioner to continue the study.”

First slice

The room, the desk and the study rule, with the next star's techniques as the only studies.

First slice: 8–10 daysWhole: 20–25 days

What could go wrong

  • Nothing arrives before the first star, and the room competes for money in the mid-center squeeze. It is optional, so no cliff, but the people who need a drip most can least afford it.
  • Refinements are numbers (+5%, shorter sessions) unless each changes what is on screen, which is gap 1's work.
  • Early techniques make stars feel smaller. Stars still open everything not yet studied, and the grants, land and karma.
  • The Foundation stays the karma sink: research earns nothing for the campaign and is spent where it is played.

C

Option C

The Wholesale Catalog

On the 1st of every month, four offers good until month's end: the next star's kit early at a markup, an upgrade kit, a limited piece and a clearance.

What the player sees and chooses

A new issue every month, a toast on the 1st, and a van at the curb when an order comes. The choice is which offer, if any, this month.

What it costs

Money only: +50 to +60% for anything early, $20,000 to $60,000 for the collector's pieces after the last star.

PA: “The catalog van is at the curb. A Caretaker will bring it in.”

First slice

The tab and a monthly draw from items that already exist: the next star's at a markup, plus a clearance.

First slice: 3–4 daysWhole: 8–10 days

What could go wrong

  • Shopping is not play: no new verb on the floor beyond placing what came.
  • It competes with the Foundation for the same pile and does the same job, money in, things out. Collector's pieces must never carry karma.
  • Overlaps the Talismans and the Peddler wild card and the karma shop.
D

Option D

Discipline Ladders

Each discipline climbs its own ladder of heals, and each rung opens one technique or upgrade level; stars keep the rooms, land and new disciplines.

What the player sees and chooses

A ladder under each discipline in the Treatments menu and a toast at each rung. The choice is which discipline to push: where guests go, who is trained, which room is built.

PA: “Acupuncture has reached fifty heals. Electroacupuncture is open to train.”

What it costs

No money at all. It costs attention, and a discipline you neglect stays locked.

First slice: 4–5 daysWhole: 8–10 days, plus star goals rebalanced

What could go wrong

  • It pays for heals the player was making anyway, and the busiest discipline gets everything first: a loop that pushes the way things already go.
  • It tops out around month 16, so after the last star it adds nothing, and it gives the money nowhere to go.
  • Star goals need rework, since stars would open less.

Recommendation

Build What's Going Around

  • It is the Two Point moment the gap names, a new ailment announced with a fanfare that sends you to build and train, and it lands about every two months, in the middle and after the end.
  • The pressure is on the floor where it can be seen: a crowd with the same prop and one line growing, warned a week ahead, with a way out that costs nothing.
  • It uses the cast and content that exist: 62 ailments, the techniques and their kit. One new rule; the first slice is about a week.
  • It is the only option that gives a finished center new rooms and new specialists, so the money goes into play while the Foundation stays the karma sink.

The Research Room is the runner-up: the faithful port, with a real path to choose, but about twice the work, nothing before the first star, and a late game of refinements that are numbers until gap 1 gives them a show. If waves land, a small Research Desk could later be how a player pulls a wave's technique in earlier.
