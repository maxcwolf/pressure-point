What preparing for a mishap looks like

Paying to prepare for a mishap used to change nothing you could see. The storm at the Boardwalk now boards up the windows and sandbags the doors (on main, e8c72b1c). This page shows the same idea for the other three campaign mishaps, and for a Shaman’s ward, as real models in the real game, in each level’s own building and scenery. Each piece sits on the things the mishap would otherwise stop, so the player can see what they protected.

The pieces recommended to build first wear an amber frame. The others are extras: they add character but say nothing the framed pieces don’t already say.

How the pictures were made: a new game in each campaign center in the browser at 1440×900 at noon, with the same small center built in each (a Restroom, a Staff Room, two treatment rooms, a Reception Desk, and a row of snacks and drinks along the front of the Restroom and Staff Room), then the mishap set to prepared. Each level is shown whole, then each piece close up, then the same spot with the mishap under way unprepared, for contrast. High Pines is shot in winter and the Orchard in harvest season, when their mishaps come. Click any picture to see it whole. The models are mockups on the branch claude/prep-cues-mockups; nothing in the simulation changes.

The Boardwalk Built

Storm · Board up and sandbag · $800

Planks across every outer window, and sandbag banks either side of the front door.

This one is built, and is here as the yardstick for the rest. From the day you pay until the storm passes, every window in the building’s outer walls is planked over, the building’s own and those of rooms that run along the outside. A window in a wall cut low toward the camera gets one plank along its strip of glass. Sandbags bank four, three and two high outside the door, past the pillars and clear of the path.

The Exchange

Power cut · Hire a generator · $1,500

A hired generator on a trailer outside the door, its cable running in under a ramp.

A hire company’s yellow generator on a two-wheeled trailer, parked against the front wall beside the door. An orange cable comes out of its end, crosses the doorway inside a yellow-and-black cable ramp, and runs along the inside of the wall to a junction box with a green light. It looks nothing like the sage Generator a center can buy and keep: this one is on wheels and has a hire firm’s name down its side, Generational Wealth plant hire, which suits the Exchange.

During the cut, the same spot
Where it goes
Outside the front door, beside the pillar on the right, parallel to the wall; the cable through the doorway’s edge.
When it shows
From the day you pay until the cut is over. In the mockup it always puffs; built, it would puff only while the cut is on.
What it tells you
“Something is powering the center.” The cable into the building says where the power goes.
Watch out for
It stands on the outdoor paving by the door; a center with a bench or sign there needs it moved along. The owned Generator already holds a power cut off, so with one in the center the hired one never appears.
High Pines

Big freeze · Lag the pipes · $1,200

Foam lagging on the pipe of every toilet, sink, drink and radiator, a tarp over the koi pond, and grit on the path.

Each thing the freeze would stop gets its pipes lagged: a pale gray foam sleeve on each side, rising out of the floor and turning in at the back, with silver tape round it and the copper showing where it goes in. Toilets get theirs inside the stall. The koi pond gets a blue tarp, roped and weighted with a brick on each corner. Outside the door is a yellow grit bin, and grit thrown over the path.

With the freeze on, the same spot
Where it goes
On each toilet, sink, water cooler, tea station, fountain, wall fountain and radiator: one sleeved pipe at each side. A tarp on each koi pond. The grit bin and grit by the front door.
When it shows
From the day you pay until the thaw.
What it tells you
“These are wrapped up against the cold.” It sits on exactly the things the freeze would frost over.
Watch out for
The sleeves stand just outside an item’s sides, so two sinks side by side share a gap; it looks fine at this spacing but could crowd a tight row. The tarp hides the koi, which some players will mind.
The Orchard Estate

Wasps · Call pest control · $900

A wasp trap on a hook beside every snack and drink, mesh lids on the bins, a decoy nest by the door, and the pest control van at the curb.

Beside each snack machine, tea station, kombucha tap, coffee machine and water cooler stands a shepherd’s hook with a glass trap hung from it: amber bait, a yellow funnel lid, two wasps that went in and stayed. Each bin gets a dark green mesh dome held on with a band. A gray paper decoy nest hangs off the door’s pillar, since wasps keep away from another colony’s nest. Out front, a white Buzz Off van with a crossed-out wasp on its roof is parked at the curb.

With the wasps here, the same spot
Where it goes
A trap at the right-hand side of each snack and drink, a mesh lid on each bin, the nest on the door’s right pillar, the van on the near side of the road a few tiles past the door.
When it shows
From the day you pay until the wasps go. The van could come only on the day you pay, and drive off.
What it tells you
“The wasps have been dealt with here.” Each trap stands where a swarm would have been.
Watch out for
A trap stands half a tile out from its item and can stand in a walkway or against the next item. The van is the only piece that reads from the default zoom, but it is the one least tied to what the wasps stop.
A Shaman’s ward

Any center · costs nothing but a Shaman’s time · holds most of the time

A chalk sigil on the floor inside the door, glowing faintly while it holds, smeared when it fails.

Once the Shaman has performed the ward, a chalk circle appears on the floor just inside the front door: two rings, a seven-pointed star, marks round the outside, and a soft lavender glow, with a bowl of sage smoldering beside it. If the ward fails as the mishap starts, the rings break, half the star is scuffed away, the chalk goes gray and the sage goes out. Shown here at the Orchard, against the wasps.

Where it goes
On the floor a tile and a half inside the front door, centered on it. Guests walk over it.
When it shows
From the moment the ward is performed; smeared from the moment it fails until the mishap is over.
What it tells you
“The ward is up”, and if it fails, why the mishap got through anyway.
Watch out for
On the floor it can sit under a Reception Desk placed right by the door. It is the faintest of the five from the default zoom.

Recommendation

Build the framed pieces, and the van

Build the framed pieces for all four: the generator with its cable, the lagging and the tarp, the traps and mesh lids, and the ward’s sigil in both states. Each sits on the things its mishap would stop, which is what tells the player what they bought. Then add the Buzz Off van, because at the Orchard it is the only piece that reads from the default zoom, and the traps alone don’t carry that far. Leave the grit, the decoy nest and the junction box for later; they are nice, but they add little to what the framed pieces already say.

Choices for you
  1. Keep the hire firm’s name, Generational Wealth plant hire, or make it plainer?
  2. The van: parked from the day you pay until the wasps go, or only on the day you pay, then driving off?
  3. The koi pond’s tarp hides the koi for the freeze. Keep it, or lag the pond’s pump instead?
  4. Should a ward that is asked for but not yet performed show anything, such as the Shaman’s chalk lying ready by the door?
