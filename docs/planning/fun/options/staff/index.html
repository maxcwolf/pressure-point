Staff you remember

In Two Point Hospital a member of staff has a face, up to three traits, a line of bio and a breakdown of why they are unhappy, so hiring is an argument about a person and letting one go is a small wrench. Here someone has at most one of twelve quirks, and 35% of applicants have none: six of the seven therapists applying in a late-game Orchard read “No notable quirks”. Morale is a bar with no reason, though moraleTarget knows every reason. So the hire is rank against salary, and the best play churns staff (the bot swapped 404 times in 30 games). Five ways to fix that follow, each with pictures first. One is recommended.

Every picture is the real game: a three-star Orchard Estate the playtest bot built over 900 days, loaded as a save, with the Preact components edited for each mockup. Portraits are drawn by the game’s own character model from each person’s own look. Click any picture to see it larger.

Option 1

Faces

A head-and-shoulders portrait of each person on every card, row and inspector, drawn from their own look.

At hiringA face to pick, which feels like a person. It does not change which applicant is better.
At a raiseNothing changes.
At letting goYou see who you are firing. A small wrench, with nothing behind it.

The verb and the rule

No new verb and no sim rule: it is presentation. It makes every other option land, because a quirk or a record belongs to a face.

Cost

First slice 2 days: one offscreen renderer that draws a look in its uniform and keeps the image, on the Hire cards. Whole 5 days: rows, inspector, Let Go dialog, raise and quit cards, awards, toasts.

What could go wrong

  • At 30 pixels everyone in one role is the same uniform: hair and skin are all that tell them apart.
  • Fifty portraits at load on a slow laptop; draw them when idle and keep them.

Fits Staff Room Politics

The noticeboard’s photos become these portraits.

Option 2

Why the mood

Staged for the picture: Rosalind Calmley’s pay cut to 86% of the going rate. The reasons and points are the real moraleTarget terms, read from the running game.

Under the morale bar, the reasons the simulation already has, each with its points and the fix beside it.

At hiringNothing changes.
At a raiseThe big one. Rosalind wants $3,640, which fixes 13 points; an Air Conditioner fixes 10 and helps everyone in the room. You pick which, or both.
At letting goYou see whether the problem is the person or the place, before you let someone go for the room’s fault.

The verb and the rule

The verb is diagnose and fix. No new rule: moraleTarget becomes the sum of a list of named reasons, so the two can never disagree.

Cost

First slice 1.5 days: the reasons list, the sum, a test that it equals today’s target, the inspector list. Whole 3 days: the worst reason on each row and on the raise request.

What could go wrong

  • It reads as a spreadsheet and makes nobody memorable: it fixes the communication half of the gap, not the people half.
  • In a well-run center there are few reasons to show; in the bot’s save most people had one or two.

Fits challenging but fair

Nothing gets easier; the player finally sees what the game already counts.

Option 3

Quirks with weight

Recommended

Staged for the picture: Tamsin Glide given the rare Burnout Survivor quirk. The other quirks are rolled by the mockup from each person’s id.

Everyone has one to three quirks, good and bad together, and one person in seven has a rare one that is stronger and shows on the floor.

At hiringAn argument with yourself. Brisk but a Grump, or Undervalued but a Sleepyhead? The Senior with nothing special, or the Experienced one who Holds Space, at $2,660?
At a raiseA rare quirk is worth paying for, and nobody else in the pool has it.
At letting goThe real wrench: a better rank comes along every few days, a Burnout Survivor does not. That is a reason not to churn, earned by keeping someone, not a penalty for letting go.

The verb and the rule

The verb is weighing a person. One rule: an applicant rolls up to three common quirks that do not clash (Brisk and Chatterbox cannot meet), and one in seven a rare one at 1.25× pay. Proven with four rare quirks, each through a hook the sim already has:

  • Former Influencer: +1 reputation a heal, 10% slower (the heal and speed hooks).
  • Burnout Survivor: morale floor of 35, a mug about it (updateMorale).
  • Healing Hands: one failed treatment in ten works anyway (the outcome roll).
  • Holds Space: colleagues in the room work 10% faster (the cheeredUp distance check).

Cost

First slice 5 days: the roll, the clash list, chips on cards and rows, the four rare quirks, pay, a save bump that leaves old staff as they are, and a bot rerun. Whole 10 to 12 days: a floor prop for each rare quirk (the mug, the phone held up, the glow) checked in the model lab, and a quirk filter in the Hire menu.

What could go wrong

  • Easier by stealth: rare quirks are power. Their pay is the price; the bot rerun must show star months and quits within the noise, or the base morale comes down.
  • A wall of text on a card. Chips with the effect on hover keep it short; only the rare one is spelled out.
  • Three bad quirks on one person: fine if visible and cheaper, as Undervalued already is.

Fits Staff Room Politics

Its two quirk rules (a Grump sours rapport, a Ray of Sunshine never does) matter more when more people carry a quirk. The two never touch the same code beyond habitOf.

Existing cast first

The twelve quirks stay; nothing is renamed. The Grump, Ray of Sunshine, Litterbug and Green Thumb already show on the floor, which is the model for the rare four.

Option 4

Likes and dislikes

Each person likes one place or thing in the building and can’t stand another: a room, a warm room, the Koi Pond, quiet; Wind Chimes, the cold, being moved about.

At hiringSome weight: the one who likes the Sound Bath Studio when you have one.
At a raiseA cheaper fix than money: move the chimes, assign them to one room.
At letting goLittle change.

The verb and the rule

The verb is suiting the building to a person. One rule: ±5 to the morale target while a like is met or a dislike is near, read from things moraleTarget can already see (room, warmth, items, assignment). Colleagues are left out on purpose: those belong to Staff Room Politics.

Cost

First slice 4 days: eight conditions, the roll, card and inspector lines, a save bump, a bot rerun. Whole 7 days: rows, a heart bubble when a like is met, a dislike shown in Assign to rooms.

What could go wrong

  • It shares a verb with Staff Room Politics (where people are posted), so the two compete for one decision.
  • Thirty staff, sixty small conditions: chores, not people.

Fits challenging but fair

Every condition is visible on the card before hiring; nothing is hidden until later.

Option 5

A file, and a local

Invented for the picture: Clementine Hale, and the record’s dates and events. Guests seen is real.

A line of bio per person and a record the game writes as they work (joined, guests seen, awards, a soul retrieved, a challenge met, ZenMart turned down), plus one named local per center with a small arc, the way Amanda Jablonsky is now.

At hiringOnly for the local: she applies once, and after a season sets you a challenge of her own.
At a raiseNothing changes.
At letting goA wrench: three years, Employee of the Year, the one who turned ZenMart down twice.

The verb and the rule

No verb for the record; it is memory. The local’s rule is one instance of the staff challenge with her name on it, proven at the Orchard first.

Cost

First slice 3 days: the day hired and a short record kept on each person, 30 bio lines by role and quirk, the Let Go line. Whole 10+ days: one local per center with a three-beat arc, five times over, and the writing.

What could go wrong

  • It answers “nobody reads the text” with text.
  • Thirty bio lines repeat across twenty staff within a year.
  • Five locals is the “five of everything” the review warned about.

Fits Staff Room Politics

“Friends with Mo” and a feud are natural lines in the record.

Recommendation

Build Quirks with weight

It is the only option that turns the hire from rank against salary into an argument about a person, and the only one that makes letting someone go cost something you can name: a rare quirk you may not see again. That is the reason to stop churning, earned by keeping people, with no penalty on firing. It grows the system the game already has (twelve quirks, pay that follows them, four that already show on the floor), it is one rule proven with four instances, and the rare quirks put a person’s character on the floor rather than in text. It also feeds Staff Room Politics, whose Grump and Ray of Sunshine rules need more people carrying a quirk.

The first slice is about five days and needs a bot rerun to prove it is not easier. Faces would be the natural next step, since a rare quirk belongs to a face. Why the mood is small and pure communication, if you want it alongside.
