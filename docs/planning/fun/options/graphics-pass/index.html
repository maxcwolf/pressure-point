A better-looking game for about a tenth more frame time

Eleven upgrades to the picture, each built behind its own switch on the branch feat/graphics-pass and shot in the late-game Orchard save (17 rooms, 182 items, about 50 people). With no switch named, the game draws exactly as it does in 0.36.3. Pick what ships; the framed pictures are what I would ship.

Click any picture to enlarge it. Try any of it yourself from the worktree’s dev server by adding to the address, for example ?fx=all&walk=character&people=seasonal or ?fx=grounding,lawn.

Everything on, against today

Cost of everything together: about 10% more frame time on this Mac (8.3 to 9.1 ms at pixel ratio 2), 30 more draw calls on 1,850, and 26,000 fewer triangles, because the trimmed people pay for the grass.

1

Grounding

Recommended

Soft warm shade where walls and furniture meet the floor, so the building stops looking pasted on.

Cost: four draw calls. A floor repaint goes from 0.6 to 1.6 ms, and the floor is only repainted when the world changes. Gap: a tall cabinet and a low mat get the same pool.

2

Soft shadows

Recommended

The sun’s shadows get a smooth, wider edge.

Cost: no draw calls; not measurable in frame time. A small change at play distance. It patches a piece of three.js’s shared shader code, guarded so it does nothing if a three.js upgrade changes that code.

3

Sky reflections

Your call

Window glass and glossy surfaces reflect a soft sky.

Cost: no draw calls; not measurable. Overall color moves about 1%. The gain is modest away from windows, it also patches shared three.js shader code, and it does not yet follow the evening tint or a storm’s gray.

4

Ground

Recommended

Turf, sand, snow and paving with some life in them, in every setting.

Cost: two draw calls and about 46,000 triangles; not measurable in frame time. I would turn it down a notch before shipping: the mowing stripes and the orchard’s lanes are strong from far back, and the dark blade strokes in courtyards look busy close up.

5

Wind

Recommended

Trees, canopies, pines, hedges and tufts sway, and their shadows with them; stronger in a storm.

Cost: none in draw calls or triangles; done on the graphics card. The sway is one or two pixels at play distance, so it is gentle; three constants raise it. Held still under reduced motion.

6

Wildlife

Recommended

Sparrows on the lawn, butterflies in spring and summer, gulls at the seaside, pigeons downtown, leaves falling in autumn.

Cost: two or three draw calls, about 1,000 triangles. Birds and butterflies are three to five pixels at play distance, so many players will only notice them zoomed in. The falling leaves are barely visible at play distance. None of it is red, amber or green, and all of it is off under reduced motion.

7

Glow

Recommended

Salt lamps, candles, embers and screens get a soft halo, so they read as light.

Cost: one draw call for every halo in the building. Subtle at play distance; two constants raise it. A real bloom pass was tried and dropped: it shifted the game’s colors, bloomed speech bubbles and floors instead of lamps, and cost 15 to 25% at pixel ratio 2. Entrance and street lamps have no halo yet.

8

Small moments

Recommended

Dust when something lands, a better cure, twinkles on a star, and twelve items that no longer sit dead still.

Cost: nothing at rest; a draw call or two while an effect plays. Also moving: plants, bonsai and topiary stir, the salt lamp breathes, candles flicker, the wall fountain runs, the crystal grid glints, embers and moxa smolder. To fix before shipping: the dust is a grayish tan that could be read as smoke, and a hovered plant shows a still copy over the swaying one.

9

The walk

Recommended

Three new walks to compare with today’s. All ease into the first step, settle on the last, and stop the legs racing at speeds 2 and 3.

  • Weighted: a real walk cycle kept gentle: the foot planted for most of the step, the shoe rolling heel to toe, hips swaying, the head steady.
  • Bouncy: a spring off each step, a little squash on landing, loose arms.
  • Character (framed): weighted, plus a stride of each person’s own and a walk for how they feel, so a hurrying, worn-out, fed-up or unwell person can be told from across the room.

Cost: no draw calls; about 0.12 ms a frame for 50 people. Watch it move before deciding: I could only judge stills. At speed 3 the feet glide, which is the price of calm legs; worn out and unwell both read as hunched; at play size weighted looks close to today.

10

People

Recommended

Tidier clay bodies at nearly half the triangles, clothes with shape, and dressing for the weather.

Cost: people’s triangles fall 26% at the default distance and 39% close up, for about 20 more draw calls that do not grow with the crowd. Each variant includes the one before it. Gaps: skirts, coats and robes are rigid, so thighs push through at full stride and they hang through seats; a guest stripping on a bad trip keeps the hat; people keep the clothes they arrived in when the season turns.

Recommendation

Ship the framed ones; leave sky reflections for later

Grounding and the ground do the most for the picture at the distance the game is played at, and seasonal people are the only change you can see in the crowd from there. Wind, wildlife, glow and the small moments are quiet by design and nearly free. Soft shadows are a small gain for nothing. Sky reflections are the weakest for the risk they carry. The character walk is my pick of the three, but it is the one choice here that has to be watched moving.

Choices for you
  1. Which walk: character, weighted, bouncy, or keep today’s? And is the glide at speed 3 acceptable?
  2. People: seasonal, or stop at dressed or clay?
  3. The ground: as shown, or toned down (fainter stripes and lanes, fewer blade strokes)?
  4. Sky reflections: in or out?
  5. Glow and the plants’ sway are subtle at play distance. Leave them subtle, or turn them up?
  6. Should the whole pass sit behind a Graphics setting (Full or Plain) for slower computers, or simply be how the game looks?

Next, once the look is settled: a pass over the weakest item models (television, wall fountain, water cooler, bin and cabinet, koi pond, snack machine), sway for skirts and robes, and halos for the entrance and street lamps.
