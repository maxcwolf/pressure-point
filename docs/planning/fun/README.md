# Making it more fun: ideas, ranked

Drafted 27 October 2026, and re-ranked the same day after three reviews. A list to choose from, not a plan: nothing here is decided. An idea moves to [planning/todo.md](../todo.md) or [planning/roadmap.md](../roadmap.md) once it is chosen.

The core loop is solid and enjoyable. This list looks for what would make the game more fun and more its own: funnier, more surprising, with more stories, and less like any other management game. It draws on the Two Point games, Theme Hospital and other good games.

The first draft came from six agents. Five brainstormed from different angles: Two Point deep cuts, mechanics from other games, the wellness industry as satire, stories and characters, and structure and replayability. A sixth read the audits, the balance log and the notes on how Max and Playtester 1 play, to find where the fun sags. About 90 ideas came back, about 55 once merged.

**[review.md](review.md)** is a critique of the first seven docs by a review session, which Max read and asked the docs to be revised against. Its rules now shape every doc:

- each idea names the verb, or the thing on the floor, it adds;
- one new sim rule, proven with one instance before more are written, and the rest in a short "Later, if it lands" list;
- pressure on the floor where it can be seen, not in a multiplier;
- incentives that point the right way: nothing pays the player for ignoring it, punishes a good finish, or hands out cash for nothing;
- the player does not book their own exam, and the game does not go quiet for a finale;
- the existing cast before a new one, and no new Brent;
- PA lines of 8 to 14 words that give instructions and never comment, satire aimed at wellness (office-speak only at The Exchange), nothing already seen on the internet, and the game's American register.

- the writing and the satire;
- the clay people and their symptom props;
- systems only a healing center could have: noise, bad vibes, set and setting, linens, the worsening condition;
- the fairness grammar of warning twice, then giving counterplay;

Why these nine:

- **The Rag and the Stars** are the two Max called great, and both lead with a cheap slice that tells the player something true: what happened last month, and what is due this month.
- **Grand Occasions** goes after the biggest structural sag, the third star, and its first phase is one function.
- **Make It Right** is the fairness rule's missing step: after a guest is let down, a way back that costs a slot at the front of a line, not money. It works at every center.
- **Staff Room Politics** gives the people a reason to be kept, and the floor something new to show: feuds in corridors, photos on a board.
- **ZenMart Express** turns the rival from a scoreboard into an opponent that copies what is working, and is the first thing to give one technique's price a reason to move.
- **Couples and Groups** and **Courses of Treatment** make the rooms and the disciplines work together: two staff free at once in one room, and a guest who needs two treatments in order. Both put the puzzle in the layout, the game's strongest system.
- **Offers With a Catch** fixes "accept everything" with choices rather than harsher penalties, and is mostly data and writing.

### If you build three

Build **the third-star unlock**, **the Rag's headline** and **the horoscope**. All three are small, two are the ideas Max called great, and the third fixes the biggest structural sag. None needs the others.

### Where to start, in order

Each step ships alone.

1. **Third-star content at the second star** (S, Grand Occasions phase 1). Today the most exotic content in the game (Distance Reiki, Chakra Balancing, Overtone Chanting, the Ayahuasca Ceremony, the Vision Quest, the Gong of Last Resort, the Spirit Fog Machine, sprinklers) opens with the last star and is never used to earn anything. Only techniques, items and sprinklers move. Upgrade level 3 and the third-star plots go by stars earned and stay. The Orchard Estate's rule needs care. It needs a bot rerun.
2. **The Rag's headline** (S).
3. **ZenMart's warnings** (S): a toast before each sale and each job offer, which today come with no warning at all. This breaks the warned-ahead rule. It is Kyle's first slice, and needs no Kyle.
4. **The monthly horoscope** (M).
5. **Make It Right** (S–M).
6. **The Fun Run** (M), the proof of Grand Occasions.
7. **Staff Room Politics' one rule** (S–M).
8. **ZenMart Express** (S–M), once ZenMart's warnings are in.
9. **Couples** (M), the first slice of Couples and Groups.

Then look at what landed. If the headline is read, build the paper. If the Fun Run works, build the other occasions. Then Mercury on the machines, Courses of Treatment's first three packages, and Offers With a Catch.

## New centers

Each would be a sixth or later campaign center. Max likes the first two, which now have center docs. [plans/campaign.md](../../plans/campaign.md) already plans a sixth, "an old wing to restore", which St. Agatha's below builds on. Gate 44's doc proposes going sixth instead, so the order is a decision to make.

1. **[Gate 44 Serenity Lounge](gate-44.md),** an airport lounge airside.
   - Every guest's flight is on the departures board from the moment they arrive. At final call they go, healed or not, and a guest still on the table runs for the gate in a robe and drops a slipper.
   - The verb is fitting people in: who goes first, who goes to an Express room, who will not make it.
   - Delays are posted two days ahead: more time, but delayed travelers sleep across the benches.
   - The machine is the Body Clock, the mishap is fog, and the occasion is the Busiest Travel Day of the Year. The paper is _The Layover_: "Read it twice. You have time."
   - "Please remove all grudges and place them in a gray tray."
2. **[The MS Serenity](ms-serenity.md),** a cruise ship's spa deck.
   - The manifest has 150 named passengers at tables of eight, aboard for a month-long voyage.
   - A passenger you let down walks the promenade under a rain cloud, and their table comes in short of patience. The verb is to comp them a treatment and make it right. A delighted passenger sends their table in.
   - Ports on days 10 and 20 change the pace.
   - The machine is the Turndown Service, which folds guests into towel swans. The mishap is the Muster Drill, and the occasion is the Crossing. The paper is _The Serenity Daily_: "Printed daily. Delivered monthly."
   - "Passengers are reminded that the towel animals are decorative. Please do not feed them."
3. **The Abbey of Perpetual Calm.**
   - No prices: every guest leaves a donation set by how the visit felt, with the reason on a floating line ("+$180: lovely quiet room, no wait").
   - A vow of silence makes noise the main pressure.
   - The staff are Brothers and Sisters.
   - The announcer holds up hand-lettered cards.
   - It is the one idea that does away with the prices lever nobody touches (gap 8).
4. **St. Agatha's Rest Cure Sanatorium,** the planned old wing.
   - An 1890s building, full and badly laid out, with everything broken, on a trustees' loan.
   - Victorian residents from the 1893 waiting list still stand in the lines.
   - New machine: the Galvanic Restorative Cabinet.
5. **Synergy Tower, Floor 31,** a corporate wellness floor.
   - No guest pays. A quarterly HR scorecard pays bonuses and claws them back.
   - Its satire is office satire, which the review keeps to The Exchange, so it would share that vein.
6. **Wellfest,** a healing field at a music festival.
   - Marquees, and set times that move a big noise blob across the field.
   - Mud from footfall.

## Modes

Ranked:

1. **Today's Intention.**
   - A seeded daily challenge, about one season long, with two or three horoscope mutators: "Venus in Accounts: prices locked at 130%", "Moon in Reception: one Receptionist, ever".
   - A weekly challenge runs a full year.
   - A streak calendar, a cosmetic poster per tier, and a share line you can copy, Wordle-style. No karma.
   - Saves already replay from their seed, so this is cheap.
   - Fun 4, unique 3, fit 4, cheap 4. M.
2. **Retreat,** a roguelite year in a borrowed hall (a church hall, a disused bank, a houseboat).
   - At each season's end you pick one of three Offerings and accept one of two Omens. An Offering might be "Free-Range Goat: eats litter"; an Omen might be "Inspector on Retainer: an inspection every month".
   - A journal between runs unlocks venues and Offerings, not karma.
   - Fun 5, unique 4, fit 4, cheap 2. XL.
3. **The Brochure Shoot.**
   - A photo mode that scores what is in frame when you start a marketing campaign:
     - points for grins, sparkles caught mid-burst and prestige items;
     - points off for litter and for a Grump.
   - The score sets the campaign's strength.
   - Weak shots get honest captions: "A guest, possibly relaxed."
   - Fun 4, unique 5, fit 4, cheap 3. M.
4. **Old Hands.**
   - Staff you raised to Senior or Expert join a roster that carries between centers. Bring up to two to a new center, at full wage and a relocation fee ("Moving expenses (one gong)").
   - Finished centers send monthly memos.
   - Fun 4, unique 3, fit 4, cheap 3. L.
5. **Accreditation,** New Game+ for a three-starred center.
   - Reshuffled plots, a new local complaint, ZenMart Platinum.
   - Heat levels you pick from a list of stated penalties, as in Hades.
   - "Accredited by the Board of Certifying Accreditors (Accredited)."
   - Fun 4, unique 3, fit 3, cheap 4. M.

## Wild cards

The biggest swings and the weirdest ideas. Any one of these could become the thing the game is known for, or a month spent on something that doesn't land. Ranked:

1. **The Trend Lab.**
   - Invent a wellness fad: a discipline, a gimmick and a crowd ("Goat Reiki for Executives").
   - A hidden fit table scores it. A hit brings a temporary trend ailment ("Goat Deficiency"), and ZenMart copies it a month later as "Goat Reiki Express™".
   - A recipe book remembers the combinations across centers.
   - Fun 5, unique 5, fit 5, cheap 2.
2. **The Cosmic Ledger.**
   - A gauge for each center that tracks shady choices: bribing the inspector, mushrooms under the counter, online certificates.
   - When it tips, the universe collects in a run of announced coincidences: "The universe would like a word."
   - It ties every shady option in the game into one trade-off.
   - Fun 4, unique 5, fit 5, cheap 4.
3. **Ley Lines and the Dowser.**
   - Hidden energy lines under each plot, shown only where a Dowser has walked with bent rods: "Two coat hangers, bent with intent. $400."
   - A table on a node works better. "A node under the restroom is, regrettably, also a node."
   - Fun 4, unique 5, fit 5, cheap 4.
4. **The Cucumber Bandit.**
   - A whodunit at each center. Clues appear in the world: cucumber slices outside rooms at certain times.
   - Accuse a member of staff from their inspector. If you're wrong, they file a grievance written on a paper towel.
   - A new verb for the genre: deduction.
   - Fun 4, unique 5, fit 4, cheap 3.
5. **Legendary Guests.**
   - One mythical visitor per center, whose likes you work out from their letter:
     - a Yeti at High Pines who hates radiators;
     - a lost Minotaur at The Exchange who needs a short path from the door ("I will arrive at nine. I may not find the room.");
     - the Previous Owner at The Orchard Estate, a gentle ghost who hates new things.
   - Fun 5, unique 4, fit 4, cheap 2.
6. **House Brand.**
   - Launch a product line from the Shop Counter: a base, and up to three claims ("Activates your inner vowel").
   - Each claim raises sales and Trading Standards risk. The officer asks "The candle claims to 'cure Mondays.' Does it?", and the only answer is "Spiritually."
   - Fun 4, unique 4, fit 5, cheap 3.
7. **The Inner Circle.**
   - Devoted regulars and happy staff build Devotion, spent on rituals: a Gratitude Circle, a Vision Board Burning.
   - Each one nudges a Culty-o-Meter, whose top level is "Documentary Pending".
   - Fun 4, unique 5, fit 5, cheap 2.
8. **Talismans and the Peddler.**
   - Five charm slots over Reception.
   - A van parks at the curb every couple of weeks with three random charms that combine, Balatro-style. Rerolling is "Ask if he has anything else in the van."
   - Fun 5, unique 4, fit 3, cheap 2.
9. **The House Rules.**
   - A staff handbook signed one page at a time, as in Frostpunk: "Shoes Off in the Corridors", "Casual Fridays: robes are now technically business wear".
   - It forks into Hustle Wellness or The Commune.
   - Fun 4, unique 4, fit 5, cheap 2.
10. **The Feed.**
    - Influencers pose in your most photogenic spot and post about you, which brings a week's free campaign or a dent in reputation.
    - The Selfie Wall: "A wall. Painted pink. Guests will queue to stand in front of it. We don't understand it either."
    - Fun 4, unique 3, fit 5, cheap 3.
11. **Biohackers and the Brr-ometer.**
    - A cold-plunge tub that chills the tiles around it.
    - Guests with Overclocked want a "protocol": three treatments in a set order.
    - "Would the gentleman in the cold plunge please stop telling people about his testosterone."
    - Fun 4, unique 4, fit 5, cheap 2.
12. **Behind the Curtain.**
    - Guests who see backstage lose the illusion: the overflowing hamper, the Generator, a therapist vaping behind the Aura Wash.
    - The answer is staff-only doors and screens: "Hides what is behind it. Some say that is all wellness is."
    - Fun 3, unique 4, fit 5, cheap 3.
13. **The Tincture Bench.**
    - An herb garden on a plot grows local ingredients.
    - Recipes are discovered by combining them, among them "Calm Down Linda" and "Seaweed Smoothie of Regret".
    - Fun 4, unique 4, fit 4, cheap 2.
14. **The Detox Bar.**
    - IV drip stands that tangle in narrow corridors.
    - Guests on a three-day cleanse refuse snacks and turn hangry: "I'm FINE. I'm releasing TOXINS."
    - Fun 3, unique 3, fit 5, cheap 3.
15. **The Testimonial Wall.**
    - Pin before-and-after shots of cured guests in the lobby, or "enhance" a mediocre review at a risk: "That's not what I said. I said it was 'fine'."
    - Fun 3, unique 4, fit 5, cheap 3.
16. **Memberships.**
    - Loyal regulars pay monthly, jump the queue, and threaten to cancel.
    - "To cancel your membership, please visit reception between three and three-oh-one AM."
    - Fun 3, unique 3, fit 5, cheap 3.
17. **The Leaving Do.**
    - A long-serving Expert retires, with a season's notice and a cake in the Staff Room.
    - They leave a keepsake ("Ray's Mug. Says 'World's Okayest Healer'.") and a protégé.
    - Fun 3, unique 4, fit 4, cheap 3.
18. **Little Ambitions.**
    - Each hire wants one small thing: "Has never seen the sea", "Wants to be Caretaker of the Year. Has already written the speech."
    - Fun 3, unique 3, fit 4, cheap 3.
