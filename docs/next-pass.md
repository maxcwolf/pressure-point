# Next pass: advice that can be followed, prices that bite, side income in proportion, demand that grows

A plan, 21 October 2026, for the pass after the fair-challenge one. It comes out of the bot sessions' findings (balance log 32 and 33, and the fairness report in [balance-harness.md](balance-harness.md)): the warnings work but the advisor's growth advice loses games; every play style sits at its price cap because prices barely put anyone off; the Qi Gong Studio is the best room in the game because its demand grows with the spots built; an extra treatment room brings only 1.5 to 2 guests a month, so the smaller, dearer center out-earns the bigger one. The rule from [fair-challenge-research.md](fair-challenge-research.md) stands: the game must not get easier; it warns before it punishes, names the lever, and leaves time for it to work.

Four pieces of work. The first three are independent and run together; the fourth moves every number and goes last, measured against the other three.

| Piece                          | Session effort | Yardstick                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------ | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A. Advice that can be followed | medium         | `BOT=advised` no longer loses the Willow Lane and High Pines games it lost in log 32           |
| B. Prices that bite            | medium         | the play styles stop settling on their caps; the good bot's profit holds within about 15%      |
| C. Side income in proportion   | medium         | a studio earns about one treatment room per Instructor; house calls under 10% of income        |
| D. Demand that grows           | high           | the hoarder stops out-earning the good bot; the Orchard survives; the summer spiral rate falls |

Each session branches from `integrate-audit` (with the three bot branches merged in, for the personas, styles and report), commits locally with lowercase Conventional Commits, never pushes or opens a pull request, and finishes with `yarn typecheck`, `yarn lint`, `yarn test`, a `yarn balance` comparison through `yarn balance:report after.json before.json`, a balance-log entry in the log's format, a guide sentence where the player sees the change, and one CHANGELOG line.

## A. Advice that can be followed

**Why.** Log 32: a player who does what the advisor says loses more games at Willow Lane than one who ignores it, and earns High Pines' first star at month 47 in one seed. The bottleneck advice asks for rooms and staff on lines of four to seven without weighing the payroll; the countdown says "prices up" when the pace it is measured against is a month long and only cutting staff works inside ten days; the broken-item line sits on top of everything for days; "borrow it" is said while the books lose money, which spends the loan room the next payday needs.

**Files.** `src/core/sim/advisor.ts` (`advice`, `bottleneckAdvice`), `src/core/sim/bottleneck.ts`, the month-end and countdown toasts in `src/core/sim/economy.ts` (`monthEnd`'s in-the-red toast, `callInOverdraft`, `updateInsolvency`), `src/core/format.ts` for an article helper if there is none, tests in `tests/unit` beside the existing advisor and economy tests, `docs/guide.md` item 19, `CHANGELOG.md`.

**Changes.**

1. **The bottleneck advice weighs the payroll.** Before it says "hire another" or "another table and a therapist to work it", estimate the new salary (the cheapest applicant in that role, or the role's base pay from `src/core/data/staff.ts`) and ask whether `cashflow(s).net` less that salary stays at or above zero, and whether the room and a month of the new wage are in the bank. If not, keep the diagnosis but change the lever: "Guests wait longest for the massage table: 6 waiting for one. Another therapist would put the books $1,800 a month in the red: raise prices, or wait for the pace to grow." While the bank is calling the overdraft in, or a payday warning has said payday is not covered this month (`s.insolvency`, or `payrollOutlook(s).covered !== 'yes'`), the growth advice is not given at all; the money advice comes first.
2. **The countdown names the lever that works in time, and who.** The advisor's countdown line and the two economy toasts (the overdraft called in, the halfway reminder) say that the pace is measured over the last month or so, so a price rise barely moves it in ten days, and that wages come off the pace at once. Name the practitioner to let go: in a role with two or more, the one whose station has been busy least over the last few days (`stages()` in bottleneck.ts tracks `busy` per item), with their wage and how far it moves the books: "Letting Priya go ($2,400 a month, her table busy 31% of the time) puts the books $600 a month in profit." Where no role has two, say so and name the other way out: above zero by selling or earning.
3. **The broken-item advice stops sticking.** With a Caretaker on the payroll it drops below the money, line and comfort advice in the chain, and it is not repeated for an item a Caretaker is already heading to or repairing (read the Caretaker's task; if nothing records it, say so and fall back to the order change alone). Fix the article: "An Air Conditioner", with a small `an()` helper in format.ts if none exists.
4. **No "borrow it" while the books lose money.** The month-end toast's "the bank will still lend $X: borrow it from the Money menu" and the advisor's "a loan from the Money menu is cheaper" lead with the cut when `projectedCashflow(s) < 0`: the loan buys time and uses the room the next payday needs; prices up or staff down first, then borrow for what is left. The wording when the books make money stays.

**Measure.** `BOT=advised yarn balance` and the default bot, seeds 1 to 3, four years, before and after. The advised persona matches toasts and advice by text prefix (`decideAdvised` in `tests/balance/bot.ts`); if a prefix changes, update the persona's match to the new words without changing what it does. Log entry 34: the log-32 table before → after for the advised persona, and the default bot unchanged to within a month on stars. Done when the advised persona loses no game at Willow Lane in seeds 1 to 3, High Pines' first star comes within the baseline's months, and The Boardwalk's gain from log 32 stays.

## B. Prices that bite

**Why.** Log 33: prices put off only 1% to 6% of guests at any level, so every style settles at its cap (1.4, 1.2 or 1.6) and the sweet spot is the cap. The rule today (`src/core/sim/stations.ts`, after a diagnosis) is a flat `max(0, (price − 1) × 0.35)` chance that the guest leaves, never for a regular or an emergency guest. The research's model (CorsixTH's price distortion, in `docs/research/two_point_and_theme_hospital.md`) has the tolerated price rise with reputation.

**Files.** `src/core/data/rules.ts` (a `PRICE_PUT_OFF` table), `src/core/sim/economy.ts` (a `putOffChance(price, reputation)` helper), `src/core/sim/stations.ts` (use it), the Money menu's Prices tab (`src/ui/components/menus/MoneyMenu.tsx` and its lib) to show the chance, tests in `tests/unit`, `docs/guide.md` items 12 and 20, `CHANGELOG.md`.

**Changes.**

1. **Measure first.** From a default `yarn balance` run, compute the real share of diagnosed guests put off at each price level the bot used (priced over healed + failed + priced + worse + stormed, from the month logs), and write it in the log entry, so the new curve is fitted to what happens rather than to the formula.
2. **A curve that is gentle to 1.3 and steep past it.** Fit `putOffChance` so that, at 50% reputation, about 3% of diagnosed guests balk at 1.2, 10% at 1.4, 20% at 1.6 and half at 2.0, and shift it by reputation: at 90% reputation the same shares come about 0.15 higher on the slider, at 20% about 0.15 lower. Put the knots in `PRICE_PUT_OFF` with a comment giving these targets. Regulars and emergency guests still never balk. Each technique's own price multiplies into the price as it does now.
3. **The cost is visible before it bites.** On the Prices tab, beside the overall level and each technique's own price, show the chance at this reputation: "About 10% of guests balk at this price; 20% at 1.6." Keep the existing counts of guests put off this month and last. The guide says the same in a sentence.
4. **The bot's target means something now.** The good bot aims for a 10% put-off share (`PUT_OFF` in the style table); check it settles below its cap after the change, and leave the styles' caps as they are.

**Measure.** `BOT_STYLE=good`, `hoarder` and `grower` over seeds 1 to 3, four years, before and after, through the report. Log entry 35: the price levels each style settles at (months 12, 24, 48), profit a month, stars, and the share put off. Done when the three styles no longer all sit on their caps, the good bot's profit a month is within about 15% of before at every center, its stars come within a month on average, and the hoarder's year-four money edge at Willow Lane and High Pines shrinks.

## C. Side income in proportion

**Why.** Log 33 and the audit's recommendation 6: one Instructor brings in $16,000 to $22,000 a month against about $10,000 from a treatment room, because class-goer interest grows with the spots built (`weeklyInterest` in `src/core/sim/classes.ts` is `spots × 2.5 × (0.4 + reputation/83) × (0.85 + 0.05 × skill)`), from a pool of its own that no line or building limits. House calls pay $1,200 a day at rank 1 and twice that at Expert, times the price level, for one to three days away while the line grows; they are 7% to 22% of income. Class size was cut from 16 to 12 in log 22 for the crowd a class let out; it stays at 12, since the size is a capacity, not a demand knob.

**Files.** `src/core/sim/classes.ts` (`weeklyInterest`), `src/core/data/rules.ts` (`CLASS_INTEREST` becomes a weekly base; `CALL_PER_DAY`, `CALL_RANK_BONUS`), `src/core/sim/calls.ts` (`callBase`, and the offer's text), the studio's room inspector and the house-call offer card in `src/ui` (find them by the strings they show), `tests/unit/classes.test.ts` and the calls tests, `docs/guide.md` items 14 and 20, `CHANGELOG.md`.

**Changes.**

1. **Class-goer interest follows reputation, not spots.** `weeklyInterest(reputation, skill)` becomes `CLASS_WEEKLY × (0.4 + reputation/83) × (0.85 + 0.05 × skill)`, with spots only capping each class in `classSize`, which does not change. Start from `CLASS_WEEKLY` of 14: about 13 class-goers a week at 50% reputation (three classes of four), 21 at 80% (three of seven), 25 at 100% (three of eight), so an Expert's class of 12 fills only at a top reputation with few class days, and switching on more class days spreads the same people thinner as it does now. Calibrate from the bot so that a studio's class income per Instructor in year four lands between 0.8 and 1.3 times a treatment room's take at the same center, using the hoarder (which builds studios) and the good bot; write the number and the figures in the log entry. The two existing tips (an Instructor's class size, a full class) stay right; check their words.
2. **The demand is visible.** The Qi Gong Studio's inspector says it: "About 21 class-goers a week come for classes at this reputation, shared among 3 class days." The guide's item 14 says that interest grows with reputation, and that spots are how many a class can hold.
3. **House calls priced against the center.** Bring `CALL_PER_DAY` toward what a practitioner earns at home in a day (a table treats about one guest in two days at the fees on offer; work it out and say it in the log) and `CALL_RANK_BONUS` to 0.15, so a call is a fair day's pay, not several. The offer card says what the call costs at home: how many wait in that practitioner's line and the days of condition the worst of them has left, so the trade is visible when it is offered.

**Measure.** `BOT_STYLE=good`, `hoarder` and `grower`, seeds 1 to 6, four years, before and after; the report's side-income column is the figure. Log entry 36. Done when class income per Instructor sits within the target band at the centers that build a studio, house calls are under 10% of the good bot's year-four income, the hoarder's year-four money edge at Willow Lane disappears, and the good bot's stars come within a month on average.

## D. Demand that grows with the building

**Why.** The audit's recommendation 5, measured in log 33: an extra room brings 1.5 to 2 guests a month, nobody is turned away in four years (the building's limit of 14 plus 5 a room never binds), and the smaller, dearer center out-earns the bigger one in year four at Willow Lane and High Pines. Building wins stars, not money, so the rational opening is the smallest one. Today a guest arrives every `(30 − 0.2 × reputation)` seconds, times the center's twist, the grade, 5% a staffed discipline past the first (`offeringArrivals`) and the lines' put-off (`admitGuests` in `src/core/sim/guests.ts`, `src/core/sim/demand.ts`). This piece runs after A, B and C have landed, since it moves every number, and it goes to a high-effort session.

**Files.** `src/core/sim/guests.ts` (`admitGuests`, `guestCap`), `src/core/sim/demand.ts` (`offeringArrivals` → `capacityArrivals`), `src/core/data/rules.ts`, `src/ui/lib/demand.ts` and the Guests tab (the arrivals breakdown), `tests/unit` demand and guests tests, `tests/balance/bot.ts` only if its room valuation (`roomValue`) needs the new rule, `docs/guide.md` item 19, `docs/deep-dive-audit.md` recommendation 5 ticked, `CHANGELOG.md`.

**Changes.**

1. **Arrivals rise with staffed treatment capacity.** Replace the 5% per discipline with a factor on staffed treatment stations: usable tables and mats whose role has someone on the payroll who is not leaving (the bottleneck's `staffed` share over the last days is the better measure where it is cheap to read). Start from `1 + 0.12 × (stations − 1)`, capped at 2.5, so six staffed tables draw 1.6 times the guests of one. Reputation stays the base. The difficulty knob for arrivals keeps working on top.
2. **A guest limit that binds.** `guestCap` is 14 plus 5 a finished room and never binds. Set it from treatment capacity instead, so a center that has outgrown its tables turns guests away rather than filling the corridors: start from 8 plus 4 per staffed treatment station plus 2 per consulting room, and calibrate so that a settled good-bot center turns away a few guests a month in its second year and average waits fall into the 5 to 10 day range the audit asked for, while the player's own intake cap still sits below it.
3. **The breakdown says why.** The Guests tab's arrivals readout and the calendar hover explain the pace: the base at this reputation, times the staffed-capacity factor with the count behind it, times the season or rush hour, less the lines' put-off, and the limit with how many were turned away this month. The guide's sentence on 5% a discipline goes, replaced by this.
4. **The Orchard's opening is the test case.** Five disciplines from day one means eight staffed stations and 1.84 times the arrivals in the first month: check the good bot and the baseline both get through the first three paydays, and that the first payday's size is shown before the bank lends (the audit's note on `FIRST_PAYROLL` and the day-0 build).

**Measure.** All three styles and `BOT=baseline`, seeds 1 to 6, four years, before and after, through the report. Log entry 37. Done when: the good bot's year-four profit is at least the hoarder's at Willow Lane and High Pines; the Orchard survives every seed for the good bot and the baseline; the good bot's stars come no more than a month later on average, and sooner at The Exchange; the Boardwalk summer's share of spirals in the report falls from 92% toward the background rate; waits in settled centers sit between 5 and 10 days; and the baseline's months at a loss are no worse than in log 31.

### Open findings

What the passes turned up and left for the owner, in the order they matter.

1. **The lots are too small.** The owner's judgement, 21 October 2026: larger areas to expand are a necessary addition. The bot's games bear it out. In 28 of 30 games a new treatment room stopped fitting 3 to 23 months before the second star, when the machines unlock, and in 23 of those every plot was already bought (entry 38); the audit found late-game money with nowhere to go ([gameplay-audit.md](gameplay-audit.md)); and the only way the bot ever had a Kneadmaster was by knocking a Massage Suite down for it (entry 37). The next round of plots is for [land-plan.md](land-plan.md), under "Decisions to make": more plots a center, some opening at the second and third stars, sized so a machine room (4×5 to 3×7 tiles) fits beside the tables. Not easier: land is bought, and the running costs stay.
2. **The Orchard's opening.** The good bot still loses 4 of 12 Orchard games, all the same way: the day-0 build takes the loan to $50,000 by month 2, then a third month heals 0 to 2 guests while the practitioners are away on courses and house calls, and the bank calls the overdraft in. Every loss is warned (the countdown, and 19 warnings in the last 30 days of seed 5). The fix is communication at the opening, not a cheaper center: the payroll the first month will owe shown before the center opens, and the advisor against hiring past what the first payday can carry. The demand session left this on a card.
3. **High Pines: the hoarder still edges the good bot,** $28.4k against $26.8k a month in year 4. The setting that wins it back, five stations a Consultation Room, loses The Orchard three more seeds in twelve, and the gap is inside what one setting moves High Pines between runs, so the rule stays at four. Worth a look again after the land (1).
4. **The machines.** The Kneadmaster pays back on its own books by day 601, at about $2.8k a guest against a $15,000 room and $1,500 a month to run, but it heals about 2 guests a month where a Massage Suite in the same game heals 6 or 7: it counts as one station and draws no more guests than the table it stands in place of, so the game with it makes $8.1k a month less. That is a demand problem, not a price or upkeep one. The Pin Press, Aura Wash, Great Alphorn and Cider Press were never built once the bot stopped knocking rooms down, so their prices and upkeep ($2,000 to $4,000 a month) are unmeasured. Two decisions, either or both: land for a machine room (1), and a machine that draws guests of its own, its two ailments arriving only once it stands and its room pulling like a finest room. Measure again with `BOT_ON=machines` once one is in.
5. **Icons to draw.** Done, 22 October 2026 (branch `feat/machine-icons-and-models`): the eleven machine items, six room signs, six techniques and the cider feeling have drawings of their own, the eleven machine models are held to the guideline in `models/parts.ts` with fewer materials each, and a machine that can be upgraded shows its level on the same brass plate of stars as the tables.
6. **The bot's blind spots.** It never fires anyone of its own accord outside the bank's countdown, builds the same opening at The Orchard every seed, and reads the Kneadmaster's worth only by its own books. Numbers from it are a floor on what a good player finds, not a ceiling ([balance-harness.md](balance-harness.md)).
