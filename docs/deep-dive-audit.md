# Deep-dive audit, 20 October 2026

A second pass over main at `5bc5302` (0.30.14 with the payday and bankruptcy rules in), a day after [gameplay-audit.md](gameplay-audit.md). Five reviewers read the code by subsystem (guests and treatment, staff, events and progression, building and saves, the interface), each writing throwaway probes that were run and deleted; the playtest bot played every center on three seeds for four years (`yarn balance`); and three experiments were run on top of the bot to test what the numbers suggested. Typecheck and all 1,364 unit tests passed before anything was touched. Nothing was played by hand, so the balance findings are the bot's, and the bugs are confirmed by probe or trace as each one says.

The aim it is measured against: the campaign should feel about as hard as a Two Point Hospital campaign. Easy to get going, demand that outruns the player, money rarely the worry early on, a reputation that moves slowly, and pressures that arrive one at a time.

Everything already listed in [gameplay-audit.md](gameplay-audit.md) and [known-issues.md](known-issues.md) is left out here unless this pass found something new about it.

## The short version

1. **The Orchard Estate is a guaranteed loss under the new payday rule.** The bot went bankrupt on day 93 in every seed. With bankruptcy switched off, the very same games earned all three stars by month 17 to 20 and never went below about −$11,000. The loan ceiling ($50,000) sits almost exactly on the cost of opening the campaign's last center, so the payday rule turned the easiest center into one a reasonable opening cannot survive.
2. **The Boardwalk collapses every summer.** Summer arrivals at 1.9× flood a building that cannot grow, waits run to 25 days, guests get worse by the handful, and reputation falls from about 100 to 0 in three months, every year, in every seed. Flattening the seasons to 1.4× and 0.8× gave two of three seeds the third star by month 16 to 18 at about $37,000 a month, against month 34 or never at $0 to $5,000.
3. **Two goals hold stars for a year or more:** "guests keep 93% of their patience" (The Boardwalk and High Pines second stars) and "keep 90%" (The Exchange first star). They ask the player to run well under capacity for a whole month, which is the opposite of the game's own growth loop, and at The Exchange the third star's goals are all met before the second's, so the two land in the same day.
4. **Most of a settled center's money is not from healing.** At Willow Lane in its second year, Qi Gong class fees ($8,000 to $24,000 a month) and house calls ($3,000 to $12,000) together outweigh treatment fees ($5,000 to $23,000), and emergency and challenge bonuses add $11,000 to $29,000 in the months they land. Prices at 1.4× cost almost nothing. A good player's income scales with the price slider, staff rank and the number of side offers, not with how many guests the building can treat; that is why four of five centers end with about $1,000,000 and nothing to spend it on.
5. **Reputation is still a knife-edge,** as the 15 October audit found and recommended changing (failure −2.5, walk-out −3, worse off −6, against a tapered gain of about +2 to +2.8 a heal; Two Point's are +1, −1, −0.5, −0.25). Nothing from that recommendation is in. It is the mechanism behind 1, 2 and 3.
6. **A room's prestige is free with size.** Prestige grows 0.4 a tile and a room costs the same at any size, so a bare 6×6 Massage Suite is prestige 4 and a 7×7 is prestige 5 for the price of a 3×3 at prestige 1. The Orchard's second-star prestige goal, demand pull, standing points and the success bonus all come with dragging the room bigger at the start.
7. **Twenty-odd bugs,** none a crash, several worth fixing before a release: a save upgrade that marks every old month as "guests kept 0% of their patience", two dialogs in one frame losing the first (a failed inspection's fine-or-bribe choice included), star karma that cannot be spent until the next autosave, a house-call fee that locks in whatever the price slider shows at the instant of acceptance, a loaded "Previous game" that exists only in memory until its first autosave, and The Exchange's rush-hour impatience applying all day.

### The Orchard Estate: bankrupt in every game

The bot opens the Orchard with every discipline the blurb offers ("Acupuncture, reiki, sound healing and a Shaman are open from the start"): 9 or 10 rooms and 7 or 8 staff in the first month, $94,000 spent from the $45,000 start plus $40,000 borrowed. The payroll is then $12,500 to $16,500 a month against takings of $9,000 in month one and $26,000 in month three, with 10 or 11 guests a month arriving (reputation 30 to 44). It borrows the rest at the second payday, and at the third (day 90) the loan is at its $50,000 limit with $12,000 in the bank against $13,800 in wages: three days' grace, then bankrupt.

Three experiments with the same bot and seeds:

| Opening                                                               | Seed 1                                | Seed 2                               | Seed 3                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| As the bot plays (every discipline on day 0)                          | bankrupt day 93                       | bankrupt day 93                      | bankrupt day 93                       |
| Lean: acupuncture only, sound at month 3, reiki at 6, the Shaman at 9 | ★ at 6.6, 11.9, 18.8; lowest −$839    | ★1 at 5.7, then bankrupt month 8     | bankrupt month 5                      |
| As the bot plays, bankruptcy switched off                             | ★ at 5.5, 12.0, 19.5; lowest −$11,172 | ★ at 5.6, 11.0, 17.2; lowest −$6,589 | ★ at 4.4, 10.8, 17.2; lowest −$10,717 |

So the center is sound once it is through its first half-year: with bankruptcy off, every seed earns every star in 17 to 20 months and is back in the black by month 11 or 12 with an overdraft that never passed $11,200. What kills it is a cushion of exactly $50,000 against an opening that costs about $100,000 and a payroll of $15,000 to $30,000, with the loan limit the same at a $20,000 center and a $45,000 one. Even the lean opening dies in two seeds of three, because the bot keeps hiring to 8 or 9 staff by month 4 while reputation 30 brings 10 guests a month whatever is built.

Two Point's equivalent: a warning at −$150,000 and the end at −$300,000, against rooms of $5,000 to $8,000 and a first-hospital start of $200,000. The cushion there is 20 to 40 rooms' worth; here it is 6 to 10 rooms' worth, and 3 days.

### What this says against Two Point

- **Demand is flat.** A guest arrives every `30 − 0.2 × reputation` seconds, times the center's twist, 8% a grade, 5% a staffed discipline and the line's put-off: about 9 a month at 50% reputation, 14 at 100%. The Exchange saw 7 to 10 guests a month for its first eleven months at reputation 32 to 71. Nothing the player builds raises it in any way that matters, and the 15 October audit's finding 3 (demand should grow with the center) was taken only as far as +5% a discipline. In Two Point more rooms mean more patients; here more rooms mean the same guests and a bigger payroll, which is why the Orchard dies and why the rational opening everywhere is the smallest one.
- **Reputation is fast and asymmetric.** Gains taper (`1.15 − rep/120`), losses do not; a heal is worth about +2.8 at low reputation and +1.3 at 80%, while a failure is −2.5, a walk-out −3 and a guest who got worse −6. A center settles at about 92% reputation on a 70% cure rate, 67% on 60%, 31% on 50%, and falls to zero below that: the knife-edge the audit described, still as it was. Every spiral above is this rule plus a line.
- **Waits are far longer than Two Point's.** Averages of 8 to 21 days in settled centers, with 20 to 60 guests in a building meant for 14 plus 5 a room, because treatment capacity is one guest per practitioner per 24-odd seconds and the practitioners are often out on calls. Two Point's queues are long but patients leave for −0.25; here they get worse for −6.
- **Money is easy late and lethal early.** Four centers end with $0.6M to $1.4M; the Orchard cannot get through month three. The late money is side income that scales with the slider; the early danger is a cushion sized for a $20,000 center applied to a $45,000 one.
- **The goal set is the wrong shape in three places:** the patience goals, the Exchange's ★2 that is met after its ★3, and "heal 15 with reiki" at two centers that are full before reiki opens (already logged).

## What to change, in order

1. **Give the money a floor the game can be played against,** not a payday that ends it. Either a loan limit that scales with the center (its starting money, or its standing), an overdraft allowed down to two or three months of payroll before bankruptcy with the morale and quitting consequences in between, or both.
