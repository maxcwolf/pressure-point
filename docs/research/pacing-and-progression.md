# Pacing and progression in management and builder sim campaigns

Scope: how Theme Hospital, Two Point Hospital/Campus, RollerCoaster Tycoon, Planet Coaster/Zoo, Frostpunk, Oxygen Not Included, Factorio, Satisfactory, Overcooked, Diner Dash and Mini Metro/Motorways structure a campaign: objective shapes, ordering of pressures, first-hour design, walls and patches, and progress signalling.

Sourcing caveat up front: the fan wikis that list objectives verbatim (two-point-hospital.fandom.com, rct.fandom.com, frostpunk.fandom.com, planetcoaster.fandom.com) returned HTTP 402 to the fetch tool, and strategywiki.org returned 403. Where objectives are quoted below they come from forum posts, guide sites, search snippets of those wikis, or the CorsixTH level format, and each is marked with its actual source. Numbers from forum posts should be treated as player-reported, not official.

## Key question 1: What objective shapes do well-liked management campaigns use, and which shapes draw complaints?

### Takeaway

The durable, well-liked shapes are plain counts and thresholds the player can watch tick up (cure N patients, N guests with park rating >= 600 by a date, deliver N parts to the Space Elevator), usually bundled three or four at a time with one "soft" quality gate (reputation, park rating, happiness). The shapes that draw complaints are composite or derived metrics the player cannot see the formula for (hospital value, park value, campus average grade, happiness/morale percentages) and goals that depend on something not yet researched or on a scripted drop in a hidden stat.

### Cited Findings

Theme Hospital (1997) and CorsixTH

- Each level sets "set goals in the fields of financial attainment, patients cured, percent of patients cured, and hospital value"; "Holding negative funds or allowing sufficient patients to die will bring about losing requirements"; clearing a level offers "a new, more elaborate hospital with tougher winning conditions and more diseases present" — [Wikipedia: Theme Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital)
- The CorsixTH level format (which reimplements the original level data) defines exactly six win/lose criteria types: "Total reputation", "Balance total", "Percentage of people your hospital has handled", "Percentage of people who have been cured", "Percentage of people who have been killed", "Hospital value". Each `win_criteria[n]` / `lose_criteria[n]` entry is a tuple `Criteria.MaxMin.Value.Group.Bound`. The same file schedules emergencies by month window with a required success percentage: e.g. `StartMonth 4, EndMonth 5, Min 4, Max 6, Illness 16, PercWin 75, Bonus 400` and a second window at months 8-10. Staff availability is also gated by month (entries at months 0, 3 and 5 with rising counts) — [CorsixTH example.level](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/master/CorsixTH/Levels/example.level)
- Level 1 "Toxicity": StrategyWiki says "it's an easy one - you've simply got to cure ten patients", and its targets table (headers not captured by the search snippet) lists the values 300, $1,000, 40, 10 and $55,000. The most plausible mapping is reputation 300, balance $1,000, cure rate 40%, 10 cures, hospital value $55,000, but that mapping is unverified — [StrategyWiki: Theme Hospital/Toxicity](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Toxicity)
- A fan strategy guide describes Hospital Value as "all of your material wealth" which "can only be boosted by buying new objects", and advises that if you need cash instead, "selling rooms could help you finish a level sooner" — i.e. the two money goals pull against each other — [Force For Good: Theme Hospital](https://forceforgood.co.uk/strategy/theme-hospital/)

Two Point Hospital (2018)

- Campaign structure: "the achievement of a one-star rating allows players to progress to the next hospital, though they may continue building up their current hospital by completing additional objectives to increase the rating to two or three stars" — [Wikipedia: Two Point Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Point_Hospital)
- Flemington (6th hospital), star goals as posted by a player citing the fan wiki: 1 star = hospital value $1,000,000, reputation 70%, cure 10 patients with Animal Magnetism, launch 1 marketing campaign; 2 stars = $2,000,000, 80%, cure 20 patients in the Fracture Ward, 2 campaigns; 3 stars = $5,000,000, 90%, cure 30 patients in the Injection Room, 3 campaigns. In the same thread one player's hospital value "peaked around $500k, then fell below $200k" and they could not get past 1 star; another went bankrupt on the map; another found only the $5M value goal hard. Players advise researching Animal Magnetism and the Injection Room on earlier maps, i.e. the cure-count goals depend on research that may not be done yet — [Steam: Flemington thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/1737715419909396845)
- Grockle Bay 3-star goals as reported by a player: hospital value 4,000 (almost certainly $4,000,000 in the game's shorthand; unverified), 80% attractiveness, 90% reputation. The poster met attractiveness and reputation but "kept losing money, and the hospital value goal kept slipping", going bankrupt repeatedly even after saving large sums. Replies explain hospital value "depends on rooms, consecutive profitable months, and cash on hand, so staying in the red drags it down", that bankruptcy only triggers at -$300,000, and that a VIP (Agatha Sphere) "drains money for several months" — [Steam: Grockle Bay 3 stars](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/3104564981114190944)
- The "90% staff morale target on Duckworth" is called "very hard"; one player raised pay heavily right before sending staff on break to spike morale past the threshold. The same thread records that star rewards "shifted from useful items to Kudosh" after a few levels, so the incentive to 3-star fades — [Steam: Go for 1* or 3* Levels?](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/2590022385665527144)
- A player on a later hospital said the hardest part "was learning 2 new techs and buying them, and that researching them at an earlier hospital halved the challenge" (search snippet of a Two Point Hospital Steam discussion; the exact thread was not fetched) — [Steam: Two Point Hospital discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/)
- Mitton University (R.E.M.I.X. version) uses a plot-count objective: "a star challenge of owning 8 plots, and you will only ever have objectives for 2 plots at a time, and the order may change depending on how you go about it"; example sub-objectives include "generating 450 research points" and "training 5 staff" (search snippet attributed to a TrueAchievements guide) — [TrueAchievements: World 2](https://www.trueachievements.com/a322709/world-2-achievement)
- Beginner "Career Goals" are hospital-independent meta-goals such as "Get 3 stars in Tumble", "Get 3 stars in Mitton University", "Get 3 stars in Flemington" and "Get 15 stars" — [MagicGameWorld: Career Goals](https://www.magicgameworld.com/?p=23540)

Two Point Campus (2022)

- Freshleigh Meadows 1-star goals include a class passed with a C grade and an "Average Happiness Rating of 60%" (search snippet) — [Prima Games: Freshleigh Meadows](https://vip-develop.primagames.com/?p=63743); a 2-star goal on a later campus is an "Average A grade on campus" (search snippet) — [TrueAchievements: Two Point Campus walkthrough, Upper Etching](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Two-Point-Campus/walkthrough/9)
- A reviewer needed an average grade of B+ to clear a level and found it "much harder to achieve" because of opaque student behaviour: students "felt bored despite weekly events and entertainment items, and felt unhygienic despite showers in every building"; two dropped out and the average rose "due to attrition". The review says a "lack of information" about why things fail "adds to frustration", and that 2- and 3-star tiers "escalated further", with some later levels changing how the campus earns money — [Jump Dash Roll: Two Point Campus review](https://jumpdashroll.com/article/two-point-campus-review)

RollerCoaster Tycoon (1999) and RCT2

- "With the exception of the final scenario, players must achieve a goal, usually a specific number of guests or park value, by a predetermined in-game date"; the end-of-scenario score is company value = park value minus loan. Example goals (search snippets): Forest Frontiers "250 guests and a 600 park rating by October 31, Year 1"; Thunder Rock "900 guests and a 600 park rating by October, Year 4"; Mega Park "has no objective" — [Giant Bomb: RollerCoaster Tycoon](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-2236/)
- RCT2 variants: Crazy Castle "1,500 guests, 600 park rating by October 31, Year 4"; Infernal Views "Build 10 different roller coasters with an excitement rating of 6.00"; Extreme Heights "4,500 guests, park rating must be kept over 700" (an ongoing floor rather than a deadline snapshot) — [Giant Bomb: RollerCoaster Tycoon 2](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-11178/)
- Corkscrew Follies adds park-value-by-date goals (Whispering Cliffs "$17,000 park value by October 31, Year 3") — [Giant Bomb: Corkscrew Follies](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-11300/); Loopy Landscapes includes a monthly ride-ticket income goal, Wacky Warren "$9,000 monthly income from ride tickets" — [Giant Bomb: Loopy Landscapes](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-14131/)
- A long-form retrospective describes guest counts (usually paired with a park rating) as the most common goal; park value as a "moving target" based on hypothetical ride income adjusted for ride popularity, where raising ticket prices does not count; and notes park value "doesn't appear in financial records", making it hard to track. Dinky Park's goal is "Double Park Value to $10,000"; Katie's World is "Double the park's value" — [Shamus Young: RollerCoaster Tycoon's Second Gear](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=57879)
- RCT3 pairs park value with debt: Gravity Gardens "$500,000 park value, repay your loan", and Alpine Adventures "$7,000 monthly income from rides" in the Expert tier (search snippets of RCT3 scenario lists; not independently verified) — [RCT Wiki: Goldrush!](https://rct.fandom.com/wiki/Goldrush!)

Planet Coaster (2016) and Planet Zoo (2019)

- Planet Coaster career: "finishing tasks such as constructing unfinished roller coasters or hiring janitors", plus "achieving park ratings" — [Wikipedia: Planet Coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_Coaster); the career premise is "a park that is being built, but has run into financial trouble" — [Coasterpedia: Planet Coaster](https://coasterpedia.net/wiki/Planet_Coaster)
- Planet Coaster 2 career: a player reported a scenario objective requiring "a coaster with a fall of at least 45 meters, which was unclear" — [Steam: Planet Coaster 2 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2688950/discussions/0/604142859631733790/); a preview notes a water-slide scenario where "a pool needs a walkway from the park entrance to the swimming area" was the non-obvious blocker — [Shacknews: Planet Coaster 2 career preview](https://www.shacknews.com/article/141391/planet-coaster-2-preview-pc)
- Planet Zoo career levels are scored Bronze/Silver/Gold; the "Hiring Freeze" scenario's Bronze goals are staff happiness, a yearly profit target, and adopting rescue animals — [Planet Zoo Wiki: Hiring Freeze](https://planetzoo.fandom.com/wiki/Hiring_Freeze). Planet Zoo 2 requirements "include a range of things, including perfecting animal welfare, attracting a set number of guests, completing research, reaching financial milestones" — [wikily.gg: Planet Zoo 2 career](https://wikily.gg/planet-zoo-2/career); a guide advises aiming only for Bronze first because "you can always circle back" and "you can leave after Bronze or Silver and come back for Gold once you have better tools and animals from higher Accreditation" — [PowerUp Gaming: Planet Zoo 2 career guide](https://powerupgaming.co.uk/2026/10/14/planet-zoo-2-career-mode-guide-tips-for-gold/)

Frostpunk (2018)

- The base scenario's crisis ("Winterhome event") is a scripted Hope drop: "Winterhome drops hope as a scripted event, separate from discontent". The original poster kept "homelessness, sickness, and hunger at zero" with "near-max hope", then got "a two-day ultimatum to raise hope" and lost after three hours; a replier suggested hope should "scale with the total bar". Most replies say it is manageable via Order/Faith laws, but a few agree it can feel harsh — [Steam: Winterhome Event Too Severe](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1696045708646208587)

Factorio and Satisfactory (milestone shapes)

- Factorio's redesigned new player experience aimed to "Give goals through the technology tree, labs, and the technology GUI early on" and "Make every recipe unlock through a technology, so research drives progression", with objectives that "have clear purpose, tied to progression rather than handed out as chores" — [Factorio FFF-241](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-241)
- Satisfactory has 10 tiers and 48 milestones; Tier 0's six HUB Upgrades must be completed in sequence; Tiers 1-2 unlock when Tier 0 completes (or Onboarding is skipped); Tiers 3-4 need Space Elevator Phase 1, 5-6 Phase 2, 7-8 Phase 3, Tier 9 Phase 4. Each milestone lists required materials; items inserted cannot be taken back — [Satisfactory Wiki: Tiers](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Tiers)

Overcooked and Diner Dash (score thresholds)

- Overcooked's postmortem says balancing level scoring was one of the hardest tasks, "with different score boundaries for 1 through 4 players across all 30+ levels" (search snippet) — [Game Developer: Road to the IGF, Overcooked](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-ghost-town-games-i-overcooked-i-)
- Diner Dash: "a level's goal is to earn a set number of points" with "expert point totals for advanced players"; the original sets "a new revenue goal for each level", with failing the target or losing customers costing a star — [GameSpot: Diner Dash review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/diner-dash-review/1900-6171988/); [Macworld: Diner Dash 2](https://www.macworld.com/article/176212/dinerdash-2.html)

Mini Metro / Mini Motorways (no explicit objectives)

- Mini Metro starts with "only three subway stations to manage at first, and the city expands rapidly, with new stations appearing in random locations" — [Wikipedia: Mini Metro](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Metro_(video_game)>); the studio describes "procedural levels, brief play sessions, and segmented content organized by city" designed "to be played repeatedly" — [Game Developer: Mini Metro postmortem](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/postmortem-dinosaur-polo-club-s-i-mini-metro-i-)

### Inferences

- The complaint cluster is consistent across 25 years: Theme Hospital's hospital value, RCT's park value, Two Point Hospital's hospital value and staff morale %, Two Point Campus's average grade, Frostpunk's Hope. All are derived metrics with hidden or multi-factor formulas, and all are the goal players report as "slipping" while the plain counts (cures, guests, campaigns launched) are met.
- "Money goals that punish growth" shows up specifically as hospital/park value: expansion costs cash now, value counts rooms and consecutive profitable months later, and loans and VIP drains push the player into the red while they build. The Grockle Bay and Flemington threads are both this pattern.
- "Needs a thing not yet unlocked" is a real Two Point Hospital pattern (cure counts for a specific treatment room whose research is still pending); players route around it by researching on earlier maps, which is a sign the campaign does not surface the dependency.
- Count-with-quality-floor goals (RCT guests + park rating 600, Satisfactory parts) are liked because every unit of work visibly moves the number and the quality floor is a single stat with its own visible meter.

### Gaps

- Verbatim per-hospital star lists for Two Point Hospital and per-level tables for Theme Hospital could not be fetched (fan wiki 402, StrategyWiki 403); only Flemington, Grockle Bay (3-star), Toxicity (unlabelled numbers) and fragments of Freshleigh Meadows/Mitton are quoted, and these are forum/guide sourced.
- No source gave Planet Coaster 1 career objectives verbatim, nor Planet Zoo Silver/Gold goals for a named scenario.
- No designer statement was found explaining why hospital/park value was chosen as a goal shape, or acknowledging it as a pain point.

## Key question 2: How do they order pressures across a campaign, and how is the first hour designed?

### Takeaway

The pattern across the genre is: first level is a single plain count with no disasters (Theme Hospital "cure ten patients", RCT Forest Frontiers 250 guests, Satisfactory's forced-order Tier 0, Factorio's three "First Steps" levels), with event pressures scheduled by level data in later maps and one new hook per subsequent level (Two Point), while the RCT-style structure escalates by changing the park's constraints (space, terrain, pre-existing problems, hidden money limits) rather than by adding event systems.

### Cited Findings

- Theme Hospital's first level: "it's an easy one - you've simply got to cure ten patients" — [StrategyWiki: Toxicity](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Toxicity). Emergencies in the CorsixTH level format are scheduled per month window (first window months 4-5, second months 8-10 in the example), and staff tiers unlock at months 0, 3 and 5, so event pressure and hiring options are explicitly time-gated within a level — [CorsixTH example.level](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/master/CorsixTH/Levels/example.level). Epidemic/inspector pressure is attached to reputation: "Failing to hide an epidemic from a health inspector results in a fine and a damaged reputation, which affects the flow of patients" (search snippet) — [Force For Good: Theme Hospital](https://forceforgood.co.uk/strategy/theme-hospital/)
- Two Point Campus: "Each campus has its own unique hook and objectives to obtain a star"; one campus is a sports (cheeseball) rival-league hook, another a wizard-school hook with "life munchers" and "weather such as meteor storms". "Early levels were easy, but objectives became harder as campuses grew and students got pickier"; twelve levels at launch — [Jump Dash Roll: Two Point Campus review](https://jumpdashroll.com/article/two-point-campus-review)
- Two Point Hospital's Grockle Bay thread shows the VIP visit (Agatha Sphere) and emergencies layered onto the money objective at that point in the campaign — [Steam: Grockle Bay](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/3104564981114190944)
- RCT escalation: scenarios "escalate through four restriction types: less space, harder terrain, pre-existing park problems, and unusual limits", with money limits "the one restriction usually not stated up front"; restricted scenarios "begin after Evergreen Gardens (#5)"; "early progress is often slow, since guest numbers must grow before a park can fund expansion"; Ivory Towers (#19) is "a well-established park with problems" that starts with cleanup. A reader comment says RCT1's difficulty curve is smoother than RCT2's — [Shamus Young: RollerCoaster Tycoon's Second Gear](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=57879). Forest Frontiers' deadline is the end of Year 1 while Thunder Rock gives four years, so deadlines stretch along with targets — [Giant Bomb: RollerCoaster Tycoon](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-2236/)
- Frostpunk A New Home pacing (critic analysis): "the Londoners, refugee crisis, and final storm are paced so tightly that players just want to survive the current crisis" and "each task is harder than the previous one, fitting the curve of the cognitive flow" — [indienova lab: Critical play report, Frostpunk](https://lab.indienova.com/indie-game-review/critical-play-report-frostpunk/). Lead designer Jakub Stokalski: the concept "puts all these different pieces together and against each other" and "it's really difficult from a design standpoint to get into a smooth good gameplay experience" — [TechRaptor: How 11 bit studios created Frostpunk](https://techraptor.net/content/how-11-bit-studios-created-society-survival-simulator-frostpunk)
- Frostpunk The Fall of Winterhome inverts the order: it "starts with a fully formed city, not a new settlement"; "Unlike the base game, where one or two events occur at a time, Winterhome throws many at once: hunger, sickness, children injured in the riot aftermath, and homes without heat"; weather is "less punishing than in the base game" and the main strain is population size. Reviewer verdict: "doomed from the start" yet "forgiving enough to feel like it can be overcome" — [COGconnected: The Fall of Winterhome impressions](https://cogconnected.com/2018/09/frostpunk-fall-winterhome-impressions/). The 1.2.0 release notes made scenario unlocks easier: "all scenarios are unlocked after playing the first scenario for 20 game days" — [GOG DB: Frostpunk release notes](https://www.gogdb.org/product/1648559910/releasenotes); update 1.1.1 added an Extreme difficulty so "every scenario can be played on the hardest difficulty without the need to turn on the Survivor Mode" — [Frostpunk Update 1.1.1](https://www.frostpunkgame.com/?p=5973)
- Factorio's first hour: the old demo "Gives the impression of being a Minecraft clone" because "Assemblers and electricity aren't introduced in the first two levels"; constraints were "so tight that players learn to solve the tutorial rather than the underlying concepts"; levels "feel disconnected" because recipes, structures and location change between them; the redesign goal is to reach automation "after 30-45 minutes", "Introduce core concepts like assemblers and electricity in the first level, with as little complexity as possible", "Start each new level where the previous one ended", and "Teach through experimentation" — [Factorio FFF-241](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-241). The replacement NPE was later removed in favour of the three-level "First Steps" campaign; FFF-342 notes "each new concept costs the player attention" (on teaching quickbar links early) and that internal testing "found many corner cases where players could get stuck" — [Factorio FFF-342](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-342)
- Satisfactory's first hour is Tier 0: six HUB Upgrades "must be completed in sequence", each unlocking one small set (Upgrade 1: Equipment Workshop, Portable Miner; Upgrade 2: Smelter, Power Line; Upgrade 3: Constructor, Power Pole; Upgrade 4: Conveyor Pole and Belt; Upgrade 5: Miner Mk.1, Storage Container; Upgrade 6: Space Elevator); after that, "Within an open tier, you can complete milestones in any order" — [Satisfactory Wiki: Tiers](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Tiers)
- Overcooked: "Levels in Overcooked always start with the 'story'"; "Normally we start with a mechanic" and look for several ways to use it, dropping it if it cannot support multiple player stories; each level "challenge[s] the players in a way that will require them to co-operate" differently; recipes are kept "simple enough and the process streamlined enough" so the level's obstacle is the challenge; early prototypes "allowed too many errors" so rules were tightened to keep players focused "on trying to co-ordinate their actions"; controls were kept simple so they "were never a barrier to entry" — [Push Square: Ghost Town Games interview](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2018/08/interview_chewing_the_fat_with_overcooked_2_developer_ghost_town_games). Overcooked 2 added "dynamic levels where recipes change halfway through" — [Red Bull: Overcooked 2 interview](https://www.redbull.com/int-en/overcooked-2-ghost-town-games-interview). The original postmortem says "Adding a mechanic or changing a rule occasionally required a level to be completely redesigned or scrapped entirely" (search snippet) — [Game Developer: Road to the IGF, Overcooked](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-ghost-town-games-i-overcooked-i-)
- Diner Dash: "new types of diners are also introduced at a fairly steady pace, and each has its own particular behaviors to consider" and "regardless of goal, the overall flow of gameplay remains the same"; about six restaurants and roughly 70 levels (search snippets) — [Pocket Gamer: Diner Dash review](https://www.pocketgamer.com/diner-dash-2015/review/); [GameSpot: Diner Dash review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/diner-dash-review/1900-6171988/)
- Mini Metro / Mini Motorways: a "calm-to-frantic arc" where decisions grow more complicated as the network grows; players "don't control spawns, passenger types, or station placement, so they make a few impactful decisions while the simulation runs"; players can "delete their network and start over with the current puzzle at any point"; a prototype that "constantly spawned roundabouts worked but didn't improve the experience" and a real-map prototype was scrapped because it "didn't evolve smoothly"; finding the core "toy" took about four months — [Thumbsticks: How Mini Motorways built its Wuselfaktor](https://www.thumbsticks.com/hustle-and-bustle-how-mini-motorways-built-its-wuselfaktor/). Peter Curry: the passenger and train AI is "far and away the most complex thing in Mini Metro" and "the cause of most of the feedback we get" — [Digitally Downloaded: Peter Curry](https://digitallydownloaded.net/?p=44565)
- Oxygen Not Included has no campaign and leans on tooltips and the Database; players ask for "a tutorial to guide the player around tasks, objects and GUI", others defend trial-and-error as part of a sandbox design — [Steam: ONI tutorial thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/457140/discussions/2/2944710017709193804); a second thread criticises pointing players to third-party YouTube guides — [Steam: ONI discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/457140/discussions/0/4299319979686414707); a review frames the first failing playthrough as the point — [Vice: Oxygen Not Included review](https://vice.com/en/article/7xgenb/oxygen-not-included-klei-darkly-comic-fight-for-survival-review)

### Inferences

- Two distinct escalation models exist. "Add a system per level" (Theme Hospital/Two Point: epidemics, VIPs, inspectors, weather, rivals appear as per-map hooks) and "change the constraints, keep the systems" (RCT: space, terrain, inherited mess, money). The second produces fewer "I didn't know that mechanic existed" complaints but more "hidden limit" complaints.
- Factorio's two reversals (demo -> NPE -> First Steps) are the clearest documented case of a studio deciding that a tutorial which front-loads too many mid-game systems is worse than three short levels each adding a little, and that each level should start where the last ended so the player's build is not thrown away.
- Satisfactory's "forced order for Tier 0, free order after" is a clean statement of the onboarding rule: lock the order only while the player cannot yet judge priorities.
- Frostpunk's Winterhome DLC shows that "many pressures at once" is acceptable to players when it is framed as the premise (a doomed city) rather than sprung mid-run; the base-game Hope drop drew the "arbitrary" complaint precisely because it arrived after the player had every visible stat under control.

### Gaps

- No primary source listing which hospital in Two Point Hospital introduces epidemics, earthquakes, VIPs, inspectors or weather first (the wiki's hospital list was unreachable).
- No day-by-day A New Home timeline (fandom 402); the ordering Londoners -> refugees -> storm is from a critic's analysis, not 11 bit.
- No GDC/Develop talk by Two Point Studios, Frontier or 11 bit on campaign pacing was surfaced by search; the Mini Metro GDC 2017 talk ("When Less Is More") is UI-focused per its listing — [Game Developer: GDC 2017 Mini Metro](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/attend-gdc-2017-and-learn-the-value-of-less-is-more-from-a-i-mini-metro-i-dev).
- Typical level durations in hours were not stated by any source for any game.

## Key question 3: What do patches, reviews and player threads say about objectives that were walls, and what was changed?

### Takeaway

Documented post-launch changes are mostly about access and difficulty settings (Frostpunk unlocking all scenarios after 20 days, adding Extreme difficulty; Factorio replacing its tutorial twice) rather than rewrites of a specific objective; the walls players name are almost always a derived-metric goal (hospital value, staff morale %, average grade, Hope) or a dependency the level did not surface, and the community's fix is to carry research or savings forward from earlier levels.

### Cited Findings

- Two Point Hospital walls named by players: Flemington's $5M hospital value and research-gated cure counts — [Steam: Flemington](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/1737715419909396845); Grockle Bay's hospital value with repeated bankruptcy — [Steam: Grockle Bay](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/3104564981114190944); Duckworth's 90% staff morale — [Steam: 1* or 3*](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/2590022385665527144); Roquefort Castle "losing money suddenly while near the three-star targets" and Croquembouche described by a guide as "easily the most challenging Hospital ... as it is the last Hospital" (search snippets) — [Steam: Two Point Hospital discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/); [SegmentNext: Two Point Studios guides](https://segmentnext.com/company/two-point-studios/)
- Two Point Hospital bug-type wall: a 2022 thread reports a stuck objective on Camouflage Falls; a developer replied that "if you restart the level I would expect that you shouldn't have this issue again" (search snippet; workaround, not a patch) — [Steam: Two Point Hospital discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/1742220359693736563)
- Two Point Campus: a reviewer could not tell why students were not reaching facilities, two dropped out, and the B+ average goal was reached "due to attrition"; "lack of information" is the stated cause of frustration — [Jump Dash Roll review](https://jumpdashroll.com/article/two-point-campus-review). Guides respond by pointing to the hover breakdown of "at least seven factors" on grade objectives, and warn that "the issue is more widespread among the student body than one struggling student" — [PCGamesN: Two Point Campus grades](https://www.pcgamesn.com/two-point-campus/grades); another guide: "hosting events will also make students happier, increasing their overall performance" — [Prima Games: Mitton University 3-star](https://primagames.com/tips/two-point-campus-mitton-university-3-star-level-guide)
- Frostpunk: the Winterhome Hope drop thread ("losing after three hours of good play feels arbitrary"; "two-day ultimatum to raise hope") — [Steam: Winterhome Event Too Severe](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1696045708646208587). ### Inferences

- Community-generated workarounds (pre-research on earlier maps, hoarding $500k before expanding, raising pay right before a break to spike morale, relying on dropouts to lift an average) are all evidence of goals whose formula is hidden; each workaround games the metric rather than improving the hospital/campus, which is the signature of an opaque objective.
- The studios that did change things changed access (unlock everything sooner, add difficulty tiers, let players skip onboarding) rather than objective text. There is no found example of a tycoon studio rewriting a specific star goal after launch, which suggests objectives are treated as content and tuned through difficulty settings instead.

## Key question 4: How do these games communicate objective progress, near-completion and stalls?

### Takeaway

The standard is a per-criterion bar with a hover tooltip giving "have / need" (Theme Hospital's status screen, Two Point's objectives tab, Two Point Campus's hover breakdown of contributing factors), plus advisor/message nudges for stalls; the best-documented reasoning on channel discipline is Factorio's (one purpose per channel, pausing bubbles only for must-know facts, the objective panel sufficient on its own), while Mini Metro replaces progress readouts entirely with a visible overcrowding countdown and a score.

### Cited Findings

- Theme Hospital: "Look at the Status Screen and keep a close eye on the win criteria bars. If you put your mouse pointer over the bars, tool tips will appear telling you how much you have and how much you need to get 100 percent in that criteria" — [Force For Good: Theme Hospital](https://forceforgood.co.uk/strategy/theme-hospital/)
- Two Point Campus: hovering a grade objective "reveals at least seven factors that will affect your students' average grades"; the objectives tab and each course's overview screen show room requirements and difficulty — [PCGamesN: grades](https://www.pcgamesn.com/two-point-campus/grades). Counter-evidence: student pathing is "only shown when placing a student project item, with no real-time way to see what's blocking students" — [Jump Dash Roll review](https://jumpdashroll.com/article/two-point-campus-review)
- Two Point Hospital: the Mitton University R.E.M.I.X. plot objective shows "objectives for 2 plots at a time", i.e. the panel surfaces only the next sub-goals of an 8-plot target (search snippet) — [TrueAchievements: World 2](https://www.trueachievements.com/a322709/world-2-achievement)
- RollerCoaster Tycoon: "Win conditions appear in scenario descriptions"; feedback comes "through in-game messages, such as blocked-construction notices and lost-guest alerts"; "Monthly awards from the in-game magazine signal success"; park value is not shown in financial records — [Shamus Young](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=57879)
- Factorio channel design: three channels, each with one purpose: the console for story text with a speaker prefix; yellow speech bubbles that "pause the game and are reserved for cases where the player must be told something" (many cut, more to be cut); and the objective panel whose text "should speak directly to the player", such that "A player who skips the bubbles and story text should still be able to finish the level". Guiding rule for the monologue channel: "nothing that is actually necessary should come via this channel" — [Factorio FFF-342](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-342). The 2018 diagnosis: "Too many messages add noise" and "Information is scattered across the objective window, character chat messages, and yellow TAB bubbles" — [Factorio FFF-241](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-241)
- Satisfactory: each milestone lists its required materials; completion is a physical act of transferring items at the HUB Terminal; inserted items cannot be retrieved — [Satisfactory Wiki: Tiers](https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Tiers)
- Frostpunk: the Fall of Winterhome added "a new resource and citizen management tab for making it easier to track income and keep people busy" — [COGconnected](https://cogconnected.com/2018/09/frostpunk-fall-winterhome-impressions/); the base game's Hope ultimatum is a two-day timer — [Steam: Winterhome Event](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1696045708646208587)
- Mini Metro/Motorways: the player's loss condition is not a target but overload, and the game offers restart of "the current puzzle at any point"; the design intent is a few impactful decisions against a running simulation — [Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/hustle-and-bustle-how-mini-motorways-built-its-wuselfaktor/)
- Overcooked's difficulty "comes much more from a team's ability to communicate" than from game experience, and levels are checked in 1- to 4-player combinations — [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2018/08/interview_chewing_the_fat_with_overcooked_2_developer_ghost_town_games)
- Planet Coaster 2: the blocker in a water-park scenario was an unstated adjacency rule (pool needs a walkway from the entrance) — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/141391/planet-coaster-2-preview-pc)

### Inferences

- The "have / need per criterion" bar is the genre's floor since 1997 and no well-liked game omits it; what separates readable goals from opaque ones is whether the bar's input is itself legible. Cures and guests are; hospital value, park value, average grade and Hope are not unless the game shows the factor breakdown the way Two Point Campus's hover does.
- No source described an explicit "almost there" cue (e.g. a toast at 90%) in any of these games; nudges are stall-oriented (lost guests, blocked construction, patients dying) rather than near-completion-oriented. Progress toward a sampled or averaged goal is therefore read by the player as a trend on a bar, which is why slipping values (Grockle Bay) feel like the goal is moving.
- Factorio's rule that the objective panel must be sufficient on its own, with pausing interruptions reserved for must-know facts, is the most concrete, designer-authored guidance found for objective communication.

### Gaps

- No screenshots or UI documentation were fetched for Two Point Hospital's objectives panel, RCT's objective window, or Planet Coaster's career objective panel; descriptions above are inferred from player and guide text.
- No source discussed how "average over a period" goals (e.g. average happiness/grade) are sampled or displayed mid-window in Two Point games, beyond the hover factor list.
- Mini Metro's weekly upgrade choice and the overcrowding timer were not described by any fetched developer source; only the general calm-to-frantic arc and restart affordance were.
