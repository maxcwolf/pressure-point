# How studios balance and test simulation and management games: bots, telemetry, directors, difficulty options

Scope note: these notes cover methods and case studies (RimWorld storytellers, Left 4 Dead's Director, Frostpunk / Oxygen Not Included / Two Point / Planet Coaster difficulty options, automated playtesting at King / Supercell / Riot / Google / academia, Slay the Spire and Hades data use, Celeste assist mode) and what each method would show for a game whose difficulty is "brittle" (spirals and cliffs) rather than uniformly hard. Vendor pitches are flagged inline. Where a primary source was paywalled or unreachable, that is stated in the Gaps.

## Key question 1: What metrics distinguish fair challenge from ease (completion rate, variance, time to goal, loss-cause distribution, attribution surveys)?

### Takeaway

No studio or paper found uses a single "fairness" metric; the practical consensus is to combine (a) an outcome rate (win/completion/score), (b) a comparison across player skill tiers or agent types (expert vs novice vs random) so that "hard" can be separated from "luck-driven", and (c) where and why losses happen (death location/cause, attempts distribution), with human attribution ("was that my fault?") captured qualitatively rather than by a standard metric. A game is "fair-hard" when skilled play reliably beats random play and losses cluster on readable causes; it is "unfair/brittle" when random and skilled agents converge, or when loss causes are dominated by a single untelegraphed system.

### Cited Findings

- Politowski et al. (2023) assess balance with RL agents along two axes: "Challenge vs. Success" (compare novice vs professional agents across versions to find difficulty spikes) and "Skill vs. Chance": if a random agent scores as well as or better than trained agents and humans, the version is judged luck-dependent; if the random agent scores much lower, the version is skill-based. In their Batkill test the random agent matched or beat trained agents on versions 3 and 4 (faster enemies, shorter cooldown), flagging those versions as luck-dominated, while in Jungle Climb the random agent scored far below humans and PPO in every version, so that game was judged skill-based. They used median score of two 180-second runs and did not measure variance across runs. — [arXiv 2304.08699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08699)
- The same paper found a designer expectation falsified by data: adding a second gap per platform in Jungle Climb (version 3) did not make the game easier as designers expected (human pro median 1908 to 1885; PPO 2251 to 2154). — [arXiv 2304.08699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08699)
- Google's Chimera case study defined imbalance as deviation from an intended win rate: the Evasion Link Gen deck won ~59.1% vs Damage-Heal 40.9% across 1M self-play games where designers intended parity; after tuning (evolve cost 3 to 1, T-Rex recovery doubled, higher starting health/healing, stronger biome penalties) the split became 49.8% / 50.2%, and average evolves per game rose from 1.54/0.86 to 2.16/1.76, which was itself a design goal (players evolving more often). — [Google Research blog](https://www.research.google/blog/leveraging-machine-learning-for-game-development/)
- Riot's Legends of Runeterra RL tool validated agent-derived balance metrics against two external references: they "consistently matched design intuition, correctly predicted the strongest deck, and showed directional congruence with live data"; RL "balance" scores became part of game designer KPIs. No quantitative agreement figures are given and the source is a vendor (Anyscale) summary of Riot's talk. — [Anyscale blog on Riot's talk](https://www.anyscale.com/blog/riot-games-and-deep-reinforcement-learning-in-gaming)
- Slay the Spire 2's open beta published aggregate outcome rates: 240M runs, 60.5M wins, 179.5M losses (25% win rate), with character pick shares between 17% and 24%; the developers attributed Ironclad's and Silent's slight edge to being available from the start (secondary report of the developers' post). — [GameGeeker report of Mega Crit's post](https://gamegeeker.com/fr/news/slay-the-spire-2-may-neows-news-update?market=FR)
- Mega Crit's GDC 2019 talk abstract: the team "took a metrics-driven approach early in development and kept using data-driven methods through early access", while using community feedback and keeping the game's feel and difficulty steady through a year of frequent updates. — [GDC Vault listing](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025731/-Slay-the-Spire-Metrics)
- A talk outline for the same session lists an internal Slack channel for playtester feedback, a metric server collecting data passively "to support more objective decisions", explicit warnings about "data deception" and "super playtester" bias, and the use of ascension levels to tune difficulty and target specific player groups. — [Podwise outline of the GDC talk](https://podwise.ai/episodes/1459444)
- Hochschule Trier's 2015 report proposes a "death-related problem level likelihood indicator" (DPLI) built from walkthrough data that quantifies both the magnitude and the density of character deaths to locate chokepoints; developed on the commercial game Angus Hates Aliens, it "correlated to enjoyment ratings for the game levels". — [Informatik-Bericht 2015-01](https://www.hochschule-trier.de/fileadmin/Hauptcampus/Fachbereich_Informatik/Forschung/Informatik_Berichte/2015/Informatik-Bericht-2015-01.pdf)
- Kristensen et al. model puzzle-level difficulty as "the distribution of actions a player performs within a level" (Lily's Garden data) rather than only the classic probability of success, arguing that completion rate "doesn't describe what the player does inside a level". — [arXiv 2107.03305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03305)
- Ian Schreiber's metrics lecture material (as summarised in a UBC course deck) stresses "where and how they lose a life" as a key difficulty metric and reports that Valve plots deaths overlaid on a level map to see clustering. — [UBC lecture deck based on Schreiber](https://cmps-people.ok.ubc.ca/bowenhui/game/lectures/6-metrics.pdf)
- Firaxis's Ed Beach called the share of Civilization VI players who finished games "depressingly" low, which Firaxis cited as the motivation for Civ VII's structural redesign (completion rate used as a design signal, not a balance number). — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/firaxis-big-swing-with-civilization-vii-convincing-players-to-actually-finish-their-games)
- Soren Johnson on early access data: "when the public gets the game, they find problems that you never did, and you must devote time to fixing them" and, on Offworld, within weeks of early access many strategies the team thought strong turned out weak because the community moved faster than the developers. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/design-success-means-knowing-what-to-do-with-feedback); [PC PowerPlay interview via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/pcpowerplay/20160401/281728383618831)
- Mike Booth (Valve) on the Director: "Algorithm adjusts pacing, not difficulty. Amplitude (difficulty) is not changed, frequency (pacing) is", and "Survivor Intensity estimation is crude, yet the resulting pacing works". — [The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead (Valve slides, PDF)](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)

### Inferences

- The "random agent baseline" from Politowski et al. is the cheapest brittleness detector available: for a management sim, run a policy that makes legal-but-thoughtless decisions and a competent scripted policy over the same seeds; if their survival curves converge on some seeds or in some phases, difficulty there is coming from the dice, not from the player's choices.
- Mean win rate (e.g., Slay the Spire 2's 25%) is a target, not a diagnosis; the studios that publish it pair it with per-character / per-ascension slices. For a brittle game, the equivalent slice is per-seed and per-phase: a 25% mean that is 0% on a third of seeds and 40% on the rest is a different design problem from a uniform 25%.
- Loss-cause distribution is the metric most directly tied to perceived fairness in the sources (death heatmaps, DPLI chokepoints, Riot "drilling down to a particular card"). A spiral shows up as a long tail of secondary causes after one primary trigger; a cliff shows up as one cause with near-zero variance in timing.
- No source found a validated "attribution survey" instrument; the closest practice is Mega Crit's warning about "super playtester" bias, which implies they segmented qualitative feedback by player skill.

### Gaps

- Could not access the full Slay the Spire GDC talk (members-only) so the exact metrics tracked (card pick rates, win rate per ascension, floor of death) are not confirmed from a primary source.
- No published Firaxis win-rate or balance telemetry for Civilization VI was found; only the completion-rate remark.
- No primary source found for a standard "player attribution" (my fault vs the game's fault) survey used in balance work.
- The Hochschule Trier DPLI and Lily's Garden papers were read only via abstracts/snippets; formulas were not verified.

## Key question 2: How do studios use a bot that plays well versus one that plays like a newcomer, and what did they learn from each (procedural personas)?

### Takeaway

Studios use strong (RL / self-play) bots to find exploits and dominant strategies before release and human-like or persona bots to predict what real players will experience; the consistent lesson is that optimal bots over-report exploitability and under-report confusion, while human-like bots require real player data to calibrate and are only as good as their designer-specified utilities.

### Cited Findings

- Holmgård et al. (IEEE ToG 2019) define procedural personas as "archetypal player models" used as synthetic playtesters "when human feedback is not readily available"; four personas in MiniDungeons 2 differ only in utility weights: Runner (proximity to exit minus 0.01 x steps), Monster Killer (0.7 x monsters slain + 0.3 x exit proximity), Treasure Collector (0.7 x treasures + 0.3 x exit), Completionist (0.7 x interactables + 0.3 x exit), each with a -5 death penalty. Evolved MCTS heuristics raised win rates from ~10% to 100% (Runner), 73% (Monster Killer), 54% (Treasure Collector), 100% (Completionist) across 11 maps x 50 trials. The Completionist's evolved formula ended up penalising opening chests, so the authors recommend revisiting utility weights when a persona's behaviour does not match its intended priorities. Limitations the authors state: only 11 maps, designer-specified utilities make results subjective, no human validation yet. — [arXiv 1802.06881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.06881)
- The same paper reports that per-persona level metrics correlate with level structure (e.g., Treasure Collector win rate vs path length r = -0.62; treasure ratio vs walls r = -0.69), which is the mechanism by which personas tell designers how different player types will experience content before human testing. — [arXiv 1802.06881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.06881)
- King (Candy Crush) explicitly chose human-likeness over strength: per AI Labs head Sahar Asadi, unlike AlphaGo the goal is to mimic real players; the bot "opts for the move most likely chosen by a human, based on behavior data from millions of game states", plays a level over 1,000 times, and reports metrics such as "Is this playable? How much of a challenge is it to the player?"; designers can also let it auto-adjust levels against criteria and then review the best solutions. — [Digital Trends interview](https://digitaltrends.com/?p=3565450)
- King's test lead Alexander Andelkovic described using Monte Carlo tree search, automatic heuristic construction, and NEAT to train bots to test and evaluate difficulty levels. — [TechWell conference speaker page](https://conferences.techwell.com/archives/adceast-2016/speakers/alexander-andelkovic.html)
- King's separate BAIT bot is a QA crawler (object detection, scene classification, state graph of 4,500+ levels) that finds rendering/functional defects, not a gameplay or balance agent; the author argues humans remain necessary for "Does this look good? Is this fun?". — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/articles/candy-crush-QA-AI-saga/)
- Supercell (mo.co, PGC Helsinki 2025): RL bots that "start by acting essentially at random" with successful actions reinforced; thousands of bots tested content, features and gear; bots "gravitated toward particular items", which flagged those items as overpowered; a new model was trained every one or two weeks as new ideas were introduced; imitation learning from real players "was not yet possible" and bots were not used as live opponents. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/how-moco-used-ai-to-test-the-player-experience/)
- Riot (Legends of Runeterra): a self-play RL agent trained from 1 deck up to 10, reaching a 48% win rate against top humans; self-play surfaced decks that were too strong or weak and "with a little digging" one overpowered card; productionised with an API, app, monitoring and dashboard. — [Anyscale blog (vendor summary)](https://www.anyscale.com/blog/riot-games-and-deep-reinforcement-learning-in-gaming)
- Google Chimera: a CNN win-probability model trained by iterated self-play from random play; the designers' three discoveries were a deck imbalance (60/40), an under-used mechanic (players evolved less than intended), and a single overpowered unit (T-Rex played even under biome penalties). Google claims imbalances were found "in a span of days" versus months of traditional playtesting. — [Google Research blog](https://www.research.google/blog/leveraging-machine-learning-for-game-development/)
- Valve built SurvivorBots as "Competent Human Player Proxies" which "allowed us to assume 4 player Survivor team for game tuning and balance" and enabled "automated stress testing with 4 SurvivorBots and accelerated game time"; the behaviour system was "built to reproduce decisions and actions players make while playing the game". — [Valve L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Politowski et al. deliberately trained two skill tiers (novice = 100K steps, professional = 1M steps; ~6 min vs ~1 hour for PPO on Batkill) alongside novice and expert humans and a random agent; PPO's cross-version pattern tracked humans most closely though its behaviour "did not look human-like" (constant jumping). — [arXiv 2304.08699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08699)
- EA's "Winning Isn't Everything" paper argues for agents that run on a bare-bones mechanics recreation rather than the live client, reporting that an agent can play in minutes what would take testers days; a related Sims Mobile study reports that "analysis of thousands of game simulations exposed imbalances in game actions and evaluated the effectiveness of optional strategic choices". — [arXiv 1903.10545](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.10545)
- Mega Crit's talk outline flags "super playtester" bias as a data hazard, i.e. the strongest human testers distort balance readings. — [Podwise outline](https://podwise.ai/episodes/1459444)
- Vendor pitches encountered (flagged, not relied on): Unity's Game Simulation service and an Illogika anecdote about reworking boost systems; "Chimera: Game Crucible" (itch.io tool); Wayline blog posts; modl.ai marketing on Riot shooter bots. — [Unity blog](https://blog.unity.com/es/news/unity-ai-interns-help-shape-the-world); [modl.ai](https://modl.ai/riot-games-and-modl-shooter-bots)

### Inferences

- The strong-bot / persona-bot split maps onto two different questions: "can this be broken?" (RL, self-play, Supercell/Riot/Google) and "what will a typical player experience?" (King's behaviour-cloned bot, Holmgård personas, Politowski's novice tier). For a management sim with no PvP, the first question becomes "is there a dominant strategy that trivialises the economy?" and the second "does a reasonable-but-imperfect policy spiral?"
- A cheap substitute for behaviour cloning when there is no player data is Holmgård's approach: same search algorithm, different utility weights (e.g., a cash-hoarder, a growth-maximiser, a worker-welfare persona), and compare their survival curves. The authors' own caveat applies: the personas are only as honest as the designer's weights.
- Supercell's "bots gravitated toward one item" is a general detector for dominant strategies: track the entropy of a bot population's choices; collapse to one option means a balance problem.

### Gaps

- No primary Supercell or Riot write-up found; both are known through conference reports (PocketGamer.biz, Anyscale). Model details and quantitative results were not published.
- Could not confirm whether King's Candy Crush bot work was presented at GDC; the talks found were at TechWell/Better Software and an industry event reported by Digital Trends.
- No Unity ML-Agents first-party balance case study was found beyond the vendor blog; the RaidEnv benchmark (arXiv 2307.01676) uses ML-Agents but was not read in full.

## Key question 3: How can brittleness (high variance across seeds, spirals, cliffs) be detected rather than mean difficulty alone?

### Takeaway

The methods in the sources that go beyond a mean are: comparing random vs trained agents per version (luck dominance), survival/attempt distributions rather than completion rates, death density/clustering, per-segment "resource margin" or intensity traces, and A/B over large doubled/halved parameter changes; none of the studio sources published a seed-variance statistic explicitly, so a seed-level variance protocol is an inference from these building blocks.

### Cited Findings

- Politowski et al. find luck-dominated versions by checking whether the random agent's score converges with trained agents' scores; this is their only "chance" test, and they note stochastic game elements make agent training harder and that they did not measure variance across runs (median of two). — [arXiv 2304.08699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08699)
- The same paper tunes by "Doubling and Halving", altering attributes by large amounts "so the difference is noticeable" before measuring. — [arXiv 2304.08699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08699)
- The DPLI indicator quantifies both magnitude and density of deaths to find chokepoints (a cliff signature: many deaths in a narrow window). — [Informatik-Bericht 2015-01](https://www.hochschule-trier.de/fileadmin/Hauptcampus/Fachbereich_Informatik/Forschung/Informatik_Berichte/2015/Informatik-Bericht-2015-01.pdf)
- Kristensen et al. argue for modelling the distribution of in-level actions rather than pass probability alone, because success rate hides what happens inside the level. — [arXiv 2107.03305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03305)
- Valve's Director maintains a continuous per-survivor intensity trace (raised by damage proportional to amount, incapacitation, being pulled off ledges, nearby infected deaths inversely proportional to distance; decays toward zero, but not while infected are actively engaging). This trace is what the Director reads to decide Build Up / Sustain Peak (3-5 s after peak) / Peak Fade / Relax (30-45 s or until far enough toward the next safe room). — [Valve L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Valve's mob size "grows from minimum just after spawn to maximum after a duration to balance difficulty of successive, frequent Mobs", i.e. an explicit anti-spiral rule so that back-to-back events cannot compound. — [Valve L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- RimWorld's adaptation factor is an explicit anti-spiral mechanism: it starts at 0.8, cannot rise for the first 30 days, accrues slowly, and loses 30 "AdaptDays" per colonist death (20 at pop 20+) and 3.5-8 per downed colonist; the factor is clamped to 0.4-1.47 and, at the "Losing is fun" default adaptation impact of 40%, to 0.75-1.19. — [RimWorld wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- RimWorld's raid points also have a floor (35) and a cap (10,000, roughly 250 tribals / 100 pirates / 25 centipedes) applied before Randy's 50-150% roll and quest multipliers, which can push raids past the cap; wealth points flatten at 4,200 beyond 1M storyteller wealth. — [RimWorld wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- Steam players describe the adaptation factor in exactly the terms of spiral prevention: "after losses adaption drops a bit to give you a breather then gradually returns to normal" (anecdotal). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/3160958175962150146)
- Valve removed a cause of designer-made variance: "Even multiple sets of manually placed triggers/scripts fails" because players memorise them; procedural population made each session "a skill challenge instead of a memorization exercise". — [Valve L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- "Dungeons & Replicants" (Pfau, Liapis et al.) uses deep player-behaviour models of Aion players to generate agents and finds "balance differences in enemy encounters" across multiple difficulty dimensions rather than one axis (read via search snippet only). — [Liapis paper PDF](https://www.antoniosliapis.com/papers/dungeons_replicants_automated_game_balancing_across_multiple_difficulty_dimensions_via_deep_player_behavior_modeling.pdf)
- A godot-autosim style headless harness "plays your game thousands of times headlessly and gives you win rates, turn distributions, and custom metrics" (open-source tool listing, not a validated method). — [Godot asset library entry](https://www.godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/edit/21910)

### Inferences

- Seed-variance protocol assembled from the sources: fix a policy (scripted or persona), run N seeds, and report the full distribution of time-to-goal and time-to-loss (survival curve), not the mean. A bimodal survival curve (many early losses plus many wins) is the signature of a cliff; a survival curve whose hazard rate rises steadily after a first adverse event is a spiral. Politowski's random-agent comparison can then be applied per seed-bucket: if random and competent policies both die on the same seeds, the seed is unwinnable, not hard.
- Spiral detection specifically: log the first adverse event per run and the time from it to loss; if the conditional loss rate after event X is far above the unconditional rate, X is a spiral trigger (the RimWorld and Valve designs both assume this and dampen it by rule).
- Cliff detection: compute death density per in-game time window (the DPLI idea); a cliff is a window whose density is an outlier across seeds and policies.
- Doubling/halving before measuring (Politowski) is a sensible first pass for brittle games: small changes get lost in seed noise, large ones show which knob moves the loss-cause distribution.

### Gaps

- No studio source published an explicit "variance across seeds" statistic or threshold; the protocol above is inferred.
- Could not read the full Dungeons & Replicants paper or the RaidEnv paper to extract their difficulty-dimension metrics.

## Key question 4: How are difficulty knobs exposed without fragmenting the design (RimWorld custom difficulty, Frostpunk per-system sliders, ONI, Two Point, Planet Coaster, Celeste assist mode)?

### Takeaway

The pattern across management sims is: a small ladder of named presets that are nothing more than saved values of a large per-system knob set, with a "custom" screen exposing every knob, plus one or two orthogonal commitment toggles (save-scumming, permadeath, pause) kept separate from numeric difficulty. Celeste is the contrast: knobs on the player's own capabilities rather than on the world's pressures, framed explicitly as departing from the tuned default.

### Cited Findings

- RimWorld: six presets (Peaceful, Community builder, Adventure story, Strive to survive, Blood and dust, Losing is fun) are each a row of ~30 custom values, e.g. threat scale 10/30/60/100/155/220%, colonist mood offset +10/+10/+5/0/-5/-10, food poison chance 30/50/75/100/110/120%, disease frequency 33/40/67/100/105/111%, harvest yield 120/120/100/100/95/80%, trade price disadvantage 0/0/0/0/10/20%, adaptation growth rate 0/25/75/100/100/100% and adaptation impact 100/100/100/90/70/40%, plus boolean toggles (major threats, quest threats, predators hunt humans, extreme weather, map ancient threats). "Set to Standard Playstyle" loads any preset's values into the custom screen as a starting point; the storyteller (Cassandra/Phoebe/Randy, controlling pacing and tone) is a separate choice, and both can be changed mid-game. — [RimWorld wiki: Difficulty](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Difficulty)
- RimWorld keeps commitment orthogonal: "Commitment" (one save, save only on quit, game ends only when the whole colony dies) vs "Reload anytime" is chosen at creation and cannot be changed afterward. A "wealth-independent progress mode" replaces wealth-driven threat with a timer (10k assigned wealth at day 0, 1M at day 720, 2.5M at day 1,800 by default). — [RimWorld wiki: Difficulty](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Difficulty); [RimWorld wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- Randy Random differs from Cassandra and Phoebe only by a 50-150% multiplier on each event's raid points after the cap, i.e. storytellers change variance and pacing, not the mean threat scale. — [RimWorld wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- Frostpunk 2 exposes four per-system sliders, locked once the mission starts: Economy (how much residents need and how scarce resources are: Abundant / Sufficient / Scarce / Starved), Weather (frequency and strength of cold seasons and Whiteouts: Mild to Severe), Frostland (people and resources available outside the city: Rich to Desolate), Society (how people react to problems and broken promises, how hard laws are to pass: Calm to Frantic). The preset (Citizen, Officer, Steward, Captain) "moves all of the individual sliders up or down"; Survivor Mode is a separate toggle. — [Escapist Magazine](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/all-frostpunk-2-difficulty-settings-explained/); [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Frostpunk-2/archives/474290)
- Original Frostpunk: scenario options offer an Easy/Medium/Hard meter and also let the player "adjust other individual options like the weather conditions and city economy"; a later update added an Extreme difficulty based on Survivor Mode but allowing pause and save. — [Twinfinite guide](https://twinfinite.net/guides/frostpunk-change-difficulty-how/); [Frostpunk 2 presets context](https://primagames.com/gaming/best-frostpunk-2-difficulty-settings-citizen-officer-steward-and-captain-differences-explained)
- Oxygen Not Included: two presets (Survival = all defaults; No Sweat = Disease "Germ Resistant", Morale "Chill", Hunger "Fasting" 500 kcal/cycle, Stress "Chipper" -10%/cycle) over a custom menu of five-step dials: Disease (Total Immunity to Outbreak Prone), Morale (Totally Blase, never gains stress, to Draconian), Hunger (0/500/1000/1500/2000 kcal), Stress (no gain / -10% / default / +10% / +20% per cycle), Suit durability (0-20%/cycle), Meteor showers (Clear Skies to Doomsday), Radiation and Wattage in DLC, plus checkboxes for 100% stress reactions, care packages, teleporters and sandbox. No Sweat explicitly does not reduce oxygen needs, temperature effects, building ratios or world generation, and enabling sandbox permanently disables achievements. — [ONI wiki: Game Settings](https://oxygennotincluded.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Settings)
- Two Point Campus sandbox has four modes: Standard (a regular level's challenge with free map choice), Creative (finances removed, build freely), Challenge (fixed money and "a challenging balance sheet to manage"), and Custom (options to raise or lower difficulty; disasters can be turned off). — [Gamer Guides](https://gamerguides.com/two-point-campus/guide/getting-started/basics/sandbox-mode); [Shacknews review](https://www.shacknews.com/article/131697/two-point-campus-review)
- Planet Coaster: Challenge mode has three difficulty levels (Medium default) where each level changes starting cash, research and guest happiness loss; Sandbox gives everything unlocked and unlimited funds. Players reported Medium felt slow to bank money and that "even the Hardest mode is simple once basic profit is achieved" (anecdotal). Planet Coaster 2 adds Creative vs Challenge inside Sandbox and "extensive custom difficulty settings". — [Planet Coaster fandom: Challenge](https://planetcoaster.fandom.com/wiki/Challenge); [planetcoaster.com game modes](https://www.planetcoaster.com/game-modes); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/493340/discussions/0/152391285460399256)
- Celeste Assist Mode changes player-facing rules (game speed, stamina, number of air dashes, invincibility) rather than removing content as Cuphead's simple mode did; it was renamed from "Cheat Mode" because that name was "judgmental"; the in-game text says "We recommend playing without Assist Mode your first time"; achievements remain unlockable; Thorson says it "breaks the game" from the designer's perspective but "sometimes that means letting go", and the aim is to let players move between difficulty levels "without judgement or implication that they aren't playing the game 'as intended'". Celeste's own difficulty controls otherwise only go one way: harder (B-sides, strawberries). — [Vice interview](https://www.vice.com/en/article/celeste-difficulty-assist-mode/)
- Valve's Director is explicitly not a difficulty knob: boss encounters are excluded from adaptive pacing because "overall pacing affected too much if they are missing", and the algorithm changes frequency not amplitude; mob interval 90-180 s is quoted "on Normal difficulty", so the difficulty setting sits underneath the Director rather than inside it. — [Valve L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)

### Inferences

- The anti-fragmentation trick every management sim here uses is the same: one knob table, presets are rows, and the custom screen is the table. The design stays coherent because every preset is testable as a point in the same parameter space, and the bots/telemetry from questions 2-3 can be run per preset row.
- Knobs are grouped by pressure system (economy, weather, society, disease, stress) rather than by outcome, which keeps each slider explainable ("how scarce resources are") and lets a player turn down the one pressure they find unfair without flattening the rest. ONI's note that No Sweat leaves oxygen and temperature untouched is a deliberate statement of which pressures define the game.
- Celeste's assist knobs act on player capability, not world pressure; for a management sim the analogue is knobs like "pause available", "undo", "forgiving deadlines", kept separate from pressure sliders, as RimWorld's Commitment mode and Frostpunk's Survivor Mode already are.
- Cliff-prone systems benefit from the Frostpunk 2 rule of locking sliders at start; mid-game adjustment (RimWorld allows it) is itself a safety valve for spirals, at the cost of achievement/leaderboard comparability.

### Gaps

- Could not retrieve a primary 11 bit studios source for the original Frostpunk's custom sub-settings and what each changes numerically (fandom wiki returned 402; the official post redirected to a store page).
- Planet Coaster 2's custom difficulty option list was not found in detail.
- No source found explaining how any of these studios validated that a preset row still delivers the intended experience (e.g., telemetry per preset).

## Key question 5: What did Ludeon learn tuning the storytellers (wealth-based threat, adaptation after losses), and Valve from the Director?

### Takeaway

RimWorld's current system is a layered formula (wealth + pawns, then difficulty, start-grace, adaptation, caps, storyteller variance) whose structure encodes lessons about spirals and runaway wealth, but Ludeon's own design notes explaining those choices were not found; Valve's documented lessons are that the Director should modulate pacing not difficulty, that a crude intensity estimate suffices, that scripted encounters degrade into memorisation, and that strong co-op teams suppress their own drama and needed specific enemies to break them.

### Cited Findings

- RimWorld raid formula: Raid Points = (Wealth Points + Pawn Points) x Difficulty x Starting Factor x Adaptation Factor, min 35, cap 10,000. Storyteller wealth counts items and creatures fully and buildings at 50%; wealth points are 0 up to 14k, 2,400 at 400k, 3,600 at 700k, 4,200 at 1M and flat above. Pawn points per colonist run from 15 (up to 10k wealth) to 140 (400k) to 200 (1M), with slaves 75%, cryptosleep 30%, children scaling with age; prisoners and quest lodgers excluded; tamed combat animals 8% of combat power; player mechs 20-40%. Starting factor 0.7 through day 10 rising to 1.0 by day 40. — [RimWorld wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- Adaptation: starts at 0.8, 30-day grace, accrual slows as it rises (gains of 3 below zero, 1 to 60 days, 0.5 to 120, 0.25 beyond, halved per 12-hour check per the wiki), minus 30 AdaptDays per death (20 at pop 20+) and 3.5-8 per downed colonist, clamped -60 to 100 AdaptDays mapping to 0.4-1.47; executing or banishing colonists does not count. The custom "adaptation impact" and "growth rate" settings scale it (impact 40% default on Losing is fun gives 0.75-1.19). — [RimWorld wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- Storytellers differ in variance, not mean: Randy multiplies each event by a random 50-150% after the cap; Cassandra and Phoebe apply no further modification; quest threats use star count (1-3) and a 70-130% roll instead. — [RimWorld wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- Wealth-independent mode exists as an alternative progression (time-based assigned wealth), implying Ludeon recognised wealth-scaling creates perverse incentives (the wiki's "Wealth management" page is a player guide to suppressing wealth growth). — [RimWorld wiki: Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points); [RimWorld wiki: Wealth management](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Wealth_management)
- Valve, stated lessons: "Adaptive Dramatic Pacing reacts to Survivor team. Generates reliable peaks of intensity without completely overwhelming the team"; "Because of player variation and procedural threat population, timing and location of peaks will differ each time the game is played"; "Simple algorithms can generate compelling pacing schedules"; "Survivor Intensity estimation is crude, yet the resulting pacing works"; and "Algorithm adjusts pacing, not difficulty". — [Valve L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Valve on structured unpredictability: population is "not purely random, nor deterministically uniform" but a superposition of designer-bounded random functions (wanderers high frequency; mobs of 20-30 every 90-180 s on Normal; special infected individually randomised; bosses shuffled from Tank / Witch / Nothing with no immediate repeats; weapon caches and item groups designer-placed and Director-selected). 75% of mobs spawn behind the team. Wanderer counts are zeroed in areas visible to survivors and during Relax. — [Valve L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Valve on why procedural rather than scripted: static placement means "players are good at memorizing all static locations", which "removes suspense", "kills cooperation and degenerates into a race", and multiple scripted variants also fail. — [Valve L4D slides](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Valve playtest lessons reported from GDC 2009: disciplined teams "dampen their own drama", so the Smoker was added to disrupt perfect plans; an early exploding Boomer met the "think before you shoot" goal but hurt new players and was merged with the Screamer; Booth's heuristic "low probability + high drama = memorable"; the four survivors were treated as a single player for tuning. — [Shacknews GDC report](https://www.shacknews.com/article/57892/left-4-dead-at-gdc)
- Gabe Newell framed the Director as "procedural narrative", "more of a storytelling device than a simple difficulty mechanism", using players' past encounters to shape later ones. — [Wikipedia: Left 4 Dead (citing Newell in Edge)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_4_Dead)
- Hades (Supergiant) used early access similarly: Kasavin said mechanics were retooled using "our own impressions, player feedback, and data we're seeing based on how players are engaging with everything that's there" (e.g., simplifying the Smithy Sprint boon); for Hades II the first patch note said balance changes would follow "based on what we've observed". — [GeekDad interview](https://geekdad.com/2019/10/narrative-and-early-access-supergiants-greg-kasavin-discusses-hades-development/); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/hades-2s-first-patch-improves-sprinting-and-gathering/)

### Inferences

- RimWorld's formula reads as a list of fixes for brittleness: a start-grace factor (no cliff in the first 40 days), a 30-day adaptation grace, adaptation that falls on deaths (anti-spiral), a floor and ceiling on raid points (bounded cliff height), wealth flattening above 1M (no runaway late-game), and a wealth-independent alternative for players who found the wealth coupling perverse. Each is a candidate knob for a brittle management sim: grace period, post-loss relief, bounded event size, and an alternative progression driver.
- Valve's separation of pacing (Director) from amplitude (difficulty setting) is the key transferable pattern: an adaptive director that lowers event frequency after a loss avoids a spiral without changing the fairness contract, whereas a director that silently lowers amplitude makes outcomes unattributable and hard to balance.
- Valve's "crude estimate suffices" is a licence to use a simple stress proxy (e.g., cash runway or morale derivative in a management sim) rather than a learned model.

### Gaps

- No Ludeon/Tynan Sylvester design notes on why wealth-based scaling or the adaptation factor were introduced or how they were tuned were found; the RimWorld wiki raid-points page contains no design commentary. Forum explanations of adaptation are anecdotal.
- Valve's slides end with "Future Work"; no post-launch telemetry on the Director's effect (e.g., retention or wipe rates) was found, and the L4D2 "Director 2.0" changes were not covered by any reachable source.
- No Soren Johnson primary source on telemetry dashboards for Offworld Trading Company or Old World economy balance was found (searches returned only general early-access feedback remarks and the GDC 2017 talk abstract, which is paywalled).
