# Two Point Hospital, Two Point Campus and Theme Hospital: how they were designed and tuned for fair challenge

Sourcing conventions used below:

- **[official]** = developer interview, publisher page, Wikipedia-summarised developer statement.
- **[wiki]** = curated fan wiki (two-point-hospital.fandom.com, StrategyWiki). Numbers there are player-measured or datamined, not developer-published.
- **[CorsixTH]** = the open-source reimplementation of Theme Hospital. Its level files (`Levels/*.level`) mirror the original game's level-config format, but several balance constants in its Lua (notably the reputation-change table) are CorsixTH's own approximations, flagged in-source with "TODO: This should depend on difficulty and level". Treat them as "how a faithful clone chose to model it", not as Bullfrog's numbers.
- **[player]** = Steam/Reddit/forum reports and user reviews.

## Key question 1: What did Two Point Studios say about difficulty, fairness, pacing and the star system?

### Takeaway

The studio's stated philosophy is deliberately low-punishment: Gary Carr says "Our games don't really punish you" and that he dislikes win criteria that block progression, so Hospital uses a one-star gate to advance with two more stars as optional, return-later goals; the founders describe balancing the interlocking systems as the hardest part of the project, and they framed the three stars as a progression system that rewards revisiting earlier hospitals with later unlocks, not as a score.

### Cited Findings

- Ben Hymers (co-founder, technical director) on the star structure before launch: levels go beyond a single objective, "there are three stars to guide you through the level and this unlocks stuff upon completing." — [TheSixthAxis interview, April 2018](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2018/04/24/interview-two-point-hospital-devs-on-their-favourite-diseases-and-slapstick-vomit/) [official]
- Hymers called balance "going to be the big one," because the many interlocking systems create emergent behaviour that is hard to tune; the team was explicitly aiming at "making sure it works for short play sessions and playing for hours on end." Richard Brooks (producer) said the balance "has already been made a lot better in the last couple of months." — [TheSixthAxis, April 2018](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2018/04/24/interview-two-point-hospital-devs-on-their-favourite-diseases-and-slapstick-vomit/) [official]
- Every hospital is saved in its current state so the player builds "an empire of hospitals"; every level is completable when reached, but later unlocks (new vending-machine types, better heating for a cold-region site) can be taken back to make earlier goals easier. Hymers contrasted this with Theme Hospital's "rather linear progression, with a sequence of levels," saying "the flow is completely different now with a non-linear structure." — [TheSixthAxis, April 2018](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2018/04/24/interview-two-point-hospital-devs-on-their-favourite-diseases-and-slapstick-vomit/) [official]
- On pacing/replayability, Hymers said they were "aiming to make it replayable," with challenges that appear over time "rather than just saying 'you're done, move on'"; Brooks cited health inspectors and regional scenario events (e.g. broken legs in mountain hospitals) as the recurring disruptions. — [TheSixthAxis, April 2018](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2018/04/24/interview-two-point-hospital-devs-on-their-favourite-diseases-and-slapstick-vomit/) [official]
- In a second TheSixthAxis interview, Mark Webley said a player who stays on a scenario's first hospital can still work toward its second and third stars, though it may be hard, because later hospitals unlock cures that can be carried back; Hymers framed the stars as a progression system rather than a score, said the system came from wanting players to return to earlier hospitals and improve them, said reaching one star lets you advance but "more remains to do," and denied the system copied mobile-game star mechanics. — [TheSixthAxis, "Why Two Point Hospital Is Worth Making An Appointment For"](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=318888) (summarised from search result; article not fetched in full) [official]
- Gary Carr: "Our games don't really punish you." He said he does not enjoy games built around "win criteria" that block progression, and that Two Point's games "let you tinker with these little people and let you advance at whatever speed you wish to advance." Mark Webley: "Finishing a game like this is really, really hard," because a small change to the interlocking systems can break the game; and "Once a game is shipped, we're not finished." The piece describes the studio's view of its games as "plate-spinning" exercises, and Webley describes the cartoon diseases as a "safer place to actually build the simulation." — [Game Developer, "How Two Point Studios carved its simulation game niche"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-two-point-studios-carved-its-simulation-game-niche) [official]
- Carr, looking back from Museum (2025): gameplay is "far more directed in Hospital compared to Museum," and "Part of the development of our series is to allow the player freedom of choice and not to feel led by the nose." — [Shacknews 10th-anniversary interview](https://www.shacknews.com/article/150023/two-point-studios-10th-anniversary-interview) [official]
- Mark Smart (art director) said the team wanted the game to be accessible and wanted players to feel confident in the sub-menus; Webley and Carr wanted a broader audience than Theme Hospital; Hymers noted that 3D made queues and corridor movement much harder to get right than Theme Hospital's 2D sprites. — [Wikipedia: Two Point Hospital, Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Point_Hospital) [official, via secondary]
- Carr on a specific Two Point Hospital level: "It is actually one of the harder levels to play because you have to make decisions that will have an impact down the line." Carr on Theme Hospital's payment model: "People thought because you got paid we were trying to recreate an American-style hospital system"; the money was there to give players rewards. Webley: "The secret is, don't make something extraordinary into a game... take relatable and understandable things, and turn them into something extraordinary." — [New Statesman](https://www.newstatesman.com/?p=221062) [official]
- Hymers also said the reputation system is "more detailed" than Theme Hospital's and that there is now an overall hygiene simulation; Brooks described a "micro-behavioural system" where patients react to surroundings (e.g. disliking vomit), contrasted with "that one Theme Hospital level where you needed 20 Janitors." — [TheSixthAxis, April 2018](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2018/04/24/interview-two-point-hospital-devs-on-their-favourite-diseases-and-slapstick-vomit/) [official]
- Carr described a retired nurse in her 80s who had played Hospital for five years as one of their biggest fans, and said "everyone gets something out of it" across play styles. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-two-point-studios-carved-its-simulation-game-niche) [official]

### Inferences

- The "one star to progress, three to master" structure is the studio's answer to the Theme Hospital problem of a hard gate per level: fail-states are soft (see Q3) and the optional stars absorb the challenge for players who want it, which is consistent with Carr's "don't punish" line.
- The fact that both founders describe balancing as the hardest, most fragile part, and that the studio says it is "not finished" at ship, suggests tuning was expected to continue in patches rather than be locked at release.

### Gaps

- I found no GDC Vault or Develop:Brighton talk by Webley, Carr, Hymers or Smart specifically about difficulty or the star system; searches returned only press interviews. A W.A.S.D. 2022 panel with the three founders exists (PCGamesN listing) but no transcript was found.
- No developer statement with concrete tuning numbers (reputation deltas, arrival curves, star thresholds) was found; every number below is wiki- or code-sourced.

## Key question 2: How does Two Point Hospital communicate a developing problem before it becomes a failure, and what overlays and panels exist?

### Takeaway

Hospital layers its warnings: per-person icons and "Feelings" (Close to Death, Dying with a tolling bell, Unhappy/about to rage quit), queue warning icons over rooms, an advisor (Albert Crank) plus emails/letters in an inbox, an always-visible objectives panel, environmental heat-map overlays (attractiveness, temperature, hygiene, hunger/thirst), and finance screens; the explicit money fail state is preceded by a warning at -$150,000 before bankruptcy at -$300,000.

### Cited Findings

- Interface structure: a bottom-left Control Panel with Menu (rooms, items, hire), Characters (Staff, Patients, Illnesses lists), Finance (Overview, Prices, Loans) and a Data View tab where "All the Visualisation Modes are kept"; an Information Panel with Mail & Inbox, Money, Reputation, Hospital Level, Kudosh and Calendar; a Task Panel with Objectives and Online Challenges; and Albert Crank, "the advisor that helps keep you on track." — [TPH wiki: Interface](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Interface) [wiki]
- The Objectives tab lists what is needed for the next star; special events such as emergencies and staff requests appear at the bottom of it. Incoming emails appear in a top row above the money display, with a tray icon for past messages. Hovering the money readout shows income, expenses and profit. — [MagicGameWorld UI guide](https://www.magicgameworld.com/two-point-hospital-ui-and-reading-your-menus/) [player guide]
- Queue communication: "Look out for queue warnings above rooms. You can also hover over a room to highlight people queueing for that room." — [TPH wiki: Hospitals](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Hospitals) [wiki]
- Dying patients: a patient whose health approaches zero shows a 'Close to Death!' icon; "when it is too late" a 'Dying!' icon appears "accompanied by the sound of a tolling bell." Patients die either when health reaches zero or when treatment fails (each illness has its own death probability on failure; survivors leave unhappy). On death there is a 50% chance a ghost spawns. — [TPH wiki: Death](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Death) [wiki]
- Patient panel shows Happiness and Health bars, needs (thirst, hunger, toilet, boredom, comfort), environment readouts (hygiene, attractiveness, temperature), a log, and action buttons: Send for Treatment "only appears once the Patient has at least 50% Diagnosis Certainty," and Send Home. Patients enter with full health and lose it at an illness-dependent rate; low hygiene makes them lose health faster. "If they become unhappy, they will leave to go to another hospital"; a patient who hits 0% will 'Rage Quit' and leave instantly, which "will affect the hospital reputation." — [TPH wiki: Patients](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Patients) [wiki]
- Feelings are the per-person "thought" layer. Patient examples with magnitudes: Treatment Failed -7% happiness; Desperate for Toilet, Very Thirsty, Very Hungry, Very Bored each -12% happiness per month; "Unhappy - About to rage quit"; "Health Low - 'Close to death'"; Too Cold/Too Hot (variable). Staff examples: Underpaid -25% happiness; Unhappy ("I hate my job") -20% speed, -20% diagnosis skill, -20% treatment skill; Exhausted -40% speed, -20% skills; Wants training -5%; Tired -20%; Cured a Patient +10%. — [TPH wiki: Feelings](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Feelings) [wiki]
- Overlays: the Attractiveness heat map glows green for positive items (plants, decor) and brown for negatives (full bins, clogged toilets, litter, Monobeast infestations, explosion debris). The Temperature visualisation shows blue (too cold), red (too hot) and yellow (right); machines that are breaking down "change from a dull Purple to bright Red" as they approach combustion. The Hygiene visualisation shows low-hygiene people bright red, high-hygiene bright green, dirty environment brown. Patients put on winter hats when cold. — [TPH wiki: Hospitals](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Hospitals) [wiki]; overlays also for hunger/thirst demand per area — [Gamepressure: hospital parameters](https://www.gamepressure.com/two-point-hospital/hospital-parameters/z0b491) [player guide]
- Emergencies arrive as a letter and "it is possible, ironically, to ignore the emergency notification until such time as the player is ready to accept the patients." VIP visitors are also announced by letter, but "VIPs will not wait, and will take silence as consent"; some (Henry Jobsworth, the health inspector) arrive whether ready or not; they visit 3-6 rooms and a results letter follows, with reputation loss or "occasionally" a fine if displeased. — [TPH wiki: Emergencies](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Emergencies); [TPH wiki: Visitors](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Visitors) [wiki]
- Money fail state and its warning: "You receive a warning at -$150,000 and then are officially bankrupt at -$300,000. At which point you can choose to play from the last save or restart the hospital completely." — [TPH wiki: Money](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Money) [wiki]. Players variously reported the threshold as -200k ("-200k money is the only fail condition") and -300k ("You get bankrupt if you hit -300k. At least on one of the hospitals"). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/1743352529779588584) [player]
- Game-level warning: "While in debt, you cannot hire new staff," and "Debt harms reputation." — [Gamepressure: finances](https://www.gamepressure.com/two-point-hospital/finances/z3b494) [player guide]
- Diagnosis messaging: when the game reports there are no further diagnostic methods, the guide advises adding another diagnostic room; "If the certainty of diagnosis is lower, the patient treatment may turn out to be lethal." — [Gamepressure: patients](https://www.gamepressure.com/two-point-hospital/patients/z9b412) [player guide]
- Critics on the UI: Paul Tamburro (Game Revolution) called the UI "seriously impressive" and said it took "all the work out of management"; Edge criticised the lack of room copying at launch (added in v1.04, 14 Sept 2018). — [Wikipedia: Two Point Hospital, Reception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Point_Hospital); [TPH wiki: Patches](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Patches) [official/wiki]
- A hospital's awards are judged "at the end of each December" (Doctor of the Year, No Deaths, Most Prestigious Hospital, Patients' Choice Award etc.). — [MagicGameWorld](https://www.magicgameworld.com/two-point-hospital-ui-and-reading-your-menus/); [TPH wiki: Hogsport](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Hogsport) [wiki]

### Inferences

- Almost every failure path has a two-stage signal: Close to Death before Dying; Thirsty (-1%) before Very Thirsty (-10/-12%); Unhappy before Rage Quit; -$150k warning before -$300k bankruptcy. This is the game's main fairness device: it rarely fails the player without a graded prior warning.
- Because emergencies can be deferred but VIP visits cannot, the game mixes player-paced pressure (most events) with a few forced inspections, which is where the "fairness" debate tends to focus among players.

### Gaps

- No official list of Albert Crank's advisor triggers or of the PA/tannoy announcements was found; the wiki's Complaints page is a stub.
- I could not confirm from an official source whether rage-quit is driven by comfort or boredom reaching zero (the wiki text is internally inconsistent).

## Key question 3: How do reputation, arrival rate, hospital level and money interact; why is "money too easy" a common complaint; what did Campus change and why?

### Takeaway

In Hospital, arrivals scale with both reputation and hospital level (a size metric from rooms and staff), reputation moves in small per-patient increments (+1 cure, -2 death, -0.25 sent home) that players found too weak to punish bad play, prices can be pushed up for cash at the cost of reputation, and the only hard failure is -$300k, so once a hospital is profitable the economy snowballs; Campus changed the pacing to discrete academic years with a summer break, let the player choose how many students to admit via course levels and Course Points, and so put intake growth under the player's control rather than reputation's.

### Cited Findings

**Two Point Hospital mechanics**

- Reputation "increases with each patient successfully cured, but decreases when a treatment fails, especially if a patient dies. A higher reputation attracts more patients and better staff for hire." It is "based in the outcome of a patient's visit, the happiness of people in the hospital, the prices you charge and other publicity." Wiki-measured deltas: Successful Treatment +1; Ineffective Treatment -1; Patient Death -2; Patient Sent Home -0.25; Patient Rage Quits -0.5; Patient Waited Too Long -0.25. Bands: Fine 50-60%, Good 73%, Great 86-100%. — [TPH wiki: Hospitals](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Hospitals) [wiki]
- Hospital Level "is a measure of the overall size of your hospital based on the rooms and staff"; higher level means "more patients and job applicants." It can degrade. Every 5 levels opens a new applicant slot per staff type (3 applicants at level 1, 4 at 5, 5 at 10, 6 at 15); max level 30. — [TPH wiki: Hospitals](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Hospitals) [wiki]
- The wiki states the arrival cap directly in its description of the later Horde mode: "In prior hospitals, patients have arrived in a constant stream, with the maximum number present at any one time determined by the hospital's level and reputation." — [TPH wiki: Hogsport (R.E.M.I.X section)](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Hogsport) [wiki]
- Reputation inputs per a guide: successfully treating a patient whose needs are met raises it; deaths lower it; emergencies (cure at least 50% within the time limit) raise it, failing lowers it; VIPs raise or lower it; marketing campaigns (recommended up to 4 months) raise it after the campaign ends; more buildings/rooms and higher room prestige raise it faster. — [Gamepressure: reputation](https://www.gamepressure.com/two-point-hospital/reputation/zbb40e) [player guide]
- Money: "The only stable source of income are the treatments the patients receive"; treatments pay more than diagnoses and "prices rise over time"; the Prices tab allows a discount or surcharge per service, but "Higher prices lower reputation and happiness." Loans: three lenders, the largest "becomes available at hospital level 5" per the guide. — [Gamepressure: finances](https://www.gamepressure.com/two-point-hospital/finances/z3b494); [MagicGameWorld](https://www.magicgameworld.com/two-point-hospital-ui-and-reading-your-menus/) [player guide]
- Loan terms (wiki): Two Point Bank $25,000-50,000 at 5% p.a., repaid $1,097-2,194/month over 24 months; Swindles $75,000-100,000 at 10% p.a., $3,461-4,615/month over 24 months; $MC (Smell My Cash) $150,000-250,000 at 15% p.a., $5,200-8,667/month over 36 months, requiring Hospital Value $500,000, Reputation 25 and Hospital Level 2. Repayments are automatic with monthly expenses; full early repayment is allowed. — [TPH wiki: Loans](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Loans) [wiki]
- Bankruptcy: warning at -$150,000, bankrupt at -$300,000 (reload or restart). — [TPH wiki: Money](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Money) [wiki]
- Star thresholds, first level (Hogsport): 1 star = cure 3 Lightheadedness patients (reward $10,000 + 100 Kudosh; unlocks Lower Bullocks); 2 stars = cure 25 patients, earn $200,000, reach Hospital Level 6 ($20,000 + 150 Kudosh); 3 stars = cure 30 patients, earn $400,000, Hospital Value $750,000 ($30,000 + 200 Kudosh). — [TPH wiki: Hogsport](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Hogsport) [wiki]
- Star thresholds, final base-game level (Croquembouche, World 5): 1 star = cure 250 patients, cure rate 70%, reputation 70%, hospital value $2.5M; 2 stars = 500 / 80% / 80% / $5M; 3 stars = 1,000 / 90% / 90% / $10M. It requires one star in both Pelican Wharf and Rotting Hill to unlock; land plots cost $85,000-130,000 and take 16 days to build. The level is described as having "the maximum number of patients eventually arriving" plus disasters, epidemics and all VIPs. — [TPH wiki: Croquembouche](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Croquembouche) [wiki]
- Emergency reward tables (example, DNA emergency): fail -5 reputation; pass +10 reputation, 10 Kudosh, $15,000; perfect +15 reputation, 15 Kudosh, $30,000; time limit 90 days; emergencies are 4-12 patients who skip diagnosis and jump the queue; "if less than half of the patients are cured, the hospital will take a hit to its reputation instead." VIP patient emergencies: 180-day limit, fail -5, pass +5 reputation, 10 Kudosh, $10,000. In the Lightheadedness emergency, "in the style of Theme Hospital, all untreated patients will immediately die upon the time limit being reached." — [TPH wiki: Emergencies](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Emergencies) [wiki]
- Epidemics: player gets a message and a fixed number of vaccines; infected patients look like normal ones except for an animation tell; rewards scale with vaccines left; too many runaways or running out of vaccines gives a "significant reputation loss." They appear mostly in Worlds 4-5 (Abominable Curse, Jogger's Ripple). — [Gamepressure: epidemics](https://www.gamepressure.com/two-point-hospital/epidemics/zbb531); [TPH wiki: Epidemics](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Epidemics) [wiki/player guide]
- Kudosh (second currency) is earned from Career Goals (e.g. Cure 100 patients = 100 Kudosh; Earn $1,000,000 = 100; Get 15 stars = 500; Get 45 stars = 1,000) and from multiplayer challenges; it buys item unlocks. — [TPH wiki: Career Goals](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Career_Goals) [wiki]
- Horde (R.E.M.I.X) mode, added later, changes the flow to waves: first wave 10 patients, +5 per wave, 70% cure rate required throughout, a 12-day countdown between waves, and a 150% income bonus after a 10-cure streak; the wiki itself jokes that stars "don't reflect anything tangible... they're just symbolic of progress." — [TPH wiki: Hogsport](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Hogsport) [wiki]

**Why "money too easy" / "no consequences" is a common complaint**

- Steam thread (9 Sept 2018, "Some observations from a long-time Theme Hospital player"): the OP argued the game is too easy because patients can die while the player still earns and wins; reputation "seems to have no real effect"; with a cure-percentage objective you can send patients home and only cure the easy ones; and "cure effectiveness" should return as a research item. Replies: one player found eight GP offices insufficient and had to send half the queue home; "Grumpy Goblin" agreed poor management has no real penalty since patients line up regardless of reputation, cited a leaderboard hospital valued at $125M earning about $22M a year with a low cure rate and a first-level hospital earning $7M a year from 55 cures, and said stacking ~50 medicine cabinets gives near-perfect diagnosis; another argued a stronger death penalty would make the early game unplayable because untrained nurses cannot prevent deaths until skilled staff unlock around level 4. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/2590022385666162871) [player]
- Other player reports: "you can have patients dying left and right, yet you still make money and win the level"; "I can have my reputation at zero, yet enough patients to complete all objectives, unless cure % is an objective"; a pricing exploit of raising all prices to bank cash (reputation drops to zero) then lowering them once built out, "there's really no consequence to poorly running the hospital"; counterpoints that "there comes a point in the game where the number of patients you treat cannot compensate the expenses," and that "early game challenge is queue management, the late game challenge is expanding whilst showing restraint." — Steam threads summarised in search results ([1](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/2590022385666162871), [2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/1743352529779588584)) [player]
- A user review: "too easy to make lots of money," and the Kudosh second currency "suggests that it's designed for grinding." — [RAWG user review](https://rawg.io/reviews/110517) [player]
- One player described falling "into a negative loop, in which you can't afford to fix the problems with your hospital," and another recovered from the "bankruptcy bump" by taking the first two loans back to back. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/1743352529779588584) [player]
- Critics mostly did not call it too easy: PC Gamer reported no noticeable lull after about 15 missions; GamingTrend said finances become a real challenge in later levels with wild month-to-month swings; PCGamesN's complaint was repetition (rebuilding the same GP's office, tannoy lines wearing thin); Fraser Brown (PC Gamer) said some "missions bleed together"; Metro also criticised repetition; GamesTM felt it was too similar to Theme Hospital. — [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/two-point-hospital-review/); [GamingTrend review](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/just-what-the-doctor-ordered-two-point-hospital-review/); [PCGamesN review](https://www.pcgamesn.com/two-point-hospital/two-point-hospital-review) (all via search summaries); [Wikipedia reception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Point_Hospital) [critic]
- Sandbox: Freeplay (v1.08, 26 Oct 2018) unlocked after the first three levels ("more or less its tutorial"), on all 15 campaign maps, with "over a dozen different settings that could make the game easier or harder" including unlimited cash, all rooms unlocked, patient numbers, temperature, Kudosh and removing epidemics; Game Revolution had criticised the lack of a sandbox at launch. — [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/content/sandbox-mode-two-point-hospital); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2018/10/two-point-hospital-is-getting-a-sandbox-freeplay-mode-allowing-you-to-do-whatever-you-want/page=1/); [TPH wiki: Patches](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Patches); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Point_Hospital) [official/wiki]
- Patch timeline (wiki, from Steam announcements): 1.01 (31 Aug 2018) through 1.04 copy-and-paste rooms (14 Sep 2018; also "improved queuing situation... balance tweaks" per a Steam thread), 1.06 customisation (3 Oct 2018), 1.08 Sandbox Freeplay (26 Oct 2018), 1.11 Interior Designer and Steam Workshop (6 Feb 2019), 1.14/1.15 Superbug Initiative (16 Apr 2019), 1.21 Room Templates (30 Jul 2020), 1.30 crossplay (14 Feb 2025). The only itemised balance change found was a Switch 1.0.8 note adjusting Chasm 24's disaster stabilisers. — [TPH wiki: Patches](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Patches); [Steam v1.04 thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/1742220359686940247); [Switch patch notes](https://benoitren.be/switchpatchnotes/twopointhospital.html) [wiki/official]

**What Two Point Campus changed and why**

- Structure: an academic year lasts roughly 20 minutes of play; the year opens with a summer break in which the player customises the campus and chooses courses before students arrive; at year end the player is graded on student performance "from 'all-star educator destined for tenure' to 'flunked out failure.'" Students stay for three-year courses, can fail or drop out, and have procedurally generated personalities. — [Wikipedia: Two Point Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Point_Campus); [SEGA press release](https://sega.prezly.com/two-point-campus-welcome-to-the-academic-year) [official]
- Ben Huskins (design director): "In Hospital, a patient comes in, they get diagnosed, they get treated" and the player is motivated to push them through quickly; students are on a course "for three years - assuming they don't drop out or fail," so the goal shifts to getting the most out of them socially and academically. Letter grades were chosen as "relatively universal." Room templates were added to Hospital post-launch and are in Campus from day one. Jo Koehler (senior producer) said the team "really tried to evolve what we did on Hospital" and wanted customisation accessible so players are not frustrated by complexity. — [TheGamer interview](https://www.thegamer.com/two-point-campus-interview-ben-huskins-jo-koehler/) [official]
- Carr on the emotional goal: "the byproduct we want is when you say goodbye to your first enrollment"; city-building was expanded for players who prefer building over management; community input came via Amplitude's Games2Gether. — [Wikipedia: Two Point Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Point_Campus) [official via secondary]
- Intake under player control: Course Points are earned 10 per Campus Level gained, banked and spent after the year ends in Manage Courses; upgrading a course increases the number of students admitted (a single level-one course starts with ten students); "a high-level course can easily generate six figures for your university every year, while entry-level courses often barely break even"; guides warn not to increase intake "until you're very comfortable with your campus." Campus Level rises with rooms, plots, staff and students. — [GamesRadar: Course Points](https://gamesradar.com/two-point-campus-course-points); [GamesRadar: Campus Level](https://www.gamesradar.com/two-point-campus-level); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/two-point-campus-guide-how-to-get-course-points) [player guide]
- Previews framed the pacing change as a response to the setting: "where the running of a hospital saw a never-ending stream of patients to treat, universities and education are much more stop-start in their nature," and the year end gives "a chance to stop... take stock... and make adjustments before an influx of new and returning students." — [TheSixthAxis preview](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2022/04/06/two-point-campus-preview-hospital-sequel/); [NME preview](https://www.nme.com/features/two-point-campus-preview-3199326) [critic]
- Campus reception on challenge: PCGamesN (7/10) found "a big difficulty spike" in later levels with "fiendish gameplay twists," but its main criticism was "too much repetitive busywork" because every level opens with the same build order (dorms, bathrooms, lecture theatres) before the unique mechanic appears; objectives such as maintaining happiness and meeting quotas were "very similar" to Hospital's. — [PCGamesN review](https://www.pcgamesn.com/two-point-campus/review) [critic]

Campus sandbox offers four modes including a Challenge option that lets players "tighten all these constraints and make Two Point Campus much tougher." — [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2022/10/two-point-campus-has-been-updated-here-are-the-patch-notes); [GamesRadar cheats/sandbox](https://www.gamesradar.com/two-point-campus-cheats) [official/critic]

### Inferences

- The magnitudes explain the complaint: a death costs 2 reputation points, a cure earns 1, and sending a patient home costs 0.25, so a hospital curing ~50% of a large stream still gains reputation on net, while prices and plot/room value feed income and hospital value independently of outcomes. Hospital value and "earn $X" star goals are cumulative, so time alone satisfies them ("all you need is time").
- Because arrivals are capped by level and reputation, and level is driven by building more, the growth loop is self-reinforcing once cash-positive; the -$300k floor is far below the point where any profitable hospital operates, which is why critics praised the learning curve while forum veterans called the fail state unreachable.
- Campus's year/break cadence and player-chosen intake are the clearest design responses to "the stream never stops and never punishes": pressure arrives in discrete, previewable cohorts, and over-expansion is a decision the player makes with Course Points rather than one reputation makes for them. The studio did not publicly say this was in reaction to Hospital complaints; the previews' "stop-start" framing is the closest statement found.

### Gaps

- No official formula for arrivals-per-day from reputation and hospital level was found; the wiki only states the cap depends on both.
- No developer statement explicitly acknowledging the "money too easy" complaint or describing a patch in response was found; the Hospital patch list contains almost no itemised balance notes.
- The exact per-course student caps and Course Point costs in Campus were not found in a source I could cite.

## Key question 4: How did Theme Hospital (Bullfrog, 1997) handle level goals, reputation, bankruptcy, epidemics and the fax/advisor system, and what did its designers say?

### Takeaway

Theme Hospital gated each of its twelve levels behind a fixed set of simultaneous targets (reputation, bank balance, percentage cured, number cured, hospital value) that rise from "300 reputation, $1,000, 40%, 10 cured, $55,000 value" at Toxicity to "800, $750,000, 70%, 750, $250,000" at Battenberg, with loss conditions on negative funds and too many deaths; events (emergencies, epidemics with a cover-up gamble, VIP visits, earthquakes) and an advisor plus fax machine carried the communication, and Bullfrog added a difficulty setting only in a patch.

### Cited Findings

- Each level sets goals in "financial attainment, hospital reputation, patients cured, and hospital value"; "holding negative funds or allowing sufficient patients to die will bring about losing requirements"; on winning the player may "move on to a new, more elaborate hospital with tougher winning conditions and more diseases present, or stick with their current one"; an advisor "keeps the player informed about what is going on"; epidemics can be covered up "by curing all affected patients before a health inspector turns up," failing which the player is fined and reputation damaged; a later Windows patch "added support for Serial Cable, Modem, and IPX/SPX network gaming, along with a difficulty setting." — [Wikipedia: Theme Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital) [official via secondary]
- Level targets (columns: reputation, bank balance, % cured, patients cured, hospital value): Toxicity 300 / $1,000 / 40 / 10 / $55,000; Sleepy Hollow 300 / $10,000 / 40 / 40 / $60,000; Simpleton 400 / $50,000 / 45 / 200 / $120,000; Manquay N/A / $300,000 / 55 / 400 / $180,000; Croaking 700 / $500,000 / 70 / 600 / $240,000; Battenberg 800 / $750,000 / 70 / 750 / $250,000. The twelve levels are Toxicity, Sleepy Hollow, Largechester, Frimpton-on-Sea, Simpleton, Festering-on-the-Wold, Greenpool, Manquay, Eastville, Eggsenham, Croaking, Battenberg. — [StrategyWiki: Toxicity](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Toxicity), [Sleepy Hollow](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Sleepy_Hollow), [Simpleton](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Simpleton), [Manquay](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Manquay), [Croaking (via search)](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Croaking), [Battenberg](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Battenberg), [Walkthrough index](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Walkthrough) [wiki]
- Battenberg pacing: "During the first two years, there aren't enough patients to fund aggressive growth. After that time there will be a sudden increase on the amount of patients. - Emergencies: "a blue emergency light might pop up on the fax machine bar"; the fax says how many people have which condition and whether you have the resources; you may decline; sending emergency patients home "drops your reputation as if you would have killed them"; "there are usually big cash bonuses on offer." Epidemics: moving infected patients to the front of queues; sending any infected patient home summons the health inspector immediately; infected patients walking outdoors between buildings also triggers inspection; "Nine times out of ten you will get away with it and get a cash bonus" if you send them all home. Earthquakes pre-announce with a tremor and add wear to machines. — [StrategyWiki: Events](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Theme_Hospital/Events) [wiki]
- A player review summarised the epidemic choice as paying "all the money you've made in the past 20 minutes" or racing to vaccinate and cure everyone. — [MobyGames user review](https://mobygames.com/game/674/theme-hospital/user-review/2443031) [player]

The author notes the hospital "must turn a profit," that "epidemics and disasters become more frequent as you progress and require constant manual attention," that it is "deceptively complex," and that the campaign is repetitive, "each scenario repeat[ing] the previous one with more diseases, requirements, and pressure"; it sold close to 1 million copies. — [The Digital Antiquarian](https://www.filfre.net/?p=6445) [history]
- Webley: "It's not about how a hospital runs," it is "about how people think [it] runs." — [The Digital Antiquarian](https://www.filfre.net/?p=6445) [official via secondary]

### Inferences

- Theme Hospital's fairness model is a hard, multi-criteria gate per level with an explicit death-count loss, the opposite of Two Point's soft one-star gate; the Two Point founders' later "don't punish" stance reads as a direct reaction to their own 1997 design and to the repetitiveness critics flagged.
- The per-level target columns show the intended difficulty ramp: cure-percentage requirements rise from 40% to 70%, reputation targets from 300 to 800 (out of 1000), and the final level deliberately starves early patient flow then floods it, which is the "sudden pressure spike" players remembered.

### Gaps

- I could not access the Retro Gamer making-of or the podcast audio, so there are no first-hand designer quotes about Theme Hospital's difficulty tuning, the fax system, or the 3-criteria structure. The theme-hospital.fandom.com wiki does not exist (the URL returns "This wiki does not exist").
- Targets for Largechester, Frimpton, Festering, Greenpool, Eastville and Eggsenham were not fetched (StrategyWiki stubs likely contain them).

## Key question 5: What do curated wikis and CorsixTH document about the numeric rules (reputation per cure/death/sent home, spawn rates, queue limits, star requirements)?

### Takeaway

CorsixTH writes Theme Hospital's rules down explicitly: reputation is a 0-1000 value whose per-event changes (+1 cure, -4 death, -3 sent home/fired, +15/-20 emergency, -50 room crash) are probabilistically dampened as reputation nears either extreme; patient spawn count per month comes from a per-level population table, scheduled randomly across the month with at least one forced arrival; win/lose conditions are six criteria per level file; epidemics fine max($2,000, $2,000 per infected) with reputation loss of fine/100; and queues default to a maximum of 6 (adjustable to 30). Two Point Hospital's wiki gives the analogous small deltas (+1 / -1 / -2 / -0.25 / -0.5) and the Hogsport-to-Croquembouche star thresholds listed in Q3.

### Cited Findings

**CorsixTH reputation model (`hospital.lua`)**

- Reputation is clamped to `reputation_min = 0`, `reputation_max = 1000`, initialised from the level's `StartRep`. — [CorsixTH hospital.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/hospital.lua) [CorsixTH]
- The change table, flagged "TODO: This should depend on difficulty and level": `cured +1`, `death -4`, `kicked -3` ("firing a staff member OR sending a patient home"), `emergency_success +15`, `emergency_failed -20`, `over_priced -2`, `under_priced +1`, `room_crash -50`; an autopsy being discovered costs `AutopsyRepHitPercent` of current reputation (default -70 if unset). Each disease also keeps its own casebook reputation that moves by the same amount. — [CorsixTH hospital.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/hospital.lua) [CorsixTH]
- Dampening: "Under 500, a gain is always effective. Over 500, a loss is always effective"; otherwise the change applies with a quadratic likelihood that is 100% at 500, "over 80%" between 380-720, "under 40%" at 100 or 900, and 0% at 0 or 1000. — [CorsixTH hospital.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/hospital.lua) [CorsixTH]
- Treatment charge scales with reputation: when reputation >= 500 the price is `ceil(price * reputation/500 * percentage)`; it "should never be less than the starting price if reputation falls below 500." — [CorsixTH hospital.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/hospital.lua) [CorsixTH]
- Price fairness (CorsixTH's model): a patient's expected price level = 0.1 x happiness + 0.6 x (reputation/1000) + 0.3 x (cure effectiveness/100); the gap to the chosen price multiplier is the "price distortion," which reduces happiness by distortion/2; when beyond the under/over-priced thresholds there is a 1-in-100 chance per bill of the adviser warning and a reputation change (+1 under, -2 over); an over-charged patient may leave without paying (happiness -0.5, adviser: "A patient left without paying for %s because it's too expensive!"). Adviser copy: "You're charging too little for %s... you won't make a lot of profit"; "Your charges for %s are high. This will make big profits in the short-term, but ultimately you'll start to drive people away." — [CorsixTH patient.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/entities/humanoids/patient.lua); [hospital.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/hospital.lua); [english.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/languages/english.lua) [CorsixTH; these strings are CorsixTH additions, not original Bullfrog text]
- Emergencies: level files define `#emergency_control[n].StartMonth.EndMonth.Min.Max.Illness.PercWin.Bonus` (template example: months 4-5, 4-6 victims, illness 16, 75% required, $400 bonus per patient); success pays `bonus x rescued` and applies the +15/-20 reputation change; uncured victims at the deadline are set to dying. — [CorsixTH example.level](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Levels/example.level); [hospital.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/hospital.lua) [CorsixTH]
- Epidemics: declaring costs a fine of `max(2000, infected x EpidemicFine)` (default $2,000 per infected) and a reputation hit of `fine/100`; a failed cover-up applies a fine plus reputation loss when infected-and-uncured count reaches `EpidemicRepLossMinimum` (default 5); patients going home or dying before the outbreak is revealed can end the cover-up. — [CorsixTH epidemic.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/epidemic.lua) [CorsixTH]. CorsixTH 0.40 added epidemics ("pay a fine to the authorities or try to hide the outbreak by vaccinating and curing"); 0.60 made spawn rate "take into account your hospital's reputation." — [GamingOnLinux 0.40](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2014/12/corsixth-040-the-open-source-theme-hospital-engine-released/); [GamingOnLinux 0.60](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2016/06/corsixth-the-open-source-theme-hospital-engine-has-a-major-release) [CorsixTH]
- Spawning: `spawn_rate` starts from the level's `popn[0].Change` and increases each month by the population table's `Change` value; each month `ceil(spawn_rate)` arrivals are scheduled across the days at random intervals, and "Give the poor hospital a patient this month anyway" forces at least one arrival; a debug cheat adds a flat +40. Patients only spawn once a reception desk is staffed. — [CorsixTH world.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/world.lua) [CorsixTH]
- Queues: room queues default to `max_size = 6`, adjustable down to 0 and up to 30. — [CorsixTH queue.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/queue.lua) [CorsixTH]
- Patient health: a patient's health is sampled daily into a 20-entry history (the in-game health chart); below a near-death threshold the patient is set to dying; waiting lowers happiness by 0.0002 per check ("waiting too long will make you sad"); only patients below 0.8 health can start a vomit chain. — [CorsixTH patient.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/entities/humanoids/patient.lua) [CorsixTH]
- Win/lose criteria: level files list up to six `win_criteria` and six `lose_criteria`, each `Criteria.MaxMin.Value.Group.Bound`; criteria numbers map to 1 reputation, 2 balance, 3 percentage cured, 4 number cured, 5 percentage killed, 6 hospital value (CorsixTH adds staff happiness, patient happiness and months played). Lose groups fire when all criteria in a group are breached; the `Bound` is the warning threshold used by the progress report. CorsixTH's demo level: start cash $60,000; win at reputation >= 700, balance >= $100,000, 75% cured, value >= $70,000; lose if reputation < 300 (warning bound 350) or percentage killed > 40. A player with an outstanding loan cannot win. — [CorsixTH endconditions.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/endconditions.lua); [demo.level](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Levels/demo.level) [CorsixTH]
- Other level-file tuning: `#town.StartCash.InterestRate 100000 300` (template), `#gbv.SodaPrice 20`, `#gbv.StartRating 100` (new drug effectiveness), trophies for 200 cans of soda ($10,000) and reputation above 850 ($20,000); staff pay rises per month are clamped between 50 and 300 and scale with `(reputation - 500) / (deaths + 1)`. Adviser strings include "Kill another %d patients to lose the level!" and, for emergencies, "If you are successful your reputation will increase but if you fail your reputation will be seriously dented." — [CorsixTH example.level](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Levels/example.level); [hospital.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/hospital.lua); [english.lua](https://github.com/CorsixTH/CorsixTH/blob/master/CorsixTH/Lua/languages/english.lua) [CorsixTH]

### Inferences

- Both games treat a death as worth roughly 2-4 cures of reputation, so death is a nudge rather than a cliff; what made Theme Hospital feel stricter is the explicit percentage-killed lose criterion and the per-level reputation floor, both absent from Two Point Hospital's career mode.
- CorsixTH's dampening near 0 and 1000 explains why late Theme Hospital targets (700-800 reputation) feel asymptotic: each cure's +1 lands only ~40-80% of the time above 720.

### Gaps

- CorsixTH's constants (+1/-4/-3, 15/-20, -50) are the clone's, not confirmed against Bullfrog's binary; no disassembly-level source for the original's reputation deltas was found.
- The original per-level `#popn` arrival tables and `StartRep` values were not retrieved (CorsixTH's `original05.level` etc. are partial overrides and the full tables live in the game's own data files).
- Two Point Hospital's per-illness death-on-failure probabilities, baseline cure chances, and the arrival-rate formula were not found in any citable source.
