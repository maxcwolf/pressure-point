# Failure states, money and demand in management, tycoon and colony sims

Scope note: numbers below are tied to a named game and version where the source gives one. Several official/fan wikis (Fandom hosts for Prison Architect, Frostpunk and Banished; Frontier's Planet Coaster news page; Paradox forum patch notes) blocked fetching, so some findings rest on search-result excerpts of those pages or on secondary mirrors; those are flagged. Where I could not find a primary source for a widely repeated claim, it is listed under Gaps rather than asserted.

## What cushion before game-over do successful sims give, relative to building costs and running costs?

### Takeaway

The pattern across the genre is a two-condition trigger (negative balance AND negative cashflow, or a meter pinned at an extreme) followed by a short, explicit grace window and a one-shot bailout, rather than instant death at zero. Several of the biggest sims (RCT, Planet Coaster, Cities: Skylines, Oxygen Not Included, Dwarf Fortress, RimWorld) have no money game-over at all; failure is scenario deadline, forced closure, or the colony physically dying.

### Cited Findings

- Prison Architect (sandbox, Alpha 20 onward): bankruptcy requires BOTH a negative bank balance and negative cash-flow; the player then gets 24 in-game hours to put a financial rescue package in place before being sacked; a Government Bailout is available once per game. Riot failure activates after a riot lasts six in-game hours, with six more hours to stop it. Campaign mode cannot be failed; failure conditions apply only in sandbox. — [Prison Architect Paradox wiki, Failure Conditions](https://prisonarchitect.paradoxwikis.com/Failure_Conditions); the same bankruptcy definition and 24-hour window appear in the Alpha 20 changelog summary — [Prison Architect Fandom wiki, Alpha 20](https://prison-architect.fandom.com/wiki/Alpha_20) (search excerpt; page blocked fetching)
- Prison Architect: the Alpha 20 fail states were added only after the previous alpha reworked finances "so funds flow better"; failure conditions were OFF for all pre-existing prisons and only applied to new ones. — [GameWatcher, 29 Apr 2014](https://www.gamewatcher.com/2014-29-04-prison-architect-alpha-20-introduces-failure-grading-and-player-jail-time); [Prison Architect Fandom wiki, Alpha 20](https://prison-architect.fandom.com/wiki/Alpha_20)
- Prison Architect: the "Legal Defense" research lets the Lawyer avert Game Over once per game, in addition to the one-per-game Government Bailout. — [Prison Architect Paradox wiki, Failure Conditions](https://prisonarchitect.paradoxwikis.com/Failure_Conditions)
- Prison Architect 2 (Paradox, 2024): players who don't want the Bankruptcy Failure Condition can turn it and other conditions off in Sandbox Mode. — [Paradox, PA2 Feature Highlight: Finances](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/prison-architect-2/news/feature-highlight-finances)
- Cities: Skylines (v1.17 wiki state): there is no bankruptcy or game-over for negative money. When money falls below -₡10,000 a one-time bailout of ₡50,000 is offered with no interest or repayment; accepting it disables achievements for that save; only one bailout per save. Three loans gated by milestones: ₡20,000 at 5% over 52 weeks (Little Hamlet), ₡60,000 at 10% over 260 weeks (Worthy Village), ₡200,000 at 15% over 520 weeks (Grand City); max three at once, repayable instantly with no penalty. — [Cities: Skylines Paradox wiki, Economy](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Economy)
- Cities: Skylines II: borrowing limits scale with milestone from ₡100,000 up to ₡26,100,000; City Hall cuts the interest rate by 1% and Central Bank by another 2%. — [MagicGameWorld, CS2 taxes and loans](https://www.magicgameworld.com/cities-skylines-2-understanding-taxes-and-loans/) (secondary guide)
- Planet Coaster (2016): per the community wiki, "losing too much money can lead to the park being forced closed"; Career mode's goal is to stay open while giving guests good value. A player on the "Cavernous Coasters" scenario reported that the 600-guest cap could not support more than a few rides, so they kept ending in the red. Another reported sitting in negative balance for months because of loan interest, unable to build until positive. — [Coasterpedia, Planet Coaster](https://coasterpedia.net/wiki/Planet_Coaster); [Steam thread (Cavernous Coasters)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/493340/discussions/0/152392549357205409/?ctp=2); [Steam thread (negative balance)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/493340/discussions/0/1649919326312127374)
- RollerCoaster Tycoon (1999) and RCT2: scenarios fail by missing the objective at a deadline, not by money. "Both tasks must be done by the end of a set year to win." The final score is company value = park value minus loan, and "it can even be a negative value and you will still win as long as the objective is met." RCT2's Gravity Gardens requires $500,000 park value AND repaying the loan. — [RCT Fandom, General Scenario Guide](https://rct.fandom.com/wiki/General_Scenario_Guide) and [Hints and Tips](https://rct.fandom.com/wiki/Hints_and_Tips) (search excerpts)
- RCT community rule of thumb: in scenarios with loan interest of 10% or higher, repay quickly; "if it's above $120 per month, then it's wiser to repay the loan quickly." RCT3 example: one scenario loan at 10.9%, "£1,000 loan = £109 per month." — [RCT Fandom, Hints and Tips](https://rct.fandom.com/wiki/Hints_and_Tips); [speedrun.com RCT3 guide](https://www.speedrun.com/sv-SE/rollercoaster_tycoon_3/guides/v6kt7)
- Game Dev Tycoon (Switch review): the game ends after a third bankruptcy, and going bankrupt is "surprisingly easy to do." A PocketGamer guide says the company goes bankrupt at about -$50,000 and that a bank loan is an option. These two sources describe different versions and do not agree on the trigger. — [Pure Nintendo review](https://purenintendo.com/review-game-dev-tycoon-nintendo-switch/); [PocketGamer tips](https://www.pocketgamer.com/game-dev-tycoon/game-dev-tycoon-tips-and-tricks-everything-you-need-to-make-a-hit-game/)
- Game Dev Tycoon: on a Steam "went bankrupt" thread, Greenheart Games replied only by linking their forum tips for players who struggle in level 2; the common cause reported was moving to the apartment and hiring staff before building capital; players advised saving ~3 million before moving on. — [Steam thread, Game Dev Tycoon](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/864977026045070690)
- Dwarf Fortress: "no win condition"; "Losing is fun!" is "the official motto that we have in our help manual" (Tarn Adams). — [Software Engineering Daily interview](https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/2015/10/22/dwarf-fortress-with-tarn-adams); [technical.ly on Adams' 2014 TWO5SIX talk](https://technical.ly/uncategorized/dwarf-fortress-adams/)
- RimWorld: raid points have a floor of 35 and a cap of 10,000; the Peaceful preset uses a 0.10 threat scale, Community builder 0.30, Adventure story 0.60, Strive to survive 1.00, Blood and dust 1.55, Losing is fun 2.20, Custom 0-500%. — [RimWorld Wiki, Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points) (page flagged "Outdated?" by the wiki)
- Banished: the food requirement is roughly 100 food per person per year; a fully staffed Gatherer Hut yields over 2,000 food a year (community figures). — [Steam Banished thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/242920/discussions/0/3122661000703918925)

### Inferences

- The "two-condition" bankruptcy (negative balance AND negative cashflow) is a deliberate anti-false-positive: a one-off purchase that dips the balance negative while income is positive never triggers failure. Prison Architect and Cities: Skylines both frame the cushion as a fixed cash number plus a fixed time window rather than as a multiple of running costs, so the cushion shrinks in relative terms as the prison or city grows.
- Where a sim has no money game-over (RCT, Planet Coaster, Cities: Skylines), designers substitute a softer penalty: the park closes, building is blocked while in the red, loan interest compounds, or achievements are disabled by the bailout. The "cost" of failure is lost time and options, not a game-over screen.
- Relative to building costs: Cities: Skylines' bailout (₡50,000) is on the order of 2.5x the smallest loan and a fraction of the largest, i.e. enough to restart cashflow repairs but not to fund expansion. I could not find comparable official ratios for other games.

### Gaps

- Exact Prison Architect bailout amount and the dollar thresholds behind "negative balance" were not stated on the wiki page; the Introversion Alpha 20 changelog (forums.introversion.co.uk) was not reachable.
- Game Dev Tycoon's actual bankruptcy rule (grace period in days or months, negative threshold) could not be confirmed from an official source; a Notion support page describing "90 consecutive days negative" refers to a different game ("Dev Tycoon").
- RCT1/RCT2 per-scenario maximum loan and interest formulas: no wiki page was returned; OpenRCT2 source would be the authoritative reference.
- SimCity 4: no reliable source on what happens at negative funds (bonds vs. forced bankruptcy); only a SimCity 2000 forum anecdote was found.
- Planet Coaster's exact "forced closure" rule (how negative, for how long) is undocumented in the sources found.

## Which games warn before a loss, how far ahead, and in what form?

### Takeaway

The clearest warning designs give a concrete, countable condition and a countdown: Prison Architect's CEO phone call at midnight with an explicit "five more tomorrow means game over," Frostpunk's multi-day ultimatum to raise Hope or lower Discontent, and Cities: Skylines' bailout offer at -₡10,000. Games criticised as unfair are those where the cause is invisible (OpenRCT2 guest stagnation, Frostpunk's scripted Winterhome drops).

### Cited Findings

- Prison Architect: deaths failure triggers at 20 deaths in one day; the CEO calls at midnight warning that five more deaths the next day means game over. Escapes work identically (20 in a day, call at midnight, five more next day). Warden deaths: after the CEO call about hiring difficulty, one more warden death fires you. Wrongful executions: warned after the first two, fired on the third. Death and escape counters are shown in the interface and "a game over countdown starts when you approach failure." — [Prison Architect Paradox wiki, Failure Conditions](https://prisonarchitect.paradoxwikis.com/Failure_Conditions)
- Prison Architect bankruptcy: 24 hours' notice to arrange a rescue package. Riot: failure activates after 6 hours of riot, then 6 more hours to stop it. — [Prison Architect Paradox wiki, Failure Conditions](https://prisonarchitect.paradoxwikis.com/Failure_Conditions)
- Frostpunk (2018): "If hope is too low, or discontent is too high for an extended period of time, the citizens of New London will revolt against the player and the game will also end." — [Wikipedia, Frostpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostpunk)
- Frostpunk: the fail state is banishment, not city collapse: "the workers banish you to the wasteland outside the city's borders." — [Slant Magazine review](https://www.slantmagazine.com/games/review/frostpunk)
- Frostpunk: "Citizens might leave town if you fail them, but first they'll spend days trying to convince others to join them in mass exodus" (i.e. a visible multi-day run-up). — [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/frostpunk-review)
- Frostpunk: "if discontent gets too high, or hope too low, you may be notified you only have a few days to reverse the trend by accomplishing a specific goal." — [RPG Site review](https://rpgsite.net/review/7104-frostpunk-review)
- Frostpunk: a player reported receiving an ultimatum to raise Hope a day before the Winterhome event with the bar stuck near 15%; an itch.io commenter says the real game gives a 2-day ultimatum (anecdotal, not official). — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1696045708664262360); [itch.io comment on Frostpunk Pico](https://8192games.itch.io/frostpunk-pico)
- Frostpunk: "Most discontent related events start as problems, but if you ignore them for long enough they will escalate to protests (if you have high discontent) and riots (if you have low hope)." — Frostpunk Fandom wiki (search excerpt via [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1696045708646208587)); Fandom page blocked fetching
- Cities: Skylines: the bailout offer itself is the warning, triggered at -₡10,000; residents and businesses "protest if taxes stay above roughly 13-14% too long" (a lagging, visible signal). — [Cities: Skylines Paradox wiki, Economy](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Economy)
- Cities: Skylines (debt recovery): high taxes make citizens unhappy and leave, but "this negative effect doesn't happen instantly," giving players a window to use temporary tax hikes. — [Guidestrats, get out of debt](https://guidestrats.com/cities-skylines-get-out-of-debt/)
- RCT/OpenRCT2: guest thoughts and the park rating are the diagnostic layer. In a 2023 complaint about stagnating guest numbers (OpenRCT2 v0.4.3: 855 guests at 21 rides falling to 745 at 26 rides with rating 999 and a soft cap of 1674), other players traced it to the dominant guest thought "I'm running out of cash" with no cash machines, so guests left faster than they spawned; a code comparison against v0.1.0 found no meaningful generation changes. — [OpenRCT2 forums, topic 6179](https://forums.openrct2.org/topic/6179-guest-generation-algorithm-changed-cant-finish-scenario-without-advertising-anymore/)
- RCT/OpenRCT2 park rating: lost guests are a hidden-ish cause: the first 25 lost peeps trying to leave cost nothing, each beyond 25 costs 7 rating points with no cap; litter costs 4 points each up to 600; each drowning 25 up to 1000; each vehicle crash with peeps adds 200 to a casualty penalty capped at 500 that fades over time. — [OpenRCT2 forums, park rating guide (v0.1.2)](https://forums.openrct2.org/topic/2798-guide-how-to-calculate-park-rating/)
- Cities: Skylines II: before Economy 2.0, Paradox warned existing saves would see "a negative money trend initially" and a "death wave" of elderly citizens lasting about 1-2 in-game months, and published a save-game checklist recommending tax increases, lower service budgets, temporarily turning off buildings, or enabling Unlimited Money if stuck. — [Paradox, Economy patch is here](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/news/economy-patch-is-here); [Paradox save-game checklist](https://paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/news/save-game-checklist)

### Inferences

- The best-regarded warnings pair a number the player can count (deaths today, escapes today, cash balance) with a stated consequence and a fixed clock. Frostpunk's warnings are narrative ("people are gathering to leave") and take days, which players accept in normal play but reject when an event drops Hope by script regardless of preparation.
- RCT's warnings are indirect (guest thoughts, rating components) and demand the player diagnose; the OpenRCT2 thread shows that even expert players read stagnation as a bug when the cause (guest cash) is not surfaced as a single metric.

### Gaps

- The official Frostpunk day-count for the Hope/Discontent ultimatum (reported as 2 days by players) could not be confirmed; the Fandom "Hope and Discontent" page returned HTTP 402.
- Whether RCT1/RCT2 shows a "park rating too low" warning and closes the park after N weeks under 700 (a rule I recall but could not source): the search found only the Six Flags Great Adventure objective "park rating must be kept over 700" and no documented warning period.
- Banished has no explicit loss warning in any source found; failure is emergent starvation/freezing.

## How do they keep late-game money meaningful?

### Takeaway

The main levers found are: (1) upkeep that scales with the thing you built (Cities: Skylines II tile upkeep, service budgets with diminishing returns), (2) threat that scales with wealth so money has a cost (RimWorld, with a cap and an opt-out), (3) soft caps on demand so more building does not mean proportionally more income (RCT soft guest cap, SimCity 4 demand caps, CS commercial ratios), and (4) milestone-gated debt with real interest.

### Cited Findings

- RimWorld raid points = (Wealth Points + Pawn Points) x Difficulty x Starting Factor x Adaptation Factor. Storyteller Wealth counts items and creatures at 100% and buildings at 50%. Wealth Points: 0 up to 14,000 wealth, 2,400 at 400,000, 3,600 at 700,000, 4,200 at 1,000,000 and no further gain. Pawn Points per colonist: 15 at <=10k wealth, 140 at 400k, 200 at 1M. Starting Factor 0.7 for days 0-10, 1.0 from day 40. — [RimWorld Wiki, Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- RimWorld adaptation (rubber band): factor starts at 0.8; AdaptDays cannot rise above 0 for the first 30 days; a colonist death removes 30 AdaptDays at low population (20 at pop 20), a downing removes 8 at pop 1 down to 3.5 at pop 20; AdaptDays clamp -60..100 giving a factor range 0.4-1.47. "Losing is fun" uses Adaption Impact 40% (effective range 0.75-1.19) and maximum growth rate. — [RimWorld Wiki, Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- RimWorld "Wealth Independent" custom mode replaces Storyteller Wealth with a timer: 10,000 at day 0, 180,000 at day 180, 1,000,000 at day 720 (default 12 years to maximum threat; slider adjustable). Version notes: 1.2.2719 ignored building hit points for wealth; 1.3.3287 slaves count 0.75x; 1.4.3555 friendly mechanoids count toward pawn points. — [RimWorld Wiki, Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- RimWorld, Tynan Sylvester (2016 Ludeon forum post, quoted on Steam): the threat algorithm "takes into account wealth, population, defensive strength, and several factors related to the time since the colony has taken damage, had people downed, or been at given population counts," and he advised players not to "spend a bunch of time trying to cheese wealth lower." — [Steam discussion quoting Ludeon forum](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/360671583800707840) (search excerpt)
- RimWorld player criticism (Nov 2021): "Scaling is based essentially entirely on wealth multiplied by difficulty"; sustained success without losses leads to "swarms of centipedes"; one player: the storyteller aspect has been lost to "escalating and often unrealistic combat focus." — [Steam thread, Nov 2021](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/3160958175962150146)
- Cities: Skylines service budgets run 50%-150% with a quadratic curve: service level = (B/10)^2 below 100% and 3B - (B/10)^2 - 100 above; 50% budget gives 25% effect, 150% gives 125%, and the last 5 points add only about 0.2% (diminishing returns as a sink). Tax income "falls as total cash grows." — [Cities: Skylines Paradox wiki, Economy](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Economy)
- Cities: Skylines: the High Interest Tower unique building requires at least ₡2,000 in loan debt plus the Big Town milestone and is a prerequisite for the Medical Center monument (debt deliberately rewarded). — [Guidestrats, High Interest Tower](https://guidestrats.com/cities-skylines-high-interest-tower/)
- Cities: Skylines II, patch 1.1.5f1 "Economy 2.0" (June 2024): removed government subsidies "to make the economy more challenging and transparent"; added a Tile Upkeep cost for owned land ("we have added a Tile Upkeep cost since the dev diaries"), which hits hardest for cities that unlocked most of the map early; companies need time to adjust production and employee numbers. — [Paradox, Economy patch is here](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/news/economy-patch-is-here); [Automaton Media on the subsidy removal](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/citiesskylines2-20240625-299054-001/); [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/?p=543785)
- SimCity 4: demand caps are per city tile while demand is shared across connected region tiles; cap relief comes from parks, plazas, airports, seaports, rewards and landmarks; there is no in-game readout of current caps (players use a mod); demolishing 50,000 population "resulted in... no change in RCI Demand." — [Steam SimCity 4 Deluxe discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/24780/discussions/0/496880503057331341); [AnandTech thread on city stagnation](https://forums.anandtech.com/threads/simcity4-deluxe-city-stagnation.1951686)
- RCT/OpenRCT2 soft cap: each ride adds a rideBonusValue (giga coaster 120, corkscrew 100) to a "suggested guest maximum"; once guests heading to plus inside the park exceed it, spawn probability is divided by 4; in "difficult guest generation" scenarios the ride bonus total is capped at 1,000, rides over 600 m with excitement above 6.00 count three times, the over-cap penalty is /16, and no guests spawn at all at suggested maximum + 150. — [OpenRCT2 forums, guest spawn guide (source as of 12 Mar 2018)](https://forums.openrct2.org/topic/2861-guide-when-and-how-do-guests-spawn/)
- Prison Architect income: an early (2015) Steam thread reports a fixed $150/day grant per prisoner regardless of security level, with intake grants of $500 (Medium) and $1,000 (Max). Prison Architect 2: each inmate yields a daily payment plus an intake fee, Super Max paying about five times Minimum Security; intake money on arrival, daily payments at midnight. — [Steam PA thread (2015)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/233450/discussions/0/864979375699002965); [Paradox PA2 Feature Highlight: Finances](https://paradoxinteractive.com/games/prison-architect-2/news/feature-highlight-finances)

### Inferences

- RimWorld is the only one of these games that makes money itself dangerous; its designers hedged this with a hard cap (1M wealth / 10,000 points), a 30-day no-adaptation grace, buildings at half weight (1.2 era), and finally an opt-out timer mode, each a response to the "wealth tax" criticism.
- Cities: Skylines II's trajectory (launch economy with subsidies that hid losses -> Economy 2.0 removing them and adding land upkeep) is the clearest recent example of designers choosing transparency plus scaling upkeep over cushions.
- Soft demand caps (RCT ride bonus, SimCity 4 tile caps, CS commercial ratio) are what keep "build more rooms, get more customers" from becoming linear; the pain point in every case is that the cap is invisible in the UI.

### Gaps

- Planet Coaster 1/2 late-game sinks (staff wage growth, ride ageing, maintenance scaling) were not documented in reachable sources; Frontier's "Deep Dive: Mastering Management" returned 403.
- Frostpunk has no money; its late-game sink is coal demand under falling temperature, but no numeric temperature/coal table was retrieved.
- Oxygen Not Included: no designer statement on failure or late-game sinks found; the Game Developer interview with Johann Seidenz (2017) covers tutorials and Early Access process only. — [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/behind-the-design-of-hit-sim-game-i-oxygen-not-included-i-)

## What counterplay exists for seasonal or surge demand?

### Takeaway

Surges are made fair by telegraphing them and by giving stockpile or throughput tools: Frostpunk announces the storm and expects coal/steam-core depots and automatons; RCT lets players throttle arrivals with pricing and campaigns and absorb crowds with path width and more rides; Banished lets the player refuse nomad waves outright. Where surges are scripted and untelegraphed (Frostpunk Winterhome Hope drop) players call them automatic defeats.

### Cited Findings

- Frostpunk: "after a certain point... your city learns of a massive storm headed your way"; guides recommend Resource Depots stocked with coal, stockpiled Steam Cores, and automatons on coal mines and wall drills so fewer people are exposed during the storm. — [Prima Games, How to Survive the Storm](https://primagames.com/tips/frostpunk-how-survive-storm)
- Frostpunk Winterhome event (A New Home, around day 14): a player: "the Winterhome event is too punishing... an automatic defeat"; a reply states the design intent: the event exists to put you at low hope, after which you must use Order or Faith to recover; another player recommends getting hope buildings (fighting arena, public house) "BEFORE triggering winterhold on day 14." — [Steam thread 1696045708646208587](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1696045708646208587); [Steam thread 1698300679779054804](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1698300679779054804)
- Frostpunk, Fall of Winterhome scenario: hope loss comes mainly from deaths ("you need to stop deaths early"); counterplay listed by players: House of Prayer with shrines, law enforcement to disperse crowds, churches only help residences in range, reduce heating hubs as population falls. — [Steam thread 1751276551822867346](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1751276551822867346)
- RCT/OpenRCT2 arrival throttles: a park entrance fee "too high compared with the rides' total value for money" divides spawn probability by 4, and again by 4 if far too high (about 6% left). More than 7,000 guests in the park cuts probability by 75%. Each positive award multiplies probability by 1.25, each negative by 0.75. Marketing campaigns add a separate spawn probability that bypasses the cap checks: free park entry +400, free ride entry +300, half-price park entry +200, free food/drink +200, park advertising +250, ride advertising +200; all at once = 1,450, about one guest per second; price-cutting campaigns are divided by 8 if the price is already very low. — [OpenRCT2 forums, guest spawn guide](https://forums.openrct2.org/topic/2861-guide-when-and-how-do-guests-spawn/)
- RCT/OpenRCT2 crowd absorption: on a stagnating park, players recommended widening single-tile paths, adding land and attractions, cash machines, and lower prices. — [OpenRCT2 forums, topic 6179](https://forums.openrct2.org/topic/6179-guest-generation-algorithm-changed-cant-finish-scenario-without-advertising-anymore/)
- Banished: nomads arrive at random times in groups of 10-30% of current population; the player can accept or deny; "if you reject a group, the next one tends to be smaller"; a Boarding House keeps nomads warm while homes are built; nomads may carry disease, so a hospital (minimum a herbalist) is recommended. — [Banished wiki, Town Hall](https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Town_Hall); [Banished wiki, Nomad](https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Nomad); [Gamepressure Banished guide, Nomads](https://www.gamepressure.com/banished/nomads/z36085)
- Banished starvation is usually distribution, not production: with large stockpiles people still starve when food does not reach houses in time; keep 2-3 labourers to move goods; "2,000 food for 35 people is actually low." — [Steam Banished thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/242920/discussions/0/3122661000703918925)
- Cities: Skylines II death wave: a one-time surge in deaths over 1-2 in-game months after Economy 2.0; the fix was to spread death ages so "you don't end up with a very large amount of seniors who all die around the same time." — [Paradox, Economy patch is here](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/news/economy-patch-is-here)

### Inferences

- The fair versions of surge all share: advance notice (storm announcement, nomad group waiting at the Town Hall), a pre-commitment tool (stockpile, refuse, price up), and a cost to the counter (campaign spend, lost workers). The unfair version is a scripted meter drop with no pre-commitment lever.
- RCT's design lets the player both pull guests in (campaigns, awards) and push them away (entrance fee), which is why its surges rarely feel like a death spiral: the throttle is in the player's hands.

### Gaps

- Cities: Skylines tourism surge mechanics (how tourist numbers scale with attractiveness/hotels) were not retrieved from an official source.
- Exact Frostpunk storm temperatures (-150 C is often quoted) and coal consumption per generator level were not confirmed from a reachable wiki page.
- Planet Coaster guest-wave behaviour at scenario start (initial surge then settle) was not documented in reachable sources.

## How do arrivals follow rating, capacity and advertising (RCT, Planet Coaster), and intake/immigration follow what the player built (Prison Architect, Banished)?

### Takeaway

RCT combines reputation (park rating) and capacity (ride-based soft cap) with advertising as a bypass; Planet Coaster 2 drives arrivals from a Park Rating whose weakest of four components dominates; Prison Architect intake is player-set per security level with cash per head; Banished immigration scales with current population and is accept/refuse.

### Cited Findings

- RCT/OpenRCT2 spawn probability (park.cpp, park_calculate_guest_generation_probability, source as of 12 Mar 2018): base 50 + clamp(park rating - 200, 0, 650), so 50 at rating <=200 up to 700 at rating >=850, on a 0-65535 random draw per tick ("a value near 1,500 spawns roughly one guest per second"). Then /4 if guests heading to + inside > suggested maximum; /4 for >7,000 guests; /4 or /16 for entrance fee vs. ride value; x1.25 per positive award, x0.75 per negative; sprite limit ~9,600 stops all spawning. Campaigns add a separate probability (see previous section). — [OpenRCT2 forums, guest spawn guide](https://forums.openrct2.org/topic/2861-guide-when-and-how-do-guests-spawn/)
- RCT/OpenRCT2 park rating (v0.1.2 guide): starts at 1150 (1050 with "difficult park rating"); guest count up to 2000 divided by 13 and subtracted from 150 (range -150 to +3, so an empty park loses 150); happiness: subtract 500 then add back 2 x min(300 x share of happy peeps, 250), no loss at ~83%+ happy; ride uptime 0 average costs 200; average excitement/intensity targets ~46/65 (x100/8 scale) with max penalty 100; summed excitement and intensity each need >1000 or up to 200 penalty; clamp 0-999. — [OpenRCT2 forums, park rating guide](https://forums.openrct2.org/topic/2798-guide-how-to-calculate-park-rating/)
- RCT scenario reputation targets: most scenarios ask for a 600 rating by a date (e.g. Dynamite Dunes: 650 guests and 600 rating by 31 Oct Year 3); RCT2 Six Flags Great Adventure: 3,500 guests and rating kept over 700. — [GiantBomb RCT](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/RollerCoaster_Tycoon); [GiantBomb RCT2](https://www.giantbomb.com/rollercoaster-tycoon-2/3030-11178/)
- Planet Coaster 2 (Frontier, 2024): Park Rating is built from Attraction Prestige, Scenery Rating, Unique Attractions and Park Reputation; "your Park Rating will be determined by your lowest performing area"; "Guest Experience feeds into your Park Reputation, which directly affects your Park Rating"; "your Park Rating in turn affects how many guests are attracted to your park." — [Frontier, Deep Dive: Mastering Management](https://www.planetcoaster.com/news/2024-09-25/deep-dive-mastering-management) and [Keeping Guests Happy](https://planetcoaster.com/player-guides/keeping-guests-happy) (search excerpts; page returned 403 on fetch). A third-party guide instead describes an average with the lowest value weighted most. — [Storefrontcloud article](https://msztodel105.europe-west1.gcp.storefrontcloud.io/articles/planet-coaster-2-how-to-increase-park-rating)
- Planet Coaster 1 scenario capacity: a player reports the hardest scenario, Cavernous Coasters, has a 600-guest cap that "couldn't support more than a few rides." — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/493340/discussions/0/152392549357205409/?ctp=2)
- Prison Architect: the Intake tab in Reports lets the player choose how many prisoners arrive per category; guides recommend "only a few extra prisoners" at first because holding cells fill fast. — [Twinfinite guide](https://twinfinite.net/pc/prison-architect-how-to-build-a-good-prison-and-keep-prisoners-happy/)
- Prison Architect cash per prisoner: $150/day flat plus $500 (Med) / $1,000 (Max) intake (2015 figures, may be outdated); PA2: daily payment plus intake fee, Super Max ~5x Minimum. — [Steam PA thread 2015](https://steamcommunity.com/app/233450/discussions/0/864979375699002965); [Paradox PA2 finances](https://paradoxinteractive.com/games/prison-architect-2/news/feature-highlight-finances)
- Banished: nomads require a Town Hall; groups are 10-30% of current population; they arrive uneducated, without tools, in fair or ragged clothing; refusing makes later groups smaller/less willing. — [Banished wiki, Town Hall](https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Town_Hall); [Banished wiki, Nomad](https://banished-wiki.com/wiki/Nomad)
- Cities: Skylines demand (RCI): commercial demand needs one commercial worker per eight citizens for zero demand, plus unused visitor spaces; "Industrial demand is simply a reflection of unemployment"; goods availability does not drive the commercial bar, which "can be misleading"; patch 1.0.6b fixed parks suppressing too much commercial demand; residential demand is defined only as citizens needing more housing, with no formula given. — [Cities: Skylines Paradox wiki, Zoning](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Zoning)
- Cities: Skylines demand vs. taxes: default tax 9% in all zones (1%-29%); higher taxes raise weekly income but reduce demand; players report stagnant RCI at high tax; residential demand described by players as driven by available homes and jobs. — [Cities: Skylines Paradox wiki, Economy](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Economy); [Steam CS discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/2791563692136719710)

### Inferences

- In RCT, building more rides does bring more guests, but only by raising the soft cap and the rating; the per-ride bonus (100-120 for big coasters) sets a capacity ceiling that scales sub-linearly with investment, and campaigns are the only way to exceed it on demand. This is the "capacity-driven with a reputation multiplier" model.
- Planet Coaster 2's min-of-four rule is a deliberate anti-specialisation device: pouring money into one area (prestige rides) cannot compensate for neglect elsewhere, which is the opposite of RCT's additive rating.
- Prison Architect and Banished make intake a player choice, which converts "surge" from a threat into a bet; the failure modes players report (holding cells overflow, nomad plague) are all consequences of accepting more than the built capacity.

### Gaps

- Planet Coaster 1's guest generation formula (how park rating and ride count map to arrivals) is not publicly documented; only PC2 qualitative rules were found.
- Prison Architect current intake defaults (prisoners per day at each security level) were not found on a reachable wiki page.
- Banished nomad frequency is "random" in every source; no interval or trigger was documented.

## How do these games avoid or create death spirals, and what did players criticise and designers change?

### Takeaway

Avoidance tools in the wild are: two-condition triggers plus short grace (Prison Architect), one-shot bailouts (Prison Architect, Cities: Skylines), adaptation that eases threat after losses (RimWorld), caps on scaling (RimWorld 10,000 points; RCT /4 rather than zero), and off-switches (sandbox toggles, Unlimited Money). The criticisms that drove changes were invisible causes and scripted drops; the fixes were transparency and opt-outs rather than easier numbers.

### Cited Findings

- RimWorld's adaptation factor is an explicit negative feedback loop: deaths and downings subtract AdaptDays (30 per death at low pop), lowering the raid multiplier to as low as 0.4, and the factor cannot grow above 0.8 for the first 30 days. — [RimWorld Wiki, Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- RimWorld's "Wealth Independent" mode exists for players who dislike wealth-scaled threat; in it, threat is purely time-based (max at 720 days by default). — [RimWorld Wiki, Raid points](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Raid_points)
- Prison Architect only introduced failure after fixing the economy ("funds flow better"), and grandfathered existing saves out of failure conditions. — [GameWatcher, Alpha 20](https://www.gamewatcher.com/2014-29-04-prison-architect-alpha-20-introduces-failure-grading-and-player-jail-time); [PA Fandom wiki, Alpha 20](https://prison-architect.fandom.com/wiki/Alpha_20)
- Cities: Skylines II players' complaints about Economy 2.0 were about the transition: existing saves faced a negative money trend and a death wave; Paradox's response was a checklist (raise taxes, lower budgets, switch off buildings) and the suggestion to enable Unlimited Money. — [Paradox, save-game checklist](https://paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/news/save-game-checklist); [Yahoo/News on the warning](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/cities-skylines-2-warns-players-143354210.html)
- OpenRCT2 stagnation complaint (2023): the player saw rating 999 and a soft cap far above the guest count and concluded the algorithm was broken; the hidden variable was guest cash. — [OpenRCT2 forums, topic 6179](https://forums.openrct2.org/topic/6179-guest-generation-algorithm-changed-cant-finish-scenario-without-advertising-anymore/)
- Frostpunk Winterhome event: "it really takes away from the game to have an automatic defeat event that will hit no matter what." No source found confirming 11 bit studios changed the event's Hope/Discontent values in a patch. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/1696045708646208587)
- Game Dev Tycoon bankruptcies: the reported spiral is fixed-cost driven (office move + salaries before revenue); the developer's response was a forum tips post rather than a rule change in that thread. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/864977026045070690)
- SimCity 4 stagnation: players describe hitting invisible demand caps and being unable to recover even by demolishing 50,000 population; the community answer is mods to display caps or starting a new tile. — [Steam SC4 discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/24780/discussions/0/496880503057331341); [AnandTech thread](https://forums.anandtech.com/threads/simcity4-deluxe-city-stagnation.1951686)
- Dwarf Fortress / Tarn Adams on embracing loss: "it's important that people learn to embrace loss, or the world can't be enriched by their passing"; watching dwarves die is what makes players value their lives. — [Goodreads author blog quoting Adams e-mail interview](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/4264915-cognitive-dissonance-as-game-mechanic)
- General design framing: positive loops amplify differences and can make outcomes feel predetermined; negative loops dampen them but "can feel unfair if they're too strong"; rubber-banding should be subtle because visible rubber-banding "makes leaders feel cheated and trailers feel patronized." — [Bugnet, feedback loops in game design](https://bugnet.io/blog/positive-and-negative-feedback-loops-in-game-design); [Chemeketa CIS125 reader, Feedback Loops](https://computerscience.chemeketa.edu/cis125greader/MechanicsDynamics/FeedbackLoops.html)

### Inferences

- The recurring complaint is not "too hard" but "I could not see why": OpenRCT2 guest cash, SimCity 4 caps, Frostpunk scripted drops, CS2 subsidies masking losses. Designer responses that stuck were exposure (CS2 removing subsidies "to make the economy more challenging and transparent") and opt-outs (RimWorld wealth-independent mode, PA2 sandbox toggles), not cushions.
- Every grace mechanism found is bounded (24 hours, one bailout, 30 adaptation days, -₡10,000 trigger), which keeps the cushion from becoming a strategy.

### Gaps

- No Chris Sawyer interview on guest generation or failure design was found in searches; the numeric RCT material is from OpenRCT2's reimplementation and dated community guides (2018 source).
- No Frontier postmortem on Planet Coaster's lack of a hard fail state was found.
- No Shining Rock (Banished) design blog statement on nomads or starvation balancing was retrieved.
- No 11 bit studios statement on Hope/Discontent tuning or Winterhome feedback was found.
- Oxygen Not Included and Dwarf Fortress yielded philosophy quotes only; neither has a money system, and no numeric failure thresholds exist to report.
