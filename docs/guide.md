# Player's guide

Everything the [README](../README.md) leaves out: how a center works, step by step, every treatment and what it needs, and the settings, controls and saves. The game's own advisor and tips cover most of this as you play, so there is no need to read it first.

## Campaign and sandbox

**Campaign** takes you through a series of centers, each with its own building, guests, goals and quirks. Earn a center's first star to open the next:

1. **Willow Lane**, a small high-street unit. Massage only: the place to learn the ropes. It opens as spring starts, with the trees in leaf, and its weather is mild all year, never cold or hot enough for anyone to mind; the other centers open in January.
2. **The Exchange**, downtown, on a paved plaza among the office blocks. Office workers with office ailments, arriving in rush hours when the office crowd comes in. Cold winters, the first center that needs Radiators. Acupuncture opens here, and Qi Gong classes at its second star.
3. **The Boardwalk**, a seaside town. Packed in summer (about twice the usual guests) and quiet in winter (a little over half), with seaside ailments, sea air that is hard on equipment, summers hot enough to need Air Conditioners, and cold winters. A fortnight before each summer and winter you are told what is coming, with the guests to expect a month against last month, and each season is announced with its numbers as it starts. Reiki opens here.
4. **High Pines**, a lodge up in the mountains. Cold all year, so keep it heated, and snowed in now and then each winter, when nobody new can get up the road. A light snow falls once or twice a winter too, only to look at. A big freeze can stop the water. Mountain ailments, and Stone Warmers that cool fast in the cold. Sound healing opens here.
5. **The Orchard Estate**, a country resort among the apple orchards. Picky guests pay half as much again but expect beautiful treatment rooms, the health inspector calls twice as often, and each star asks for a higher standing. Cold winters and hot summers, both harsher than the Boardwalk's. Acupuncture, reiki, sound healing and a Shaman are open from the start.

Every campaign star earns **✨ karma**, and so do eleven one-off **achievements**, like seeing an emergency through or healing 100 guests in one center. Three of them are hidden, shown as mystery cards on the campaign's **Achievements** tab until they are earned. Spend karma in the **karma shop** (under Campaign on the title screen) or on a locked card in the Items menu. Each purchase stays unlocked in every center and the sandbox. Karma unlocks comfort and decor items early, before their star comes round, and five you can only get with karma: a **Massage Chair** that keeps a waiting guest patient, a **Topiary**, a **Bonsai**, a **Koi Pond** and a **Fireplace** that warms and soothes the room around it. It also unlocks floors and walls to decorate rooms with, and the **Carousel of Rebirth**, a treatment machine, from its card in the Rooms menu. The whole campaign earns enough karma to unlock every item in the karma shop, but not every floor and wall and the Carousel as well, even with the Foundation's gifts below, so choose.

Once every star in a campaign center is earned, the **Pressure Point Foundation** opens on the Money menu's Books tab: three gifts of $50,000, $100,000 and $200,000 ($75,000, $150,000 and $300,000 at The Orchard Estate, where guests pay more), each worth 1 karma the first time it is made in that center. The card says what each gift would leave in the bank against the next payday, since a gift is gone for good. Gifts go in the books on a line of their own, and the Healthy Books award judges the year without them. A game the cheat console has been used in cannot make them.

**Sandbox** is a big lot of your own, with no deadline. Choose the starting money, the difficulty, which treatments to offer, and whether everything is unlocked from day one. **Relaxed** eases every pressure, **Standard** is the game as the campaign plays it, and **Demanding** turns every one up. Or set each on its own, easier, usual or harder: how often guests arrive, their patience, how often applicants turn up, equipment wear, staff tiredness, salaries, treatment fees, the loan limit, how often trouble comes (mishaps, stress outbreaks and the health inspector), how often emergencies, challenges and house calls are offered, and how fast reputation grows. Point at a setting to see what it does.

## How to play

### Tutorial

Your first Willow Lane game, before the campaign has any stars, starts with the **tutorial**. A card in the advisor's spot at the bottom left gives the step in hand (look around, pause the clock, place a Reception Desk, hire a Receptionist, build a Consultation Room and hire a Wellness Consultant, build a Massage Suite and hire a Massage Therapist, start the clock again, put down two Benches once guests are coming), and a pulsing gold ring marks what to click: the menu's button, then the tab or card in it, then the blueprint's **Build room** once a room is planned. On a hiring step the ring goes round the row of applicants rather than one of them, and the card says how to read theirs: rank, pay a month and a quirk, green where it helps and red where it costs; a Consultant's rank means surer diagnoses, and every Therapist knows Swedish massage. **Show me** opens the menu the step wants. The Reception Desk's card says that everything is paid for from the bank. In a first game the center's next needs come as steps of their own as they start to matter (a Caretaker once guests are being healed, Smudging, a Staff Room as staff tire), ahead of the Benches if those are not down yet; a step the bank alone could not take without dropping under a month's wages says what it would leave, and that the loan is cheaper than the red. Pausing is asked for so that nothing happens while the opening is built, and starting the clock again once the desk, both rooms and all three hires are in; a player who never pauses is not asked to start it. Once all are done the advisor takes over, and the heads-up on land for sale comes as a message at the first star. A **Tutorial** list under the objectives ticks off each step. Nothing is ever blocked: build in any order and the steps tick off as they are done, and a pressing nudge from the advisor (someone about to quit, a guest about to go home worse off) takes the card's place until it is seen to.

The first time some things happen, a **lesson** card opens over the game, paused, with a strip of pictures and a few lines: your first guest walking in, your first heal, the weather first getting indoors with nothing to warm or cool the place, staff tiring, your first payday, the bank first holding less than the next payday's wages, a failed treatment, a guest storming out, and a raise request. Each comes once across the campaign, in whichever center it first happens, and never two in a day. **Show me** takes you to whoever it is about, and **Got it** carries on. The one on the weather says how cold or hot it is outside and in, and has a button that puts a Radiator or an Air Conditioner in hand to place. The one on money says what the bank holds, what payday takes, and that a loan is cheaper than the red, and its **Show me** opens the Money menu's Loan tab. The first star's dialog also says what a star opens and what karma is for.

**Skip the tutorial** on the card turns off the steps and the lessons, in that game and the ones after. Once you have been through the steps, the next Willow Lane game starts without them, and the lessons carry on through the campaign; **Settings** and the switch on Willow Lane's card in the campaign turn the steps back on, for a replay as well.

### Building your center

1. Place a **Reception Desk** and hire a **Receptionist**.
2. Build a **Consultation Room** (drag the floor, click a wall for the door, place the furniture) and hire a **Wellness Consultant**. A room's price covers floor up to one tile more each way than its smallest, 4×4 for most; every tile you drag out past that costs $200 more, as a resize would, and the hint says the price as you drag.
3. Build a **Massage Suite** and hire a **Massage Therapist**. The center opens once all three exist.
4. Guests check in, get diagnosed, get treated, and pay. Keep them patient with benches, tea, snacks, plants and a restroom, and as you earn stars, an arcade, a **Fortune Teller** (now and then it tells a guest they will be healed today, and they wait patiently), a **Kombucha Tap** (it earns more than tea, but once worn it fizzes over into a puddle that slows guests down until a Caretaker mops it up), an aquarium and more. Once you own the **Carousel of Rebirth**, bored guests nearby pay $25 for a ride on it while it treats nobody, and tell everyone about it in their review, and get off at once for a guest due for rebirth.
5. Staff members get tired. A **Staff Room** lets them recover; a **Caretaker** clears litter, cleans the toilets, waters plants and keeps the massage stones hot.
6. Salaries come out at the end of each month. If the bank cannot cover them, borrow the difference from the bank (2% a month) or pay them into the red (5% a month, and the staff worry about their wages: their morale settles lower, and after a month in the red the unhappy ones ask for a raise to stay). Five days before payday you are warned if the wages are not covered, with what the bank will do. The bank judges the business, not the building: a one-off outlay that dips the bank is no trouble while the books are in profit. But a payday that leaves you in the red deeper than the loan still to be had would clear, while, at the pace of the last month or so, the takings do not cover the wages and interest, has the bank call the overdraft in: ten days to get back above zero (sell something, or earn it) or into profit (prices up, or staff down), or the center is bankrupt. Hit the objectives to earn stars. Click an objective to see the numbers behind it: for a goal judged in a month, what counts this month and last, the sample it needs, and how many more heals would reach it; for a balance goal the money, the loan and the gap at the pace of the books; for a prestige goal the room's score terms; for a grade the standing points by source. It says plainly when a goal cannot progress this month. An objective judged **in a month** can be met in this month so far or the last one finished. "Heal 75% of guests treated or sent home in a month" needs at least 10 of them, and a guest sent home untreated counts against the rate like a failed treatment, so culling the hard cases is no way to it. "Heal 12 guests in a month, waiting 6 days or less on average" wants both in the same month: the heals, and the guests who left that month (healed, failed, sent home or stormed out) having waited no longer than that on average, so it is earned by a center that treats its guests promptly, not by one that runs empty. The **Stats** menu charts the waits and the **patience** guests keep month by month.
7. Stars unlock new **massage modalities**. Build a **Training Room** and send therapists on courses to deliver them. Staff earn experience and rise through five **ranks**; each rank brings a qualification slot for a course, and a promotion comes with a raise. An **Expert** can learn every treatment of their discipline, even with every slot filled. Changed your mind? **Cancel course** in their inspector, once you confirm, sends them back to work and refunds the whole fee, with no qualification.
8. A **Posture Lab** runs itself: its Posture Scanner needs nobody to work it, only $250 a month to run and a Caretaker when it wears out. It scans the guests the Consultant was unsure about, in place of a second consultation, and it is the only way to be sure of a **structural condition**: Crick of the Neck, Text Neck, Croquet Back and Wipeout, none of them seen before the first star. A Consultant can see a structural condition is there but gets no surer of it than 60%, so without a working scanner its treatment is a best guess, 5% less likely to work on top of the doubtful diagnosis, and the guest's inspector says so. A scan costs a little more than a consultation ($250 against $200), makes the diagnosis certain and adds 10% to the guest's chance of success, more from an upgraded scanner, and the Chance of success in their inspector counts it.
9. **Decor** (salt lamps, statues, rugs, magazine racks) makes its surroundings nicer to wait in and raises a room's **prestige**, which lifts staff morale and results. Small things like a plant or a poster brighten only a couple of tiles around them, and centerpieces like the Zen Fountain or the Koi Pond a whole stretch. Point at a card in the Items menu to see what an item does, and with one in hand the hint says what it would change where it is about to go, and the floor is colored to match. With a **Woven Rug** in hand, pick its pattern from the swatches under the hint before you put it down: Kilim, Ocean, Sunset, Forest, Lotus or Beach stripes. The next rug starts with the same pattern, and a rug's inspector changes it later, for free.
10. Staff have **morale**. Underpaid staff ask for a raise, and anyone kept unhappy for long, underpaid or overworked, quits. Promote whoever is ready and give raises from their inspector, or from their row in the **Staff** menu, which also promotes everyone waiting, or pays every raise asked for, with one button once two or more are.
11. Equipment **wears out** with use. A Caretaker services it; left alone it breaks down. From 70% wear (`WEAR_WARN` in `core/data/rules.ts`) it warns you, more and more as it goes: a machine puffs smoke and its light flickers, furniture shudders, and a mat gives off dust. Broken, a machine goes dark and smolders and furniture lists to one side, under a wrench (one wrench for a huddle of broken things side by side). From the first star, **upgrade** a treatment table, mat or scanner from its inspector, up to a level for each star: it wears more slowly and its treatments work more often. A Caretaker with the **Mechanics** course installs each upgrade. Upgraded equipment wears a plate with a gold star for each level, big enough to count from the usual zoom. A machine with a flame or a motor (the Hot Stone Kiln most of all) that overloads while smoking may **catch fire** instead, never otherwise: the alarm goes, guests in the room are frightened and the room is out of action, and every 12 seconds the flames spread to something beside it that wears out, never past the room's walls. A Caretaker drops everything to put it out: in 3 seconds with a **Fire Extinguisher** within 10 tiles of the room's door, or 15 seconds beating it out without one. From the third star, **sprinklers** (a button in the room's inspector, $1,500) put a fire out on their own after 4 seconds, though they flood the room until a Caretaker mops it. The **fire brigade** is called the moment a fire starts, and half a minute later their truck comes up the street with its siren going and parks near the door. Two firefighters run in and hose out whatever still burns, anywhere in the building, for one fee of $2,500 a call-out; if it is already out, they shrug and leave for nothing. What burnt is charred black, broken until a Caretaker repairs it, with soot beside it to sweep up. The advisor says who is on the way, and warns of a smoking machine with nothing near its door to put a fire out quickly.
12. The **Money** menu offers loans, with the next payday's wages beside them before you borrow, and lets you set prices. A new guest who hears what their treatment costs may balk and leave: few up to 130% of standard, then steeply more (about a third at 140% and two thirds at 160%, at 50% reputation), with a good name forgiving a dearer price and a poor one less, though no one balks at standard prices or below. A plain regular balks half as often, and loyal regulars and emergencies never do, so across all your guests that comes to about one in ten at 140%. The Prices tab says the share of new guests who balk at your price, and two tenths dearer, before you raise it. Its **Payroll** tab shows the next payday against the bank: the wages due, the balance, the loan room left, last month's net and the net a month at the pace of the last month or so, with a verdict in a line: covered, covered by borrowing (and where the loan will stand), or short past what the bank will lend, with what the bank then does. A toast says so as soon as the pace first says payday is not covered, and again five days before if the bank alone will not cover the wages. The tab also weighs a month's wages against the bank and the loan left, through a new center's first three paydays and whenever there is less than a month in hand, and with less than a month the advisor asks for no new hire, course or house call until there is one; each center's card in the campaign gives what its opening costs in wages and rooms against the money it starts with and the loan. Under Prices it also lets you limit how many **guests at once** the center lets in, below what the building takes (4, and 2 for each staffed treatment station your Consultation Rooms keep in guests, each signature machine with someone to run it and each staffed Consultation Room; those past it are turned away and counted): guests who find it full go elsewhere, fees and all, but nobody waits long enough to go home worse off, which is a way through a summer the building cannot grow for. **Emergencies** bring a batch of guests to heal against the clock for a bonus.
13. From the first star you can add **acupuncture**: build an **Acupuncture Clinic** and hire an **Acupuncturist**, and a second kind of guest starts arriving.
14. From the second star you can add **reiki**: build a **Reiki Studio** and hire a **Reiki Practitioner** for a third. At High Pines and The Orchard Estate, and in the sandbox, **sound healing** opens at the first star: build a **Sound Bath Studio** and hire a **Sound Healer**, who plays for every guest lying on the studio's mats at once (2 mats as a Trainee or Junior, 3 as Experienced or Senior, 4 as an Expert; any more stand empty). From the second star, everywhere but Willow Lane, a **Qi Gong Studio** holds weekly classes for guests who come in just for them: hire a **Qi Gong Instructor**, add **Practice Spots** (each a place in the class; an Instructor leads 6 of them as a Trainee, 8, 9 and 11 as they rise, and 12 as an Expert, and any more stand empty), and pick the days of the week in its inspector. A Practice Spot turns to face the Instructor's Mat as it is placed, and R turns it by hand. A new studio holds classes on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. An Instructor rests when the next class is a couple of days off, and gets up in time to walk back for it, so a Staff Room near the studio keeps them from missing one. With class-goers waiting, they hold off a drink or a snack until they're properly thirsty or hungry, as other staff do with a line. How many class-goers come each week grows with your **reputation** and the Instructor's rank, not with the studio: Practice Spots are how many a class can hold, and the studio's inspector says how many come a week at this reputation ("About 21 class-goers a week come for classes at this reputation, shared among 3 class days"). Each week's class-goers are shared among its classes, so more classes a week fill fewer places each. Class-goers set off a day or so ahead and lose no patience on the walk in. Anyone early for a later class waits in line rather than take a spot from someone come for the class before, and anyone still on the way or in line when a class ends is put down for the next one. Class-goers who have had no class three days after theirs go home disappointed, and you're told why: no Instructor, the Instructor on a break or away from the mat, the class before still going, or they got there too late.
15. Click a built room to change it: add **windows**, **move its door**, or **resize** it by dragging a wall in or out. Floor up to what the room's price covers (a tile more each way than its smallest) is free; past that it costs $200 a tile, and giving up floor you paid for, when the room was built or since, refunds half. **Move** picks the room up, furniture and all, to put down somewhere else for free, and **Copy** builds as many copies as you click, at what building one would cost; R turns either. A room can go right up against another room's windows: each window carries on through into the new room, as a window in both walls. Windows can also go between two rooms. Only a window that would come through where a moved or copied room has something hung on its wall is taken out, for half its price back, and the hint says so before you click. **Save as template** keeps the room, furniture and all, under the Rooms menu's Templates tab, to build again in this game or any other, and to export to a file or import from one. **Decorate** it with a floor and walls of your choosing, for that room or every room of its kind, free and for looks alone: oak, slate, sage and sky to start with, and terrazzo, bamboo, a chakra rainbow, wood paneling, tie-dye, a starry night and more for karma. Switch on **Priority** to have staff fill it first. To keep someone to particular rooms, press **Assign to rooms** in their inspector and click the rooms.
16. Click a desk or table to see its **line**: move guests up or down, over to another line, or **send them home**. A diagnosed guest's inspector shows the **chance** their treatment works.
17. The **Policy** menu sets how sure a diagnosis must be before treatment, whether emergency guests go first, when staff take breaks, and, with a Shaman, whether the mushrooms are put away for the health inspector.
18. A consultant can be kept to consultations or scans, and a Caretaker from any of their jobs, under **Duties** in their inspector. New applicants turn up every few days in the **Hire** menu, and can be turned down to make room; each card shows their quirk in green where it helps (Brisk, Tireless, Undervalued and the like) and red where it costs you (Chatterbox, Sleepyhead, Dawdler, Litterbug, Grump). Each list in the menus, the applicants included, has a **Sort by** dropdown, and keeps the order chosen while the game runs.
19. When guests pile up, the advisor says which stage of a visit holds them up most (check-in, consultation or a treatment) and why: too few staff, staff called away to breaks and courses, or a station busy nearly all the time. It weighs the payroll first: when the books could not carry another wage, or the bank could not pay for the room and a month's wage, it says so and names prices or saving up instead, and while payday is not covered or the bank is calling the overdraft in it asks for no new staff or rooms at all. Staff kept away by breaks call for a Staff Room nearer by rather than another wage, and a new hire on the way or someone out on a course or a house call is waited for. When it is time to cut, it names who to let go (in a role with two or more, the one whose station was busy least) with their wage and where the books would stand without them. Select a station to see how busy it has been over the last few days, and how often someone was at it. Click the **reputation** figure to see what guests are saying, and how the center does on results, experience, value and staff. Reputation moves more slowly toward the ends of its scale: below 50% every gain lands in full and a loss counts less the lower you are (three quarters at 10%, nothing at 0%), and above 50% every loss lands in full and a gain counts less the higher you are (two fifths at 90%, nothing at 100%). So a center on its knees can climb back, a good name is hard to keep perfect, and a center that heals only half its guests still sinks. The **🗺️** button at the top right colors the floor by how pleasant each spot is, guests' patience or needs, staff energy, or upkeep. The **Stats** menu also has a **casebook** of every visit by condition, and a **Guests** tab showing which discipline new guests come for, and why. Where a center has seasons (The Boardwalk) or rush hours (The Exchange), the Guests tab and the bottom bar's date, on hover, give this season's arrival multiplier and the next's with the day it turns, or whether a rush hour is on and when it ends; each season's announcement says how many guests a month to expect from last month's count. Each discipline draws by how popular it is (massage is an everyday thing, sound healing and shamanism niche), its **following**, which grows with every guest it heals, shrinks with every one it lets down and fades slowly while nobody is treated, its finest room (prestige and quiet draw guests, as do spare mats in a Sound Bath Studio), how much of it your staff are trained for, and how long its line is: once more than three guests wait for each of its practitioners, some new guests hear of it and stay away altogether. A new discipline starts with a small following and grows as word gets round, as long as it heals more guests than it fails. How often new guests arrive starts from your reputation and grows with what you can treat: 12% more for each **staffed treatment station** past the first, up to 2.5 times as many, where a station is a table, or a mat a Sound Healer can play for, with a practitioner on the payroll at work to use it (one away on a course or a house call counts again once back), for as many stations as your Consultation Rooms can keep in guests, four each, since every guest is seen there first. A **signature machine** is not one of those stations: its two ailments come only for it, only once it stands with a practitioner of its own on the payroll, who runs it before any table (so one built without hiring for it leaves a table standing instead), at a pace of their own (35% of what your reputation draws for each one, times the season and the rest, and its room's pull as a finest room's), fewer while nobody is trained to run it or once more than one guest waits for it, and none while it is down. Its guests are seen in a Consultation Room first like anyone's, so it takes one of the four stations each keeps in guests, before the tables. The building lets in only so many guests at once, set by the same count, and turns the rest away. The Guests tab and the bottom bar's date, on hover, give the pace factor by factor with the counts behind it, and the Guests tab gives the limit, what it is made of, how many found the center full this month, and what would let more in.
20. From the first star, a **Shop Counter** sells books, crystals, oils and candles to guests on their way out; the better their visit, the likelier they are to buy. The **Money** menu sets each treatment's own **price**, which counts with the overall level toward the share who balk (each treatment's row shows it), runs **marketing** campaigns, and manages the loan. Its Books tab has a **Where the money comes from** table: treatments, consultations, classes, house calls, shop and vending, and bonuses, this month and last, with each one's share, and a line saying when classes, calls and bonuses rather than healing are carrying the center. Now and then a **house call** is offered: send someone out for a day or more, for about what they would earn at home in that time, a little more for each rank. The offer says, beside each who could go, how many wait in their line and how many days of condition the worst of them has left, so you can see what the days away cost. Beside **Rooms**, **🏡 Expand** outlines the plots of land around the building, each with a For Sale board; point at one to see its name and price, and click to buy it. Pick a room that fits nowhere in the building at its smallest and the hint says so, naming the cheapest plot for sale, the one the builders are still at, or the star the next comes up at; in a first Willow Lane game, the advisor says at the first star what has come up for sale and that land is for once the building is full. Each center has its own plots, opening with stars, and some only once the plot beside them is built on. Every center's lot runs further east than its building does, and at the second star, when the treatment machines open, one plot comes up for sale that is big enough for the center's machine room beside the rooms you have: a building of its own, with its own door, so nothing you build can wall it off. More comes up at the third star, joined to the building, so leave a corridor to it. Land is never cheap, and the later plots cost the most for their size. Some plots are buildings of their own, with their own door and a path from the pavement: guests and staff walk outside to reach them, and mind it in a storm or in bitter cold or heat. Once bought, a plot is fenced off for a few days while the builders are at work, and the building grows into it when they are done. Click a plot of yours to see it: name it, watch the work, and see the rooms on it. The Money menu's **Land** tab lists them all, with **Show me** to go and look.
21. Now and then a member of staff sets a **challenge** with a deadline, shown under the objectives; meet it for a reward. Each year ends with an **awards ceremony**, and the trophies won fill a **Trophy Cabinet**.
22. Guests' **condition** worsens while they wait, faster with some conditions; the worse it gets, the less likely the treatment is to work, and if it runs out they go home worse off, at −6 reputation each. You are warned twice first: once as a line's first waiting guest turns poor, naming the line, the count and the levers (shorten the line, move them up it, or send them home for −1), and once by name for each guest within two days of running out, with the advisor saying what it costs and what to do. A guest's inspector and a station's line show the days of condition each has left, and the **🗺️** button has a **Condition** overlay. A failed treatment leaves **bad vibes** over its room, which fade slowly unless a Reiki Practitioner, or a Caretaker with the Smudging course, clears them sooner with a bundle of sage. **Wind Chimes** make them fade four times as fast in their room, with nobody to clear them. From the second star, a **stress outbreak** may be offered: calm the guests you spot sweating and fidgeting before it spreads.
23. Keep the place comfortable. Noise from arcades, pool tables, machines and crowds spoils treatments nearby, so keep the fun items away from the treatment rooms, or hush them with **Soundproof Panels**, which are cheap but add nothing to a room's looks. Anything loud enough to bother a guest on a table sends out ripples while a guest uses it, the wider the louder. The **Gong of Last Resort** goes further: while a session plays it rattles every room that shares a wall with its studio, loudest along that wall, so build the studio away from treatment rooms and other sound baths: everyone in a rattled room flinches at each strike. Each session soils a table's linens: place a **Linen Hamper** so a Caretaker can change them. A toilet or a Sound Bath Mat grows grimy as it is used, and draws flies once it is filthy. Winters are cold and summers can be hot: **Radiators** and **Air Conditioners** fix that (warm wisps rise off anything heating the air, and cool ones spill out of an air conditioner while it runs), and the **🗺️** button has **Calm** and **Temperature** overlays. Willow Lane's weather is mild all year, and each center after it asks more of you: The Exchange's winters are the first to need heating, and the Boardwalk's summers the first to need cooling. Each one has a **thermostat** in its inspector: on **Auto**, the default, it works only as hard as the weather calls for and is off while the weather is nice, or set it to Low, Medium or High whatever the weather, or Off. **Set all** gives every one of its kind the same setting, and **Temperature overlay** colors the floor until you turn it off. A Fireplace has one too. Staff get thirsty and hungry too, so keep drinks and snacks within reach, or from the first star put a **Staff Fridge** in the Staff Room, which sees to both on a break.
24. **VIPs** write ahead and tour a few rooms, each judging by their own tastes, and the **health inspector** checks every room for dirt, broken equipment and a Fire Extinguisher within 10 tiles of the door, or sprinklers. A report afterward says what they made of each room. A failed inspection can be **made to go away** for twice the fine, at least $10,000, though one time in ten word gets out, reputation takes a knock, and the inspector is back within the month.
25. The center climbs through five grades of **standing**, from Back-Room Practice to Healing Institute, by working: each room where staff see guests counts 16 points, and 4 more for each star of its prestige, while someone on the payroll can work in it, and any other room (a Restroom, a Staff Room, or a Massage Suite with no therapist) counts 4; each member of staff counts 3 points a rank, from 3 for a Trainee to 15 for an Expert; and every 5 guests healed add a point, for good. A grade once reached is kept. Each grade brings more guests and better applicants, but guests at a grander center expect grander treatment rooms. A **ZenMart** opens across the street a few weeks in: it holds sales that draw away guests who think you charge too much, offers jobs to unhappy staff, and competes with you for **Center of the Year**, which goes to whichever stands higher when the year ends. It grows about 18 points a month until it stands a quarter above you, and no further, so a center that grows faster can catch it, and one that stands still cannot. A tip says when it stops growing, and another the day you pass it. You can knock it back: each VIP you delight takes 5% off its standing, word of mouth stops its growth and takes about 5 points a month off it while it runs, and winning Center of the Year takes a tenth off. Both are in the Stats menu under **Standing**, which also says how far the rival can grow and what staffing an idle room would add.
26. Every few months a **mishap** is announced three days ahead: a power cut, a storm, a big freeze or wasps, depending on the center (Willow Lane has none). A notice at the top of the screen says what it will do, with a button to prepare for it at a price. With a Shaman here, **Ward it off** costs only their time and holds three times in four. The storm at The Boardwalk also floods a restroom as it starts: water on the floor and its sign's lamp red, out of action until a Caretaker has mopped it out, however long that takes after the storm has passed. The guests mind: anyone who wades through the water slows down and says so in their review, and so does anyone who needs the toilet and finds it under water. Where power cuts come, a **Generator** from the Items menu keeps the power on through every one, for as long as it does not break down, and costs $150 a day it runs, in diesel, under the machines' upkeep: it stands outdoors on the grounds, beside the pavement or a path so a Caretaker can service it, as it wears a little every day and a lot on a day it runs, and it is loud, though the building's outer walls keep most of its noise out. You can tell when it is carrying the center: it puffs from its stack, shudders and rumbles, and its light is full on, where standing by it glows low. You can see what a mishap has stopped: in a power cut the building goes dark and everything with a plug goes out, while the sun still shines outside; in a freeze the toilets, sinks and drinks are frosted over with icicles; and wasps swarm over the snacks, drinks and bins.
27. Now and then a guest who enjoyed their visit becomes a **regular**: they come back every few weeks under their own name, and Reception greets them. Good visits make them **loyal** and then **devoted**: more patient, back sooner, never put off by prices, and tipping on top of the fee. A bad visit or two and they stop coming. The Stats menu lists them under **Regulars**, up to twelve at a time, and anyone a Shaman's **Vision Quest** heals comes back as a loyal one even past that.

### Pencilling rooms in

Know where a room should go but can't pay for it yet? Press **✎ Pencil in** in the Rooms menu, pick a room and drag out its floor and door as you would to build it. It costs nothing: the room is sketched on the floor, hatched in its color with its name on it, and nobody walks round it. Nothing can be placed on a sketch or built over it, so the space stays free: a bench, another room or a wall dragged into it is turned down, and the sketch is selected so you can erase it. A sketch can go anywhere the room could, or on a plot of land still for sale. The Pencil in tab says how many you have and what building them all would cost.

Click a sketch to see what it will cost and whether it still fits. **Build here** opens its blueprint in that spot, door and all, with the first piece of furniture in hand; if something has got in the way since, it hands you its floor to click as it is or drag out afresh. **Move** draws it again elsewhere, and **Erase** rubs it out. A blueprint you can't afford yet can be kept with **✎ Pencil in for later**, which refunds its windows and extras.

## Reading people

Guests and staff show how they are doing before any bubble goes up, so you can see trouble coming from across the building.

- **Waiting guests** grow more impatient in steps you can tell apart at a glance. Content, they stand at ease. Under 60 patience they turn **restless**, shifting from foot to foot and glancing about. Under 40 they are **impatient**: arms folded high, stamping a foot, and a frown. Under 25 they are **fed up**: hands on hips and turned toward the front door, or sitting with their head in their hands, and a red hourglass goes up over them as the last warning before they leave; while merely impatient, they show an amber one now and then. A restless crowd is the time to add benches, tea or another member of staff.
- **Speech bubbles** say what someone is about in three colors. **Red**, pulsing, with a white ! at its corner, is trouble to act on now: a member of staff about to quit, a guest fed up with waiting, or a guest getting worse while they wait. **Amber** is a need that will become trouble: a waiting guest bursting for the toilet or parched, or a member of staff worn out with no break in sight. **White** is everything else, including someone already on their way to what they need. A bubble stays up long enough to read at any speed, and bubbles and markers stay readable however far out you zoom.
- **Faces** follow how guests feel: a frown as their patience runs short, a wince when their condition is poor, wide eyes and an open mouth when frightened on a bad trip or stressed in an outbreak, eyes closed resting on a Lounger or lying back for a treatment, and a big grin once they are healed, as the symptom shrinks away in a puff of sparkles and a bright ring. A guest healed lying down stays on the table until it is gone, then climbs down and hops for joy.
- **Getting on and off a table** takes a moment at any speed of the game: a guest stops at its edge and climbs on, onto their hands and knees and then stretched out face down on a massage or acupuncture table, face into the table with the soles of their feet up, or sitting on the edge and lying back on a Reiki Table or a Lounger. Getting off, they do the same backwards, down to the edge, and walk on from there.
- **Staff with nothing to do** pass the time after a few seconds: a long stretch with both arms overhead, a slow look round with their hands behind their back, or a long look at their watch, and they stroll about with their arms barely swinging. Someone on a break or on a course keeps to it. A clinic full of stretching staff has more staff than guests.
- **Rooms** carry a sign on their door with a lamp on top: **green** when someone is at work there (or, for a room nobody needs to staff, such as a restroom, a staff room or a Posture Lab, when it is finished and whole), **amber**, pulsing, when nobody is at its station, gone on a break, away or not yet hired, **red**, pulsing faster, when a station or something the room was built with is broken, and **grey** while it is still a blueprint.

## Settings

**Settings**, on the title screen and in the pause menu (☰), has a **Tutorial** switch under **Help**: on, a new Willow Lane game walks through its opening, and a game in any campaign center explains each thing the first time it happens in the campaign, such as the first cold snap (see [Tutorial](#tutorial)). Until you set it, the walk-through is on only while the campaign has no stars, and it switches itself off once you have been through it; the lessons keep coming, once each, until you turn the switch off, which skipping the tutorial does too. In a campaign game it also starts or stops the tutorial in that game (only the lessons outside Willow Lane, or once that game has been through the opening); on the title screen and in the sandbox it is kept for the next campaign game. The switch beside **Play** on Willow Lane's card in the campaign turns the walk-through on or off for the next Willow Lane game.

It also switches acupuncture, reiki, sound healing, the Shaman and the Qi Gong classes on and off, each on its own. Switching one off hides its room, staff and equipment and stops its guests arriving, while anything already built stays where it is. The choice on the title screen applies to new games, and each saved game remembers its own. Under **Building**, **Item stats** chooses where the Items menu shows what each item does: in the tooltip, in a side panel, or on the cards. **Preview while placing** switches off the hint and floor coloring that show what an item in hand would change. Sound and music are there too.

Under **Display**, **Interface size** draws the panels, buttons and words in a game at 90% to 150% of their usual size, and **Messages stay** keeps the messages on the right long enough to read, twice that, or until you close them. **Share play statistics**, under **Privacy**, is on to begin with: it sends anonymous figures on how games go, such as the rooms built and how each month ends, and never your saves or their names.

In the desktop app, **Full screen** under **Display** fills the screen with the game, and the app opens the same way the next time. Leaving full screen any other way switches the setting off with it: the green button or Ctrl+Cmd+F on a Mac, F11 on Windows, or **Game › Toggle Full Screen** in the menu. The browser version has no such switch, and leaves full screen to the browser.

The **Controls** there are kept for every game on this computer: **edge scrolling** (off to begin with), how often the game **autosaves** (every 12 seconds, every minute, every 5 minutes to begin with, or only on leaving; it always saves as you leave), and the **keys**. Click a key and press the one you want in its place; a key already in use swaps over, and **Reset the keys** puts them all back. The arrow keys always move the camera and Esc always cancels, whatever the keys are set to.

## Cheat console

For testing, or for fun. Switch on **Cheat console** at the bottom of **Settings**, and then **~** (the key under Esc) opens it in a game. Type a command and press Enter. The up and down arrows bring back commands you typed before, and Esc or ~ closes it. The console is off to begin with, and ~ does nothing until it is switched on. Two Point's games have no console of their own, so the commands follow Theme Hospital's cheats and what Two Point's trainers offer.

| Command                      | What it does                                                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `help`                       | List the commands                                                                    |
| `money [amount]`             | Add money, $10,000 unless you say; a minus takes it away (`money -5k`)               |
| `rep [points]`               | Add reputation, 10% unless you say                                                   |
| `karma [amount]`             | Add karma to spend in any center, 10 unless you say                                  |
| `star`                       | Earn the next star, prize and all                                                    |
| `win`                        | Earn every star left in the center                                                   |
| `staff`                      | Every member of staff at full energy and morale, fed and watered                     |
| `happy`                      | Every guest at full patience, with no thirst, hunger or need for the restroom        |
| `guests [count] [condition]` | Send in guests, 5 unless you say, with a condition if you name it (`guests 3 slump`) |
| `emergency`                  | Offer an emergency now                                                               |

A campaign game you use any command but `help` in earns no more achievements or stars for the campaign, and the console says so before you do. Karma added with `karma` is kept for the campaign all the same.

## Saved games

The game autosaves every 5 minutes unless **Settings** says otherwise, and always as you leave it, in the browser and in the desktop app alike. **Save or load** in the pause menu (☰) keeps up to 20 named saves, and the title screen can load any of them. Starting a new game or loading a different one never destroys the game you were in: it moves to a "Previous game" slot.

Saves live in the browser's storage, so the web version and the desktop app each have their own. **Export** writes a save to a file and **Import** reads one back, which is how to back a game up or move it from one to the other.
