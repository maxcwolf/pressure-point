# Changelog

Each release's notes are taken from its section here. Keep them short.

## Unreleased

- **Staff with more to them.** Every applicant now has one to three quirks, never more than one that costs you, and about one in seven has a rare one as well: a Former Influencer, a Burnout Survivor, Healing Hands or someone who Holds Space. Each shows as a chip on their card, their row and their inspector, with what it does when you point at it, and pay follows them. Staff you already have keep the quirks they came with.

## v0.34.3

- **Under the hood.** On a Mac, an app that updated itself no longer has all its files dated hours in the future for players west of Greenwich, which could make Spotlight complain. Nothing changes for play.

## v0.34.2

- **Under the hood.** The installed app now reports crashes, hangs and runs that ended without quitting to Sentry, including when the whole computer went down, so trouble on players' machines can be seen and fixed. Nothing changes for play.

## v0.34.1

- **Under the hood.** The interface's styles now come from one theme (Panda CSS): every color, size and line in the menus, dialogs and panels is drawn from the same scale, so they line up a pixel or two tighter here and there. Nothing changes for play.

## v0.34.0

- **Pencil rooms in.** The Rooms menu's new Pencil in pill sketches a room's floor and door for later, for nothing: nothing can be built over it, and Build here in its inspector opens its blueprint on the spot when you're ready. A blueprint you can't afford yet can be pencilled in for later too.
- **Sketches that keep up.** A pencilled-in room whose doorway gets blocked, or that no longer fits, turns amber and says so on its label, and the Pencil in tab counts them. No door may open onto a sketch, and a sketch on a plot stays put once the plot is built on.
- **Pencil templates in, and see every plan.** With Pencil in on, a template pencils in, furniture and windows outlined on the floor, and Build here builds the whole room at once. The new Plans overlay shows every sketch with its price and fit, and lists them to go to or build.

## v0.33.7

- **The payday warning keeps up.** While the payday warning is on screen, its figures follow the bank and the month's pace, paused too, and once the bank alone holds the wages it turns green to say payday is covered now. It goes once payday has passed, and one dismissed stays gone.
- **The Pressure Point Foundation.** Once every star in a campaign center is earned, the Money menu's Books tab takes three gifts to the Foundation, of $50,000, $100,000 and $200,000 (half as much again at The Orchard Estate), each worth 1 karma: something to do with the money that piles up after the last star. The card says what a gift would leave for the next payday.
- **The weather, center by center.** Willow Lane is mild all year, never cold or hot enough for anyone to mind. The Exchange's cold winters are the first that need Radiators, the Boardwalk's summers are now hot enough to need Air Conditioners, and the Orchard Estate's winters and summers are the harshest of both.
- **The first cold snap, explained.** The first time the weather gets indoors with nothing to warm or cool the place, a card says how cold or hot it is outside and in, what is comfortable and what fixes it, with a button that puts a Radiator or an Air Conditioner in hand. The tutorial's cards now come once each across the campaign, in whichever center a thing first happens, and the Settings switch turns them on or off in any campaign game.
- **A simpler Willow Lane.** Qi Gong classes are no longer on offer at Willow Lane, so its second star brings the Kneadmaster alone, and no Instructor applies. The campaign meets the classes at The Exchange's second star, as its card now says. Willow Lane sells one plot a star, the unit next door, the old bakery and the yard behind: the back room and the shop to the west are no longer for sale, and a game that bought either keeps the land.
- **Practice Spots face the Instructor.** A Practice Spot turns to face the Instructor's Mat as it is placed or moved, with an arrow showing which way, and R turns it by hand instead. Spots already placed keep the way they face.
- **Fires.** A machine smoking with wear can catch fire when it overloads, and only then. A Caretaker puts it out, quickly with a Fire Extinguisher within 10 tiles of the room's door, slowly without, while it spreads to whatever wears out beside it. From the third star, sprinklers fitted in a room put a fire out on their own, but soak the room. The fire brigade is called the moment a fire starts: their truck comes up the street with its siren going, and they charge $2,500 if they have to hose it out themselves, or shrug and leave for nothing. The sandbox has a switch for fires.
- **Promote and pay raises from the Staff menu.** Each row has a button to promote whoever is ready or raise their pay, gold for whoever asks, and when several are waiting, one button promotes them all or pays every raise asked for, with what it adds to the monthly payroll.
- **Light snow.** Where winter goes below freezing, a light snow falls once or twice a year for a few days at a time. It is only weather: nothing changes for the center.
- **Three hidden achievements.** Three new achievements show as mystery cards on the Achievements tab until they are earned. One is for a month of charging above standard prices in which nobody is put off by them; the other two are for the mushrooms, and you will know them when you see them. To keep finishing the campaign enough to unlock everything, the Bookcase, Mindfulness Arcade, Espresso Machine and Koi Pond each cost one more karma.
- **Windows between rooms.** A room can now go right up against another room's windows: each window carries on through into the new room, as a window in both walls, instead of standing in the way. Windows can also go in a wall between two rooms.
- **Trees through the seasons.** Autumn leaves come in five bright colors, scarlet, orange, gold, crimson and a yellowing green. In winter the park's trees, the street trees and the orchards drop their leaves and show bare branches, turned a different way on each tree. Pines keep their needles.
- **Cards that say there is more.** The inspector, the wide dialogs and the title screen's panels show a scrollbar in the game's colors while there is more to see, a shadow along the top once scrolled, and a More tab at the bottom that scrolls the card on a press.
- **Two new announcements, both voiced:** staff are asked to stop mixing their salad in the singing bowls, and a guest is asked to come back for the emotional baggage they left in the hallway. The lines about knots, whale song, humming along with the fountain and the lost and found are gone.
- **Small fixes.** A guest needing a treatment only one member of staff is trained in waits for them, rather than being taken by someone untrained while they are about. The Kneadmaster Suite's inspector lists only the practices its chair delivers, so it no longer asks for a Stretch Rack and a Stone Warmer. The Posture Lab's lamp stays green with nobody in it, as its scanner runs itself, and guests lining up at the scanner no longer bring up a blank announcement. A press anywhere on a Staff menu row, its bars and chips included, selects that member of staff.

## v0.33.6

- **Sort the menus' lists.** Each list in the menus has a Sort by dropdown: items and rooms by name, price, decor, quiet or earnings; staff by name, role, rank, salary, energy, morale or what they know; applicants by rank, salary, what they know or quirks; and templates, the casebook and the regulars by their own figures. A list keeps its order while the game runs.
- **Small fixes.** Moving a treatment table, or selling its room, keeps the order of the line waiting for it, and whoever was on the table goes back to the front. Staff sent on a house call or a course they cannot get to stay at their post, and staff who cannot reach their best post take the next one. An award cheers up the member of staff who won it, not another with the same name. A guest who cannot reach a Lounger no longer feels rested on it. A receptionist at an empty Shop Counter now goes over to a Reception Desk with a line and nobody at it. And the advisor now says the wasps "have" put things out of action, not "has".

## v0.33.5

- **Willow Lane's high street.** The road and its pavements run on past Willow Lane's lot, with hedges on a low brick wall along the pavement and lamp posts further down the street, so the first center stands on a street rather than in a field. The way in to the door stays clear.

## v0.33.4

- **Money when it matters.** The tutorial's word on the bank and the loan now comes as a card the first time the bank holds less than a month's wages, with what it holds, what payday takes and when, and a Show me that opens the Loan tab. The Reception Desk step just says that everything comes out of the bank. Hiring the role the tutorial asks for closes the Hire menu, so its next card is not hidden.
- **Room signs stay in sight.** A sign beside a door in a wall cut low no longer hides behind the tall wall of a room just beyond it: it moves to the other side of the door, or, with no clear wall on either side, such as a door at the end of its wall, stands up over that wall.
- **Climbing on and off the table.** Guests stop at the edge of a table and climb on, in the same time at every speed of the game: face down, they lean over onto their hands, kneel up and stretch out; on their back, they turn round, sit on the edge and lie back. Getting off is the same played backwards, down to the edge. They no longer pop up onto it, or stand up on it before getting down.
- **The cure where you can see it.** A guest healed lying down stays on the table while a bigger puff of the cure plays, then climbs down and hops for joy. A guest getting up from a Lounger just gets up.
- **Symptoms you can read.** The goat has ram's horns, the wet blanket is a real blanket that drips, the guest with a stiff neck holds the phone they are staring at, and the hen is bigger.
- **Face down and impatient.** A guest lying face down on a massage or acupuncture table lies with the face into the table and the soles of the feet up. An impatient guest stamps a foot with arms folded high. Anything held, a cup or a wrench, is half as big again.
- **Out of each other's way.** People step aside rather than walking through each other, and the ring under someone selected stays under them as they climb on and off.
- **Guests out of uniform.** No guest wears a top in a staff uniform's color any more, in new games or old saves.
- **Lighter crowds.** People seen from the usual distance cost about 60% fewer triangles to draw.
- **Icons as clear small as large.** An icon's lines are as heavy as the pixels of the screen it really has call for, so nothing small turns into a navy blot or smears grey, and nothing jumps lighter at 22 pixels; each comes out at whole pixels, and on a sharp screen a small icon keeps its full drawing. Five roles, seven rooms and the busiest small menu icons have simpler drawings of their own, and the scene's icons lose their dark fringe.
- **Icons that each say one thing.** The Recovery Lounge is a recliner, not a second massage bed; the Condition overlay has its own heart, and the calm and upkeep overlays a color that shows on the picker's white card; the loan is a note with a coin, the casebook a book under a magnifier, the institute's standing a pagoda, the center of the year a house with a star, and turning the view a floor tile with an arrow, none of them now sharing a picture with something else.
- **Tidier panels.** Comparing two members of staff keeps inside its card, and lists others in the same role first, with an Everyone button for the rest. The overlay key sits beside the system buttons, as tall as they are, and the tutorial's ring round the applicants has their cards' rounded corners.

## v0.33.3

- **Alarms you can see coming.** Only trouble happening right now turns a bubble red: someone about to quit, a guest fed up with waiting, a guest getting worse while they wait. Red bubbles pulse and carry a white "!" so they stand out without the color. A bubble turns amber for a need that will become trouble: a waiting guest bursting for the toilet or parched, or staff worn out with no break coming. A guest running out of patience shows an amber hourglass now and then before they are fed up. Everything else is white, including anyone already on their way to what they need.
- **Bubbles stay up long enough to read.** Every bubble pops in and shrinks away, and stays at least 1.4 seconds, so it can be read at speed 3. With reduced motion turned on in your system, bubbles neither pop nor pulse.
- **Readable from where the game starts.** Speech bubbles and markers grow a little as the camera pulls back, up to half as big again, and their pictures fill more of the bubble.
- **Icons told apart by shape.** Thirsty is a glass with a straw, a break a steaming mug, hungry a burger, thanks a flower, and filthy rising stink lines. A failed treatment shows a thumb down instead of the hourglass. The guest marker is a whole figure, and a staff square's marker sits on the square's purple.
- **Faces in emoji yellow.** Every face, and the hands in the thumb down and the hush, are yellow instead of orange, so a face stands out in a red bubble as well as a white one.
- **No towers of markers.** Neighbouring broken or filthy items share one marker, and a runner's red ring pulses.
- **An advisor you can read.** A note stays up at least three and a half seconds rather than flipping between two several times a second, and a slow line is named once it reaches four guests and let go only once it is down to two, so the note does not come and go as a queue hovers. Something going wrong now, a money crisis among them, still takes over at once with a red edge. Each note's first sentence is in bold.
- **The date in a narrow window.** Below a wide screen the bottom bar keeps the season's icon, the day and the temperature rather than dropping the date, and its hover gives the whole date with the seasons. A game that opened later in the year, Willow Lane's among them, shows its own month there and in the Guests tab, where it used to count from January. Panels above the bar keep clear of it however it wraps.
- **Messages above the inspector.** With the inspector open, the newest two messages show above it rather than under it, and a count of the rest says how many are warnings, in red; pressing it opens them all. The inspector keeps its place as messages come and go.
- **Warnings you can tell apart.** In the inspector, trouble now (about to quit, broken, stopped, flooded) is in dark red on light red, and what wants seeing to (an empty station, smoke, grime, stale linens, a rival's offer, misery, what a promotion needs) in brown on gold, where every line was cream on navy. Bars go green, then amber, then red, needs included, with their value beside them, and morale in the Staff menu follows the same colors. A member of staff's inspector says what they are doing and anything wrong first.
- **One card for every raise, one for every promotion.** However many of the staff are asking, a single card says so ("Ines Crickmore and 2 more want a raise"), and pressing it lists them to pick from, each with what they said in its tooltip. Their asking no longer comes up as a message as well.
- **A room's inspector starts with what needs doing.** Who staffs it, or that nobody does, and anything wrong come first, then its line, how busy it is and what it can deliver; the room's description, prestige and comfort follow, with what makes a room prestigious in the hover over its stars.
- **Interface size.** A new Display setting draws the panels, buttons, words and tooltips in a game at 90% to 150% of their usual size, for a big screen or one seen from across the room. Everything still keeps within the window, the inspector scrolling where it runs out of room.
- **Calmer motion.** Announcements take turns, so a joke over the speakers no longer cuts off news of an emergency. Something new in the column at the top, such as a guest on a bad trip, goes below an open offer instead of pushing its buttons out from under the pointer. Money floating up stays on its spot as the camera moves, and amounts earned together add up rather than piling into a smear. Panels, menus and dialogs shrink away when they close, and the dimming behind a dialog fades in and out.
- **"An Acupuncturist".** The interface says "an" before a name that starts with a vowel, in a station's staffing, a room's missing equipment, a machine's demand, the Treatments menu and a house call offer.

## v0.33.0

- **A tutorial for your first game.** A new Willow Lane game walks you through its opening a step at a time: a card in the advisor's spot says what each step wants and why, a gold ring marks the button, tab or card to click, and a Tutorial list under the objectives keeps your place. The first thing bought says that everything comes out of the bank and that the Money menu lends, as a new center often needs for its first paydays. After the opening it carries on through what the center will need next (a Caretaker, Smudging, a Staff Room), saying on any step the bank alone could not take what it would leave and that the loan is cheaper than the red, then hands over to the advisor. Skip it from the card at any time.
- **Lessons as things first happen.** The first guest to walk in, the first heal, staff tiring, the first payday, a failed treatment, a guest storming out and a raise request each open a short card with pictures, once a game and never two in a day, with Show me to go to whoever it is about.
- **Tutorial on and off.** Settings has a Tutorial switch under a new Help heading, and Willow Lane's card in the campaign has one beside Play. It is on by itself only before your first campaign star, and switches itself off once you have been through the opening, so a replay starts without it unless you turn it back on.
- **When to buy land.** In your first Willow Lane game, the advisor says at the first star what land has come up for sale and when to buy it (once the building has no room left for a treatment room, and not before), with a line on the "Getting ready" checklist for it. In any game, picking a room that fits nowhere in the building says so in the hint, and names the cheapest plot for sale, the one the builders are still at, or the star the next comes up at.
- **The Posture Lab runs itself.** Its scanner needs no Consultant, only $250 a month to run and a Caretaker when it wears out, and a scan now makes the diagnosis certain, adding more to the chance of success on an upgraded scanner.
- **Structural conditions.** Crick of the Neck, Text Neck, Croquet Back and Wipeout are in the spine and the joints: a Consultant can see them but not pin them down, so without a working Posture Lab their treatment is a best guess and less likely to work. The guest's inspector shows which guests are structural and which were treated on a guess.
- **The Exchange looks downtown.** A paved plaza with street trees in planters, lamp posts, a crossing over the road and office blocks across the streets round it, in place of Willow Lane's lawn and woods; land for sale there is a gravel lot.
- **Building that feels sure.** A new item settles in with a little squash and its price floats off it, and the next one's preview keeps out of the way until you move the pointer rather than sit red on top of it. An item you can't afford shows red, saying so, before you click, and a refused click shakes the preview and pulses the reason.
- **A steadier camera.** Turning, gliding and zooming take as long on every screen. Jumping to someone eases the zoom in with the glide, the wheel zooms toward the pointer, the walls swap halfway through a turn, a selected room is outlined in gold, and a chosen overlay shows at once and fades in. With reduced motion asked for in your system, the camera cuts rather than glides.
- **Rooms that tell themselves apart.** No two rooms that can stand in the same center share a trim any more, for color-blind players too: Reiki is gold, with a white and gold table, Qi Gong jade, the Posture Lab indigo, and the lodge, alphorn hall and press house red-brown, ochre and olive.
- **Things that read at a glance.** Pictures and clocks on the walls are half as big again, each with one bold shape; the Gong of Last Resort, the Spirit Fog Machine and the Kneadmaster each look like nothing else; the Aquarium shows its water and fish; and the shelves, the Trophy Cabinet, the Recliner, the Soundproof Panels, the Serenity Statue and the default rug no longer look like one another or shout. Upgrade plates show their stars from the usual zoom, and the receptionist has a screen in front of them.
- **A calmer street.** ZenMart keeps its purple to its sign and awnings, under a plum roof on a paved forecourt, with snow on the roof in the mountains; the beach huts are soft pastel huts with their doors toward you; a window in a wall cut low is a strip of glass along the wall instead of a pane floating in front of the guests; and the welcome sign stands out by the pavement, clear of the room by the door.
- **Tell us what you think.** The game is in alpha and changes every day, and a welcome on your first visit says so. Give feedback, in the corner of the title screen and in the pause menu, sends a bug, an idea or anything else straight to the developer, with an email to be answered at if you like.
- **Mishaps show in the scene.** In a power cut the building goes dark while the sun still shines on the lot, and everything with a plug goes out; a freeze frosts the water with icicles hanging off it; wasps swarm over the snacks, drinks and bins.
- **Progress bars from every side.** A treatment's bar faces the camera however it is turned.
- **Weather stays outside.** Rain and snow no longer fall inside the building; snowflakes are round, rain falls in thin streaks, and flood water lies in bright pools with rings spreading over it.
- **Willow Lane opens in spring.** A new game there starts in March, in mild weather with the trees in leaf, so a first game does not open in a bare winter. The other centers, and games already saved, keep their own calendars.
- **Trees through the year, and a calmer day.** The park's trees turn gold in autumn and bare in winter, and the orchards blossom in spring; the light no longer pulses through each day at normal speed.

## v0.32.2

- **A heads-up on your first go.** In your first Willow Lane game, the advisor says what the center will need before the trouble comes: a Caretaker once guests are being healed, Smudging once there is one, a Staff Room as staff tire. A "Getting ready" checklist under the objectives ticks them off.

- **Rooms against a neighbor's windows.** A copied or moved room, or one built from a template, can now go where a window next door looks onto its floor: the window is taken out and sold back at half its price, and the hint says so first.

## v0.32.0

### More room to grow

- **More land to grow into.** Every center, the sandbox too, has two more plots on a wider lot: at the second star a building of its own, big enough for the center's machine room beside the tables, and at the third more room joined to the building.
- **Floor is paid for however a room gets it.** A room's price covers floor up to one tile more each way than its smallest, 4×4 for a 3×3 room, whether it is built that size or resized to it. Every tile past that costs $200, at build and on resize alike, the hint shows the price as you drag, and floor paid for comes back at half when given up.
- **A Generator, out of doors.** The first thing that stands outside: on the grounds, by the pavement or a path, it keeps the power on through every power cut while it works, needs a Caretaker now and then, and is loud enough that the outer walls are welcome. It puffs, shudders and rumbles while it carries the center through a cut, at $150 a day in diesel.

### Standing and the rival

- **Standing earned by working.** A room counts fully only while someone on the payroll can work in it, staff count by rank, and every guest healed adds to it, so Restrooms and Trainees no longer buy a grade.
- **A rival you can catch.** ZenMart grows only until it stands a quarter above you, so a center that keeps growing can overtake it. A delighted VIP, word of mouth and winning Center of the Year each knock it back, and the Standing tab shows its ceiling and what knocks it back.

### Treatment machines

- **Machines draw guests of their own.** A signature machine's two ailments now come only for it, at their own pace once it stands with a practitioner of its own to run it, more for a finer room, and the Guests tab and the date's hover say how many.
- **The Gong of Last Resort rattles the rooms next door** while it plays: noise through every shared wall, shown on its card and in the inspectors, so build its studio away from quiet rooms.
- **Rides on the Carousel of Rebirth.** Bored guests pay $25 for a ride while it treats nobody, and get off for a guest due for rebirth.
- **The machines in their own clothes.** Each treatment machine has an icon, a room sign and a treatment icon of its own, its model is polished to match the rest of the furniture, and an upgraded machine shows its stars on a brass plate, as the tables do.

### Warnings you can act on

- **A word when the consultations are the limit.** The advisor, and a warning the first day it happens, say when staffed treatment stations draw no guests because every guest sees a Wellness Consultant first, and that another Consultation Room would let them draw where another treatment room would not. The Orchard Estate's card says so of its opening.
- **The first paydays in sight.** Each campaign center's card gives what its opening costs in a month's wages and rooms against the money and the loan, and the Payroll tab weighs a month's wages against the bank and the loan left, with the advisor asking for no new hire, course or house call while there is less than a month in hand.

### Events

- **A really bad trip, now and then.** A guest whose ceremony goes badly may run round the institute freaking out everyone they pass. A runner yelps, the announcer has a word, heads turn as they pass and a red ring marks them in any view. A pill at the top counts the days to the door and sends someone in one click, a Shaman first; one who finds the door costs reputation, and one calmed within three days earns a point back.
- **The storm floods a restroom.** At The Boardwalk the storm now also floods a restroom as it starts, out of action with its lamp red until a Caretaker mops it out; boarding up and sandbagging, at the same $800 as before, stops it. The water moves as a Caretaker mops it, and guests who wade through it or find the toilet under water say so in their reviews.

### A livelier look

- **A moment for every cure.** The chicken, the knot or the cloud shrinks away in a puff of sparkles and the guest hops once, grinning.
- **More faces in the crowd.** People come slight, average or stocky, a quarter wear glasses, shoes come in four colors, and men have two more haircuts.
- **Eyes that blink and shut.** People blink, rest with their eyes closed on a table or a Recliner, lie face down for a massage or needles, and look frightened on a bad trip or in an outbreak.
- **People who do what they are doing.** Feet plant where they land at every speed, a receptionist types and nods, guests drink, snack, shop and play, and Caretakers stoop for litter, water the plants and crouch with a wrench.
- **Icons that read small.** Icons at 20 pixels and under, beside words and in the stats bar, draw with heavier lines and white fills from a second sprite sheet, and every speech bubble and marker has a simpler drawing of its own for small sizes, used in the scene too, so what a guest is saying can be told from where the game starts.
- **Small things noticed.** Everyone in a room the Gong rattles flinches at each strike, a ride on the Carousel of Rebirth gets a review of its own, and a tip says when ZenMart stops growing and the day you pass it.

### Fixes

- **Small fixes.** The bottom bar fits windows 1240 to 1540 pixels wide and shows the date whole at 1440, the tool hint reads as a row of chips, the sign out front reads from behind, and the Treatments button's word sits in a slight plate welded to its circle instead of crossing the border.

## v0.31.0

### A center that tells you where it stands

- **A bank that warns before it forecloses.** A payday the bank can't cover stops the game: borrow the difference, or pay the wages into the red at 5% a month, with the staff worried and, after a month there, asking for raises. The bank calls the overdraft in only from a center in the red past what it will still lend _and_ losing money, and then gives ten days to put one of the two right, with the numbers shown. Bankruptcy can be switched off in a sandbox game.
- **Payday in plain sight.** The Money menu's new Payroll tab shows the wages due against the bank, the loan room and this month's projected net. The payday warning comes as soon as the month's pace says payday is not covered, and again five days before. Wages are paid for the days worked since the last payday, so a new hire or a leaver costs only their days.
- **Where the money comes from.** The Books tab breaks income down by source, this month against last, and says when side income rather than healing is carrying the center.
- **Objectives show their numbers.** Click an objective for what counts behind it and how far it is from being met.
- **Two warnings before a line costs reputation.** A toast names the line as its first guest's condition turns poor, and a louder one names each guest within two days of going home worse off. Lines and the guest inspector show the days of condition left, and a new Condition overlay colors the floor by it.
- **Advice you can afford to follow.** The advisor weighs the payroll before asking for staff or rooms, asks for none while payday is not covered, and in the bank's ten days names who to let go.
